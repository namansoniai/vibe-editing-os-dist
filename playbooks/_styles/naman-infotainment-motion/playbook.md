# Naman · Infotainment Motion Style Playbook (template v1)

**Purpose.** You (Claude) receive a talking-head video of {{BV-01.name|the creator}} ({{BV-01.handle|@yourhandle}}) plus its script, and optionally screen captures of the tools, sites or apps they talk about (SW-01 `talking_head`, SW-12 `medium`). Use this playbook to **ideate and build** every hook, headline slab, caption, motion graphic, zoom, cue moment and transition, so the reel **stops the thumb at frame 0** and keeps teaching and entertaining to the last frame. It is a hype, result-first, infotainment style: the viewer *sees* every claim, every number and every roast.

**Lineage of this template.** This is Naman Soni's own proven playbook (v2 "Infotainment Motion", 3 Oct 2026), migrated into the v3 template structure with nothing removed. Where the creator directives and anything else disagree, **the directives win**. They come first because every rule below serves them. His October reels (r01–r10) are the evidence (App. B). The niche examples marked A (AI app building) are his; a second example niche B (personal finance) shows how the same machinery works for any topic.

### Style DNA `[DNA]`
A reel in this style is recognisable from one frame: a **tilted yellow Headline Slab with a thick ink stroke and a hard offset shadow** sits at the top; under it, a **result** (the viewer's bad version, already marked up in red marker, or the creator's premium version) floats in a tilted device card; the presenter is **cut out in front of that card** (depth sandwich), already pointing at it. From there the reel never sits still: the presenter drops into a panel under a light grid canvas or a night "Data Stage", every number becomes **countable things that move** (5 avatars, 5,000 dots, a gauge, a crashing phone), every risk becomes a **physical machine** (a bouncer, a gift box, a data wall), and every roast gets a **red stamp, a marker circle, a sticker and a meme cue**. Bright colours, each with one job; yellow is always the loudest thing on screen.

**Copy these 5 things:**
1. **The Headline Slab** (yellow, 6 px ink stroke, hard +12/+14 shadow, −1.5°, one CAPS keyword chip, ≤ 9 words, readable on frame 0, flips once, rockets out): §5.2, §6.5.
2. **The Result-First Roast hook** (bad result marked up → stamp verdict → shatter → light-sweep premium reveal, ≥ 10 state changes in 3 s, a result on screen by 2.0 s): §6.2.
3. **Data Theatre on the night stage** (numbers, comparisons and risks become countable crowds, gauges, versus lanes and metaphor machines that run on the spoken word): §3.5, §8.3 P-40…P-60, §8.5, §18.
4. **The tone-gated comedy layer** (marker circles, stamps, stickers, freeze-frame roasts, crash zooms and meme cues, only on `mock` beats): §8.6, §22.
5. **Three worlds and the stage-move + zoom grammar** (Studio / Canvas / Data Stage; panel drop, pop-back, bubble, slide-aside, depth sandwich; snap punch, crash zoom, rotation snap…): §3, §9, §10.2.

Why each value:
- **source_type `talking_head`**: every reel is the creator's own A-roll (selfie or tripod); screen captures and result mocks are inserted (r01–r10).
- **presenter `anchor` 85–100 %, max absence 2.5 s**: the creator stays on screen or one tap away (panel, bubble, depth sandwich); full-screen graphics without them last ≤ 2.5 s (H11).
- **spine `talking_head`**: the take is the timeline; jump cuts on word boundaries; visuals are inserted on trigger words.
- **captions `full / support / mute_safe`**: every spoken word is subtitled (CS-1), the hook and CTA use chunky captions (CS-2); the first 3 s must tell the story on mute (ST-2).
- **graphics `primary`**: Data Theatre, metaphor machines and the comedy layer carry the argument; 50 patterns (§8).
- **duration `short` 30–60 s**: the reference reels run 57–62 s; budgets are written per 60 s.
- **language**: Naman's own reels are Hinglish speech with romanised Hinglish captions (Latin script, from the script text), slab and labels in English (`on_screen: en`, Instagram-native). The template defaults to English and setup always asks (English, Hinglish, Hindi; BV-05). This copy: {{BV-05.speech|en}} speech → {{BV-05.captions|en}} captions.
- **numbers**: written as in the script ("5,000"); no compacting (the numbers are the picture). Numbers follow the language (BV-06): English → international `$` and K/M/B (unless the buyer picks ₹); Hinglish / Hindi → ₹ with Indian grouping and lakh / crore. The finance examples below stay in ₹ (Naman's own reels).
- **tone `hype / roast`**: the energy is maximal and the creator roasts the viewer's result; the comedy layer and meme cues exist only on `mock` beats.
- **themes `single`**: one palette; the three worlds are worlds, not themes.
- **formats `F-A`**: one format; the Data Stage is a world inside it, not a format.
- **footage_dependency `medium`**: the creator's A-roll + screen captures of what they name + (ideally) their own premium build; every missing capture has a created fallback (§12.3).
- **cta**: comment keyword → deliverable in DM, an early loop mid-reel, and a community chip (link in bio).
- **modules**: `data_figures` (every number on screen traces to the script), `ink` (marker circles, flaw markup, arrows), `brand` (community chip, key-cap CTA end moment, sponsor disclosure when needed). Everything else is OFF (one line each in §16–§25).

## §1 Procedure (follow in order) `[REQ] [DNA]`

The craft steps of this style are **P7b hook-pair design** (what is the viewer's bad state, what is the premium / fixed state, and how both are shown) and **P8b the data story** (which Data Theatre machine makes each number visible).

1. **P1 Inventory.** Run `ffprobe` on every input (resolution, fps, duration, audio; pick one voice track). Identify the camera setups (§12.1). Conform phone VFR footage to 30 fps CFR. Register every asset with its origin (`creator` or `created`, §12.5).
2. **P2 Matte.** This style needs a person matte in almost every reel: the depth sandwich (G-5), cut-out stickers, the reaction bubble (G-4), the lean-in (G-6) and the silhouette strobe (T-02). Generate it for the whole clip (§12.4).
3. **P3 Transcribe** with word timestamps. Whisper writes Hindi in Devanagari: keep that for timing only. Captions are romanised Hinglish from the script (`transform: transliterate`); in an English copy they are verbatim. Check every brand and tool name against the glossary.
4. **P4 Segment** into `HOOK`, `LOOP`, items/steps (ordinal words: pehla, doosra, teesra… / first, second…), `PAYOFF`, `CTA`. Mark jump-cut points on word boundaries.
5. **P5 Classify every sentence** with a line type (§8.4) and mark its **trigger word**.
6. **P6 Tone-tag every sentence:** `hype` · `mock` · `awe` · `explain` · `warn` · `win` · `cta`. The tone decides colour, zoom, caption treatment and cue type (§10.2 tone table). A sentence is `mock` when the creator roasts the viewer's result, assumption or habit ([NICHE: example A] "wo ekdam bekaar hai", "paanch nithalle dost", "hackers ke liye gift"; [B] "aur tum abhi bhi saara paisa savings account mein rakh rahe ho?").
7. **P7 Hook plan.**
   - **P7b Pick the hook pair** (§6.4): the viewer's *bad* state and the creator's *premium / fixed* state; plan the assets for both (captured or built live).
   - **Write 3 headline slabs** (§6.5) and **3 hook variants** (§6, archetype per variant). Recommend one. Run the stopper tests (§6.1).
8. **P8 Visual plan.**
   - a pattern per line (§8.4);
   - **P8b the data story:** for every number, comparison or risk, the Data Theatre pattern (P-40…P-60) that makes the viewer *see* it (§8.5), and its figure in `plan/figures.json` (§18 data check: inputs, formula, recompute, flag mismatches with the script);
   - the ink pass: every marker circle, arrow and note with its target (§22).
9. **P9 Beat sheet** (§13): one beat per trigger word, an event every ≤ 1.5 s (≤ 1.0 s in the hook). Each beat gets mode/layout, pattern, family, caption, tone, zoom, transition and cue.
10. **P10 SFX ledger** (§11, catalogue ids from the SFX pack) and the **transition map** (§9.3). Check the budgets.
11. **P11 Assets.** Capture or build them (§8.7). Run the ask-then-create flow for third-party moments (§12.5). Resolve fallbacks (§12.3) and say which were used.
12. **P12 Checkpoint** (§13.5), then **wait for approval.**
13. **P13 Build** act by act; `veos validate`; preview and QA (§15, at most 3 passes); final render.

## §2 Hard rules `[REQ] [DNA]`

### 2.1 Editing rules (every style)
The ten editing rules in `playbooks/_global/GLOBAL-RULES.md` apply. They are directions, not limits: smooth, seamless motion; nothing overlaps by accident; keep the face clear (behind the speaker is fair game, text included); readable at a glance; one idea at a time; show the thing, not the word; say what was said; hook titles hook; pace like the style, not like a timer; the style decides the look.
- **Facts the engine checks:** accidental overlaps, jumps, the face covered, unreadable text, numbers and quotes that don't match what was said, the promised count. Every count, timing and budget this playbook gives is direction for the edit, not a limit.
- **Picture first, in this style's own look:** every key beat shows the thing being said (an object, a screen or app, a diagram, numbers in motion), not just its word; text supports the picture and never replaces it. When the speaker points with words ("this, this and this", "from this to this", "ye dekho"), show what they mean. Illustrations may use made-up but realistic numbers and names ("212 views", "1.2M views"), with no label; a number or quote the speaker says is shown as said. This overrides any rule below that bans made-up numbers or asks for an example tag: those rules now cover claims (the creator's results, prices, benchmarks, testimonials), not illustrations.
- **Hook titles hook:** the on-screen title promises the viewer something (an outcome, a curiosity gap, who it's for) and is true to what the reel delivers; it need not repeat the spoken words. This playbook sets its shape (§5.2, §6.5: lines, sizes, word limits, case), never its voice (§6).
- **Retired (8 Oct 2026), whatever this playbook says below:** no REPRESENTATIONAL or example labels on made-up cards, no credit lines, no flash limit (flash as often as this style calls for; any "NC-11" cap below no longer applies), and text may sit behind the speaker without an exception.

### 2.2 Declared exceptions
**None declared.** This style is stricter than G1 on the face: the slab bottom sits ≥ 40 px above the head top. The depth sandwich (G-5) puts cards, objects and text behind the presenter freely (no exception needed). The busy hook stays inside G2 by sequencing: a stopper device lands while the previous one settles or exits (slab + result card + one annotation group + CS-2 = 4 elements, 3 text blocks).

### 2.3 Style MUST rules H1–H18
(H-n = the creator's former M-n; the numbering is kept so old notes still cite correctly.)
- **H1 Frame 0 is a thumbnail and a stopper.** It holds ≥ 3 layers: **headline slab + result + presenter**. At least one element is moving on f0, and the slab is fully readable on f0. `check: V-F0`
- **H2 ≥ 6 state changes in the first 3 s.** Caption steps, flaw circles, stamps, zooms, cards and chip pulses all count (`hook_sc_3s: 6`; measured 5–7 in r01 and r09 at 0.3 s steps: card settle, 3–4 CS-2 swaps, one marker/stamp group). `check: V-CADENCE`
- **H3 Result first.** By **2.0 s** the viewer has seen a result: the bad state, the premium state, or both (D3, §6.2). The premium / fixed state lands by **4.6 s**. `check: V-F0`
- **H4 The slab obeys §5.2 / §6.5:** ≤ 9 words, ≤ 2 lines (3 only with a number), one keyword chip, ≤ 2 emoji, readable at 25 % scale, and saying the same thing as the hook pair on screen. `check: V-TITLE`
- **H5 Zero dead air** (spine `talking_head`). At most 1 gap ≥ 150 ms per 15 s, except a deliberate ≤ 0.3 s record-stop before a pivot or punchline. Jump cuts on word boundaries ±1 f. `check: V-CADENCE + review`
- **H6 Literal, on-the-word visuals.** Every tool, number, command, step and risk gets its visual 2 f before the word and fully on within ±5 f. Tool cards land within ±1 f of the tool name. `check: V-ONWORD`
- **H7 Something changes every ≤ 1.5 s** (body) and **≤ 1.0 s** (hook). Nothing is visually static for more than 2.5 s. Body cadence 8–20 weighted state changes per 10 s. `check: V-CADENCE`
- **H8 Numbers become pictures** (D5). Every spoken quantity, comparison or risk is shown with a Data Theatre pattern: quantity as countable things, comparisons on the same axes, risk as a mechanism. Never just a number on a card. `check: V-DATA + review`
- **H9 Tone drives treatment.** Comedy visuals and meme cues appear only on `mock` beats (and the one allowed `win` meme, §11). `awe` beats are clean and premium. `warn` beats are red and tense. `check: V-COMEDY`
- **H10 The cue ledger is clean** (D7): no sound file used more than 2×, except the parallel list cue (≤ the number of items). Each meme file is used once. No file on two consecutive cues. `check: S5 (global) + V-LEDGER`
- **H11 The presenter stays on screen or one tap away.** B-roll lives in cards, splits, the Data Stage with a panel or bubble, or the depth sandwich. Full-screen graphics without them last ≤ 2.5 s; presenter share 85–100 %. `check: V-PRESENCE`
- **H12 The face is never covered.** The slab bottom sits ≥ 40 px above the head top, captions at chest height, stickers never on the face. `check: V-FACE + V-SAFE`
- **H13 Promise integrity.** The slab count equals the items shown. Every "end mein dunga" is paid off on screen. The CTA keyword {{BV-08.keyword|KEYWORD}} is on screen ≥ 1.5 s. `check: V-PROMISE`
- **H14 Facts only from the script or sources.** Illustrative numbers that the script itself presents as hypothetical ("5,000 users aaye toh…", "₹10,000 har mahine daalo toh…") may be shown, with the "example" tag (§18); **never decorative fake metrics** (made-up response times, percentages, revenue). `check: V-DATA`
- **H15 Spelling:** romanised Hinglish may be phonetic, but English words and brand names are exact. `check: V-CAPTION`
- **H16 Hold before you cut.** Titles and labels hold ≥ 10 f after building. On-screen text holds ≥ 0.25 s per word. `check: G3 + review`
- **H17 Audio:** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP (NC-8). A hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word. `check: mix gate + review`
- **H18 Deterministic, frame-based animation:** all crowds, particles and shakes come from seeded noise of the frame number (NC-9); max yellow + 2 bright hues per frame. `check: V-HUES + review`

### 2.4 NEVER list N1–N12
- **N1** Readable code, stack traces or raw error logs as a main visual. Illegible code-shaped lines are allowed as `TC-decorative` texture inside a scanner, magnifier or blur (e.g. P-52). Commands go in chips.
- **N2** Contrast failures: white or yellow text on the white canvas; dark logos on dark footage; red text on the night stage without a white outline; pink text on white without an ink stroke.
- **N3** Raw full-bleed screen recordings. Use a card, a device mock, the depth sandwich, or a ≤ 2.5 s full-screen moment with a frame.
- **N4** Meme cues or comedy visuals on `explain`, `awe` or `cta` beats, or under a trigger word. Meme sounds from the excluded categories (§11).
- **N5** The same sound file more than 2× (outside the list cue). The same zoom type twice in a row. The same transition 3× in a row.
- **N6** More than 3 bright hues in one frame (yellow + 2). Comedy Pink outside the comedy layer. Coloured words inside subtitles.
- **N7** Niche clichés: hooded hackers, glowing robot brains, Matrix rain, random stock tech footage; [NICHE: example B] money rain, stock-chart-going-up clips, piles of cash. Use mechanisms instead (red cursors, gift box, data wall, bouncer; a leaking bucket, coins counted into jars).
- **N8** Transitions outside `T-01…T-24` or zooms outside `Z-1…Z-7`. No RGB-split packs, film burns or lens-flare PNGs.
- **N9** Fake UIs presented as real products, fake dashboards, invented revenue or testimonials. Bad and premium example screens are generic, unbranded mocks or the creator's own builds.
- **N10** A black tail > 0.2 s, or an outro louder than the voice.
- **N11** Text layers overlapping (a subtitle under a title, a sticker over the slab).
- **N12** Visuals that only decorate. Every motion graphic shows the thing being said.

## §6 Hook system `[REQ]`

### 7.3 Item ritual (identical for every item)
1. G-1 panel drop (or T-18 zoom-through into the Data Stage, or the SM-9 chapter interstitial) 0–8 f before the ordinal word. **List cue** (the one allowed repeat).
2. Marker 0.6–1.2 s.
3. Title + card or Data Theatre pattern on the tool or risk name (±1 f).
4. 2–4 beats of evolving visuals, one per noun, every ≤ 1.5 s.
5. **Pop-back (G-2)** to the presenter for the opinion, aside or mock line, with a Z-1 or Z-2.
6. Optional reaction bubble (G-4) when the visual must stay full screen while they react.

### 7.4 Open loops and re-hooks
- Count loop (markers count up to N).
- "Last one is the most dangerous / best" loop (r10: "last wala sabse khatarnak").
- Deliverable loop (the DM deliverable).
- All loops are paid off on screen.
- Duration `short`: no mid-reel re-hook obligation; the intro (hook + loop) ≤ 25 % of the runtime and ≤ 14 s.

### 7.5 Infotainment rhythm and energy curve
- **Every 6–10 s, an entertainment beat:** a roast (`mock`), skit line, reaction bubble, freeze-frame or sticker. It must also carry information; no filler jokes.
- **Never two comedy beats back to back.** Between them comes at least one `explain` / `win` beat.
- **Energy curve:** hook (max) → item 1 (high) → middle items (steady, alternate the worlds) → the last item (escalate: Data Stage or `S-DARK`, a bigger hero move) → CTA (clean, confident).

### 8.5 Data and truth rules (D5: make it so they *see* it)
1. **Quantity is countable.** 5 means five avatars and 5,000 means five thousand dots. Never a lone number on a card.
2. **Same axes.** A comparison uses identical frames and scales (P-44, `scale_id`, §18); only the variable changes.
3. **Show the mechanism, not the jargon.** An index is a book index, caching is make-once-serve-many, rate limiting is a bouncer. The label names it once (CS-3 violet), then the machine runs.
4. **State change = colour change.** OK is cyan or green, stressed is yellow, broken is red. The change lands on the spoken word ("aaye" / "phat jayega").
5. **One idea per screen.** Max 3 animated groups at once. Everything else is dimmed 40 %.
6. **Sync to speech:** counts finish on the number word (±5 f), crashes land on the crash word, fixes land on the fix verb.
7. **Real or script-stated numbers only** (H14, NC-6). Units and labels in plain language ("users", "requests", "₹ per month"). Script-hypothetical numbers carry the "example" tag (§18).
8. **Escalate.** The last item gets the biggest data move (hero number, full-screen stage, reaction bubble).

- Gate: **`mock` beats only** (plus the one allowed `win` meme cue). Never on `explain`, `awe` or `cta` beats, never under a trigger word.
- Budgets: stickers ≤ 1 on screen, ≤ 5 per reel; Z-2 ≤ 2 per reel; T-22 ≤ 2 per reel; meme cues ≤ 4 per reel, ≥ 4 s apart; an entertainment beat every 6–10 s; never two comedy beats back to back.
- Comedy Pink (`comedy`) appears only inside this layer (scene `roles: ["comedy"]` only in mock beats).
- A buyer may lower `comedy` to `light` (visual gags, no meme cues) or `off` (VAR); `mock` beats then keep their stamps/markers (light) or become `explain` beats (off).

### 8.7 Asset rules
- **Real captures** for tools, sites and prompts the creator names (their own screen recordings).
- **Result mocks** (bad/premium sites, phone apps) are built live in the renderer as **generic, unbranded** designs (B-15), or captured from the creator's actual builds (preferred for premium).

| **Ducking** | The bed sits 18–22 dB under the voice while the voice speaks (`duck_db: -18` floor) |
| **Loudness** | −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP; voice chain high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, light compression; hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word; an optional ≤ 1 s button ≤ voice level (NC-8) |

The tone → sound mapping (S2): `hype` whoosh / sub-hit / pop; `mock` meme / marker / stamp; `awe` riser → impact, shine, sparkle; `explain` UI pops, digital text, light typing; `warn` tension riser, alarm, glitch, low boom; `win` ding, level-up, correct; `cta` key click, shine, swoosh.
