# Cinematic List / Montage Style Playbook (template v2)

## The feel

This reel looks like it cost money and sounds like a thought. A dark room, one lamp, the creator mid-sentence, and a
single slab of cobalt swinging into place above their head while its words type themselves out, as the camera eases back
from too close. Under it, one small white word at a time. Nothing shouts. You stop because it looks calm and expensive in
a feed that is neither.

Then the words take over. Each arrives alone, and its size says what it's worth: small words whisper, the words that
carry the idea step up in a heavy sans, and once a line the word that carries the feeling lands huge in an italic serif,
as if the creator leaned in and lowered their voice. Size is meaning. That's the engine.

It moves in one of two ways. In the list, the take never cuts: a numbered title in italic serif and a photo hanging behind
the creator's head swap on every "one", "two", "three", flicker once and hold, and the stillness is the point. The creator
talks; the list builds above them like a gallery wall. In the montage, the creator's own footage cuts on the words
themselves: the back of their head at a glowing desk, hands at work, a silhouette at a window, a run of pictures on a run
of words, then a breath on their face for the line that matters. Fast, fast, fast, still. Pictures on the nouns, the face
on the opinion.

One blue is the only loud thing. Cobalt means read this: the promise, the stressed word, the one decisive word on a full
blue frame. Everything else is white, cream and shadow. No shakes, no stickers, no bounce: the energy is the cut, the jump
in size and a short blur breathing through the frame. It ends on one word in curly quotes, cream and glowing: the word to
type.

**The test:** pause on any frame: it could be a still from a short film, and the one word on it tells you how to feel.

## What this playbook is

You're editing a calm, premium talking-head reel of a creator who teaches or reflects from their desk: a list of ways,
habits, tools or mistakes, or an idea, a process or a story told over their own footage. You have the authority to make it
the most expensive-looking reel in their niche. This playbook is the style: how it looks, moves, sounds and thinks, pulled
from five of ant.lon's reels (v01–v05: two lists, three montages), measured frame by frame, then sharpened. Read it all,
every time. Use it the way a great editor uses a reference: take what fits this reel, invent when a moment needs more, and
never ship a frame that breaks the feel above.

**Who it's for and what it needs.** Creators who talk to camera from a dark, practically lit room: editors, designers,
coaches, money and fitness creators, anyone whose reel is a list or a reflection. One style, two formats (§3.1): **F-A
List overlay** needs only one take (their own photos for the items lift it; a tool or brand an item names gets its real
logo or screen, fetched; anything else missing is built); **F-B Cinematic
montage** needs the take plus a bank of the creator's own cinematic B-roll (25 or more usable clips; 12–24 makes a thinner
montage; under 12, the reel is a list). The person cut-out (matte) is needed for F-A (the item photos hang behind the head)
and for anything behind the head in F-B; make sure it's there before the storyboard. Captions are verbatim, English or Hinglish in Latin script;
Devanagari works but loses the italic word (the serif has no Devanagari italic), so prefer Latin script. Machine values live
in `tokens.json`; where this text gives a number tokens also holds, they agree.

## §1 Procedure: how to approach a reel in this style

You watch, listen, plan, build and look at the storyboard yourself; the edit skill has the mechanics. This style
lives or dies on three decisions: which format the reel is, which word of every line is the big one, and (in the montage)
which words get a new picture. Make them in this order.

1. **Pick the format** (§3.1). The script runs through parallel items (ways, habits, tools, mistakes) and the creator
   recorded one take → **F-A**. The script is an argument, a parable, a process of a handful of steps or a story, **and**
   the bank holds 25 or more usable clips → **F-B**. A bank of 12–24 → F-B thinner (FB-1; note it in `ideas.md`). Under 12 →
   F-A whatever the script (FB-1b: the key points become the numbered titles).
2. **Count on the cut-out.** F-A hangs every item photo behind the head, and every behind-the-head numeral, ring, card
   wall or community card needs it; it's also how the engine sees the real line of the hair (`matte: optional`, so it
   runs once the scenes are written; make sure it's there before the storyboard).
3. **Tag the bank (F-B).** From contact strips, write one row per clip in your notes: `{id: "BR-07", asset, shot:
   "SH-1…SH-6", subject, mood: calm|tense|warm|cold|bright, light: practical|daylight|night, motion: static|slow|fast,
   faces: true|false, usable: [[in, out], …], best_in}`. Count the usable clips now; it decides the format.
4. **Segment.** F-A: `HOOK` (until the first ordinal word) → `ITEM-1…N`, each opening on its ordinal ("one", "first",
   "number two", "next") → `CTA`. F-B: `HOOK` → `SEC-1…n`, each opening on its section word ("first", "the second thing",
   "step three") or a topic turn → `CTA`. In F-A mark only the take's in and out: no cuts inside it (§7.3).
5. **Feel the tone of every line:** `explain` · `awe` · `hype` · `warn` · `win` · `cta`. There is no mock. The tone decides
   the caption treatment, the camera and what the picture leans toward (§10.3): awe goes italic and pushes in, a warning
   turns the plate black and white with one red word.
6. **The word-tier pass** (this style's own craft). In each line, find the one word you'd underline if you could underline
   only one: the judgement ("hard", "alive", "elite"), the contrarian verb ("stop", "never"), the time or quantity that is
   the point ("6 months", "2 years"), or the noun the reel is about. That word goes big (tier 2): `captions.overrides: {i,
   emph: true}`; switch off engine picks that land on weak words with `{i, emph: false}`. Never a stop-word, never two big
   words back to back, never the first word of a sentence unless it is the point. In F-A the big word is rare and earned;
   in F-B it's the heartbeat of the montage, but a run of big words stops being emphasis.
7. **Write the hook** (§6): F-A the chip and its sub-line, F-B the opening word and the first plate. Write 8–10 candidates,
   pick by the stopper test (§6.1), keep two alternates.
8. **Plan the pictures.** F-A: the item visual for every item (a photo, a real logo, a screen, a counter ring) and the
   one internal event an item may carry. F-B: **the burst map**: mark every content word `cut` (a new plate starts on it),
   `hold` (the picture continues) or `frame` (a type frame), then match a bank clip to every cut by subject and mood
   (§9.3). Where no clip fits, use a type frame (P-14/P-15/P-16) or an A-roll punch (P-27), never a stranger's clip.
9. **Plan the numbers.** Every proof count (views, clients, kilos, savings) gets its figure in `plan/figures.json`
   (`from: script` with the spoken words, or `from: creator`) and its counter (§7.6).
10. **Third-party moments:** fetch the real thing, source noted (§12.4). **Sound:** sparse (§11).

## §2 Hard rules: the craft and the style's never-list

**Craft, by eye** (judge it on the storyboard, in context, the way an editor does):
- **Keep the person clear.** Keep the head region (face, hair and the room above the head top, from the cut-out) clear of
  front layers, captions included; a word brushing the chin for a beat is editing, a head chopped or a face buried by
  accident is not. The framing that does it: in F-A the title row (y 205–300), the chip (y ≈ 330–470) and the sub-line
  (y ≈ 487–543) sit above a head whose top sits at y 570–660, and the micro caption sits on the chest (cy 1130), far below
  the chin. Photos, rings, card walls and the community card that reach the head hang **behind** it (`behind: true`): the
  head and hair draw in front. In F-B a face-beat caption sits under the chin (cy 1000; the engine moves it lower when the
  chin is low); in L-split its bottom edge sits 24 px above the seam (y 932) and the head stays in the band below y 956.
  Front graphics keep 24 px between their bottom edge and the head region (§3.7).
- **Text behind the head stays readable.** For its whole hold, ≥ 65 % of its glyph area is visible and its first and last
  glyphs are at least half visible; one behind-the-head text at a time; it holds ≥ 0.6 s. Behind text sets `behind: true`,
  `exception: "behind_text"` and `text_class: "TC-display"` so the engine measures the occlusion.
- **No text over text.** One caption word, one title or chip, one picture element. Items never share a frame: the next
  title starts only after the last one is gone. Big type (z8) hides the captions while it's up.
- **On the word.** Titles, photos, counters, numerals and type frames start 2 f before their trigger word and are fully on
  within ±5 f; a caption word appears ≤ 1 f before its onset. Every F-B cut lands on a content word's onset −2 f (±2 f),
  never inside a word and never on a function word ("the", "and", "of"). The voice is never cut or offset.
- **Say what was said.** Every number about the creator (their views, clients, kilos, savings, followers, their profile)
  comes from the script or the creator (`plan/figures.json`); never an invented result, dashboard or a verification tick
  they don't have. Illustrations (a file name, a generic settings value, a made-up card) may use made-up but realistic
  numbers and names, with no label (`illustrative: true`). A number or quote the creator says is shown exactly as said.
- **Promise integrity.** The chip's count equals the items shown (F-A) or the section numerals (F-B); numerals count up
  without gaps; every loop the hook opens is paid on screen; the CTA keyword appears exactly as spoken, ≥ 1.5 s.
- **Spelling.** Captions verbatim; tool, brand and place names exact (the glossary); punctuation kept ("you." carries its
  full stop); every number through `ctx.fmtNum`.
- **Readable.** Display type ≥ 40 px; captions ≥ 54 px except the two quiet spans (`E3`): CS-1 at 46 px and CS-3 at 48 px
  on its chip, weight ≥ 600, one line, ≤ 16 characters, contrast ≥ 7:1 on footage (CS-1) or ≥ 4.5:1 on the chip (CS-3).
  A CS-1 word that measures under 7:1 (a light shirt, a bright wall) switches that span to CS-4 (54 px). Labels ≥ 40 px;
  handle and disclosure ≥ 22 px (the small-print class). Contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (3:1 for display ≥ 96 px). Meaning text stays
  inside x 64–1016, y 110–1500, and never in x > 970 between y 900 and 1540. Text holds ≥ 0.25 s per word; titles hold
  ≥ 10 f after they finish building; a colour frame holds ≥ 0.4 s.
- **Smooth.** Two camera moves never collide (≥ 0.4 s apart); every frame is a pure function of its index (seeded
  randomness).
- **Audio.** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP, the bed ≥ 18 dB under the voice, the B-roll's own sound muted, a
  hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word, a black tail ≤ 0.2 s.

**Never in this style:**
- Stock clichés: stock B-roll, "hustle" city time-lapses that aren't the creator's, AI-generated scenes, famous paintings
  or press photos as decoration; strangers' footage as montage plates. (A real logo, post, screen or artwork the reel
  names is fetched and shown as itself, §12.4.)
- A second saturated text colour: no yellow, orange, teal or pink type. Cobalt is the only colour that carries words on
  footage, and there it is a fill behind white text, never cobalt letters (they vanish on dark plates). Red type only on a
  P-24 mono plate or on paper; green only inside P-39.
- Shakes, crash zooms, rotation snaps, whip pans, glitch packs, RGB split, film burns, light-leak PNGs, bouncy overshoot.
- Stickers, emoji, memes, comedy sounds, marker scribbles.
- Two caption words of the same size side by side as a sentence; 2–3-word subtitle cards.
- All-caps captions or titles. Case is as spoken; a caps pair ("STEP BY STEP") appears only as a P-17 stacked pair, and
  only when it is the reel's spoken thesis.
- A grade on the A-roll; the same wash on two plates running.
- Fake UI presented as real.
- A fade in from black, a chip that flies off, a dissolve between plates.
- Decoration for its own sake: every chip, card, frame and numeral shows the word being said.

## §3 Worlds, layouts, stage moves, safe zones

### 3.1 Two formats, one style
One look in two shapes. Both share the cobalt chip, the italic-serif and heavy-sans duet, the one-word captions, the dark
premium grade and the quoted keyword after a community card; only the montage cuts.

| | F-A List overlay (the default) | F-B Cinematic montage |
|---|---|---|
| When | Numbered tips, ways, habits, tools or mistakes, delivered in one continuous take (5–10 items, about half a minute) | An idea, a process or a story told over the creator's own cinematic B-roll (3–7 sections, about a minute) |
| Needs | "Just you talking" (+ optional photos for the items) | "You + your own B-roll": 25–40 usable clips (12–24 → FB-1; under 12 → edit as F-A) |
| Layouts | L-full the whole reel; L-low as a framing fix | L-full (the breaths), L-broll (the plates), L-type (the type frames), L-split (screens) |
| The face | Never leaves | The anchor you return to: every section comes back to it, and the CTA is on it |
| Default hook | HA-05 Promise chip | HA-19 Mood montage |
| Structure | A list; marker SM-1 "n. Title" | An essay; marker SM-2 "No.n" (SM-3 "step n" for a process) |
| Captions | CS-1 micro duet (46 px) | CS-2 duet (64 / 102 / 160 px) |

For reference, measured on the source reels (a description, not a target): the lists ran 29 and 34 s with no cuts; the
montages ran 55–76 s at 26–29 cuts a minute, median shot 1.0–1.6 s, and the face on screen for roughly a fifth to a half of
the reel.

### 3.6 Safe zones and bands
- **Meaning text:** x 64–1016, y 110–1500; nothing meaningful in x > 970 between y 900 and 1540.
- **Headline band:** chip cy 400 (top ≥ 330), sub-line cy 515. **Title row:** y 205–300 (cy 250). **Above-head zone:**
  y 300–780 for photos, rings, tiles, the community card and the keyword (§3.7 for what goes in front and what behind).
- **Caption bands:** F-A chest cy 1130 (L-low 1250); F-B face cy 1000 (pushed lower when the chin is lower); plates and
  type frames cy 960; L-split bottom edge at y 932.
- **Side bands for step numerals (P-09):** left x 120–420, right x 640–960, at cy 1120, ≥ 40 px clear of the head region.

### 3.7 The person
- **People come for the creator.** In F-A the face never leaves the screen. In F-B it's the anchor: every section comes
  back to it, the opinions and the numbers are said to it, the CTA is on it. Returns are always hard cuts (G-2), never a
  slide or a shrink.
- **Framing.** F-A: head top y 560–660, face centre x 440–640 (setup A; the engine's base reframe puts the take there). The
  hook's sub-line ends at y ≈ 543, so the hair has to start at y ≈ 570 or lower in the frame; a take that can't get there
  gets G-5. F-B: head top y 360–580, a little tighter (setup B), so the face can fill the frame after a burst.
- **The head line.** Every front element above the head (the chip, the sub-line, the title row, the keyword, the stacked
  pair, pills and tiles) keeps its bottom edge 24 px above the head region: the real hair line from the cut-out, not the
  brows. When the take puts the head higher, the element moves up (never into the title row) or the reel lowers the face
  (G-5).
- **Behind the head is the depth layer.** Pictures that need to reach lower (the F-A item photo, the counter ring, the card
  wall, the community card's collage, the behind-head numeral) are drawn behind the person with `behind: true`: the head
  and hair stay whole in front, and the graphic seems to hang in the room. The source let the photos overlap the hair and
  once reached the eyebrows; this style doesn't. Text that goes behind keeps ≥ 65 % visible (§2).
- **The pull-out open.** Z-3 starts at 2.0×. About the face, the head would fill the chip's band at f0, so in this style it
  pulls out about the chip line (`p: {origin: {x: 540, y: 400}}`): the head starts low and rises into place under the chip,
  never into it.
- **In F-B face beats** the face sits higher, so the room above the head is small: an above-head element on a face beat
  hangs behind the person or waits for a plate. The caption sits under the chin.
- **In L-split** the head is cropped into the band below the seam (y 956) and nothing crosses the seam toward it; the
  caption's bottom edge stops at y 932.

### 4.2 Meanings
- **Cobalt = read this.** It marks the promise (the chip), the word being stressed (a chip highlight) and the decisive single

- **Red = the mistake; green = the level reached.** Fixed meanings; never decoration.
- **Cream = the ceremony.** Section numerals and the CTA keyword are cream-to-white, warmer than the caption white, so they
  read as "chapter" and "the word to type".
- **A sponsor's colours** appear only inside P-48.
- **Footage keeps its own colour.** The creator's room (teal-green, warm window light) is the palette's background.

### 4.3 Rules
- **Cobalt plus, at most, one other bright** in a frame: the violet ring, the red word or the green panel. On a paper frame,
  cobalt alone.
- Coloured text on paper is cobalt (6.75:1) or red at ≥ 96 px; never violet or green text on paper.
- On footage, cobalt is a **fill** behind white text, never the colour of the letters.
- No glow except the CTA keyword (24 px cream glow) and the section numeral (30 px cream glow at 35 %).

## §6 Hook system

**The hook title.** The chip promises the viewer something: an outcome they want, a curiosity gap, or who it's for ("10
ways to earn money" + "as a freelance designer", not the label "Freelancing tips"). It doesn't have to repeat the spoken
words; it has to be true to what the reel delivers. A title shown as someone's words (in quotes) is word for word. This
section sets the chip's shape (§5.2) and the hook's choreography; the voice is the creator's. Write 8–10 candidates from
the formulas (§6.6) and the proven patterns ("How to X as a Y", "Why your X isn't working", "The X nobody tells you",
"Stop doing X", a number or a contrast), score them on outcome, curiosity, who it's for and brevity, check the best against
the stopper test, pick, and keep the next two as alternates.

In F-B the hook is a mood, not a title: a picture already moving, one word arriving, the premise complete on screen within
three seconds. The promise lives in the sentence; the edit makes you feel it.

### 6.1 The stopper test
| Test | F-A | F-B |
|---|---|---|
| Thumbnail (f0 at 25 %) | The chip reads at 26 px (104 px × 0.25) and the face is visible | (a mood open: the plate and the first word carry it) |
| Mute (first 3 s) | Chip + sub-line + micro captions tell the promise | The premise sentence is on screen word by word by 3.0 s |
| Motion at f0 | The chip swinging in + the live presenter under the pull-out | The rack-focus plate + the opening word's blur-in |
| Read time | The chip in ≤ 1.2 s (≤ 5 words) | — |
| Payoff | The chip readable by 0.7 s; item 1 starts by 4.0 s | The premise by 3.0 s; the face or the first section by 6.0 s |

The first three seconds feel full without feeling busy: the chip types, the sub-line follows, the camera settles, a word
arrives at the chest; each thing lands while the last one settles.

### 7.3 The unit ritual (identical every time)
**The F-A item ritual** (frames at 30 fps; 0 = the ordinal word's onset):
| Frame | Event |
|---|---|
| −1 → 0 | The previous title and photo vanish on one frame (hard out, no fade) |
| 0 | **P-10** "n. Title" and **P-05** item visual appear on the same frame, in place, no scale or slide; the photo hangs behind the head |
| 0 → +3 | The title **flickers** once (opacity 1 → 0.35 → 1, one frame each); the photo stays solid (measured v04 @0:09.12, @0:13.92; v03 @0:09.09, @0:15.16) |
| +3 → end | Hold; the micro captions continue; the item's own word may go big; one internal event at most (a counter landing, or a second photo hard-swapping in on the next noun) |
| 0 | The list cue on the ordinal (the one sound allowed to repeat) |

The take never cuts after item 1: no cut and no footage blur on items 2…N (the item-1 entry is the hook's ritual, §6.2).
The take keeps its breaths; a slip may be fixed with one hidden cut on an item boundary, never with a visible jump.
Items never share a frame. For reference, the source swapped items every 2.0–2.6 s (v04) and 3.0–3.7 s (v03): the speech
sets the length.

**The F-B section ritual:**
| Step | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | A hard cut (T-01) to a fresh plate on the section word, or the marker behind the creator on a face beat (v01 "No.2") |
| 2 | **P-08** "No.n" lands on the cut frame and flickers in over 5 f (v01 @0:37.12) or de-blurs over 4 f (v05 @0:09.82); the sub-line builds on its spoken words (captions hidden while it's up, 1.2–2.0 s); or **P-09** "step n" (1.0–1.5 s) |

### 7.4 Open loops and the re-hook
- **Loops:** the count loop (the chip's number, paid by the last item or numeral), the parable loop ("Picture this…" →
  "Here's why."), the deliverable loop (the community card at the end). Every one is paid on screen.
- **The re-hook (F-B):** the middle section's numeral card is the re-hook (`kind: "rehook"` on its scene, or `rehook: true`
  on the beat); a question type frame (P-14 / P-16, the question as spoken) does the same job. In F-A every item is a
  re-hook.
- **The hook is over fast:** the first item or the turn arrives while the promise is fresh.

### 7.5 Rhythm: burst and breath
**F-A is calm by design.** The take is still; the energy is the swap: a new title and photo on every ordinal, the micro
words at the chest, a big word now and then. The last item (the CTA item) is the warmest: the community card is the largest,
brightest picture in the reel.

**F-B breathes like a heartbeat with a held breath.** Hook (a mood, a slow de-blur) → the turn (the face, the italic word)
→ sections that go **burst → breath** → the last section escalates (the longest burst, a colour frame, a macro) → the CTA
on the face, still and clean, ending on the cream keyword.
- **A burst** is a run of pictures on a run of words, one subject family, each plate on its content word. It's fast
  because the words are fast; the last plate holds as the landing.
- **A breath** is the creator's face for the line that matters: the opinion, "here's why", a number. It lasts as long as
  the thought, and if it runs long, something lands on it (a proof element, a split, a stacked pair) so it never sits.
- **Contrast is the rhythm.** Never two bursts back to back without a breath; never a run of breaths without a burst. The
  pictures come because the words give them a reason, never to fill a gap.
- For reference, measured on the three montages (a description, not a target): bursts of 3–7 cuts in 0.9–2.8 s (v01
  16.08–18.84, 39.36–40.28; v02 33.4–35.4; v05 6.32–8.24), breaths of 2–8 s on the face, the longest single picture 9–11.6 s
  and always the face with graphics landing on it.

### 7.6 Running state (counters)
- **The state variable** `views` (`tokens.running_state.vars`): a counter, start 0, formatted as numbers, shown by P-30,
  living within its beat. It holds any proof count (views, followers, clients, kilos, savings, days). Ops per beat:
  `state_ops [{var: "views", op: "tick_to", value, at}]`, with `value` from `plan/figures.json` (`from: script` with the
  spoken words, or `from: creator`).
- **Display:** one position per world (on L-full behind the person, centred above the head; on W-paper centre (540, 760));
  the value only changes by rolling; it lands within ±5 f of the spoken number; it never contradicts the caption.
- Counts shown by P-32 / P-33 are figures too (`kind: counter`, `formula: none`).

### 7.7 Series furniture (when the creator runs a series)
The series tag speaks the SM-2 numeral language: "No.{n}" in the cream serif, 120 px, at cy 250, for 1.2 s inside the
hook (between 0 and 3 s); the name and number come from the reel brief.

### 8.5 Data and truth
- **Every counter or stat about the creator is a figure** in `plan/figures.json` (`kind: counter`, `formula: none`,
  `from: script` with the spoken words, or `from: creator`).
- Profile pills and tiles show the creator's real name, handle and counts; never a verification tick they don't have.
- No charts and no invented results. An illustration (a file label, a generic UI value, a made-up card) may use made-up but
  realistic numbers and names, with no label (`illustrative: true`); it never poses as the creator's result.

### 8.6 Assets
- **Real captures first:** the creator's own plates, stills, screen recordings and profile; then the real logos, screens,
  posts and artworks the reel names, fetched from the web with the source noted.
- **Built when nothing turns up:** generic, unbranded UI; logo plates set in type (P-06); type cards (FB-8).
- **Never:** stock, AI scenes, famous artworks or press photos as decoration; a real one appears only when the reel names
  it.
- **Blur** personal data on screens.
- **Fetch the real thing** for every third-party moment (§12.4).

### 9.3 Montage shot grammar (F-B)
- **R-1 Cut on the word.** Every plate starts on the onset of a content word (noun, verb, number, adjective) −2 f, and
  the content word is the one the plate shows ("headphones" → SH-3 with headphones).
- **R-2 Literal first, mood second.** If the bank has a literal match for the word, use it; else the mood match (tags
  `mood`, `light`); else a type frame. Never a mismatched literal (a phone when the word is "notebook").
- **R-3 Burst families.** A burst stays in one family: the same location from several angles (desk: back of the head →
  hands → screen glow → profile) or one action across places (writing → typing → editing). Never mix bright daylight and
  night practicals inside one burst.
- **R-4 Burst speed.** Plates inside a burst move at the speed of the words (0.4–1.0 s each in the source); the last plate
  of a burst holds as the landing (1.0–2.5 s).
- **R-5 Breathe on the face.** After every burst, return to the face (G-2) or hold one plate; the opinion line, "here's
  why", the numbers and the CTA are always on the face.
- **R-6 Macro on attention words.** "Look", "notice", "focus", "detail" → an SH-5 macro (P-26).
- **R-7 Silhouettes for the abstract.** "Goal", "future", "years", "dream" → SH-4 environment and silhouette plates (the
  amber wash suits them, once).
- **R-8 No repeats in reach.** A plate is a new picture every time: a clip seen a moment ago reads as a mistake, and a
  section never opens and closes on the same plate.

### 9.4 How the moves breathe
The cut is the drama, so most moves are invisible: hard cuts on words, hard swaps on ordinals, titles that flicker once
and hold. The blur pulse is the style's only accent: a breath through the whole frame on a structural beat (the item-1
cut, a face push, the keyword), never a decoration on every word. The blur-through and the wash change the mood between
plates, so they come when the mood really changes. In F-A the only moves are the hidden cut, the title swaps and the chip's
exit; in F-B the cuts carry the rhythm and the colour frames punctuate it. Inside a burst's peak the source once flashed a
phrase white for a single frame (v01 @0:16.72, "you need to"); it's allowed where a burst peaks. Cuts sit within ±1 f of
word boundaries.

## §11 Sound

Quiet and premium: the voice, a calm bed, and a sound only where something lands. (The source reels' sound couldn't be
measured, so this is a decision, not a measurement.)

| Line | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Where sound goes** | The hook (one cue on f0); F-B burst starts (one soft whoosh on a burst's first cut, not on every cut; a type frame may take a soft tick); reveals (a counter landing, the community card); the list cue (F-A: one file on every ordinal, the one sound allowed to repeat); the CTA (one soft shine on the keyword quote). Caption swaps never carry a cue. If in doubt, leave it out |
| **Meme sounds** | None |
| **The bed** | On from f0, calm and premium, under the voice the whole reel; a 0.5 s drop-out allowed before the keyword quote |
| **Ducking** | The bed sits ≥ 18 dB under the voice while the voice speaks; the B-roll's own sound is muted (plates are picture only) |
| **Loudness** | −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP; a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word |

No file more than twice in a reel, except the list cue.

  caption, not the prop; CS-1 `avoid_face` + 40 px clearance).
- **No reaction bank.**
- **The cut-out (matte):** for the whole F-A take (the photos hang behind the head) and for any F-B face beat with a
  behind-the-person element. It also lets the engine see the real hair line. Check the hair at 200 % on the frames where a
  photo or numeral sits behind it.
- **Resolution:** a 1.35× punch needs 1080p; a 1.6× punch or a 2× macro crop needs 4K (2160 px).

### 12.4 Third-party moments: fetch the real thing
1. **Find** the moments in the transcript that call for third-party material (a platform's logo, a tool's screen, a famous
   artwork, another creator's post, a person's photo).
2. **The creator's own files come first;** otherwise search the web and fetch it: the real logo, the real screen or page
   (captured and framed on the part that matters), the real post, the real artwork. Note where it came from.
3. **Use it as it is** (P-06 logo, P-07 screenshot, P-05 photo), framed in this style's cards, never altered to say
   something it doesn't; a post is shown word for word.
4. **Nothing usable to be found:** rebuild it, in this style:
   - a platform, tool or company → a **P-06 logo plate** (the name set in Inter Tight 800 on a white card; `fx.logoPlate`);
   - an app screen → **P-40** `fx.appUI`, a recreated unbranded UI;
   - a post or quote → `fx.quoteCard` in the dark glass skin, verbatim;
   - a person → `fx.silhouette` (name and role from the script);
   - a famous artwork or press photo → an F-A type card (FB-8).

   Created cards carry no credit line and no label.

### 12.5 Frame rate and audio
30 fps CFR output, 1080×1920, BT.709. One voice track (the take): high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, light compression, −14 LUFS.
Plates are silent.

## §15 Your look at the storyboard: the checklist

Watch it once as a stranger with a thumb over the next reel, then once as the editor whose name is on it. Fix what
bothers you, in one pass.

**The style (does it feel like §The feel?)**
- Frame 0 is moving and premium: the chip swinging in and typing under the pull-out (F-A), or the rack-focus plate and one
  word (F-B).
- Size is meaning: the big word in each line is the word you'd underline, never a stop-word, never two in a row.
- F-A: the take never cuts after item 1; every item uses the identical ritual; the last item is the warmest picture.
- F-B: the pictures cut on the words and match them; bursts breathe on the face; the opinions and numbers are said to the
  face; the last section escalates; every section opens with its marker.
- One blue: cobalt carries the words, plus at most one other bright; cream only for the numerals and the keyword.
- Calm: no shakes, no bounces, no stickers; the camera moves only where it means something; grades are events, never on
  the A-roll.
- It ends on the quoted keyword, still and clean, after the community card.
- Start to end: it looks like a short film, and you feel each line before you read it.

**Craft (by eye, in context)**
- The person reads whole: the chip and sub-line clear the hair under the pull-out at f0; F-A photos, rings and cards hang
  behind the head with the hair intact in front; in L-split the caption sits above the seam and the head below it;
  nothing chops the head or buries the face by accident.
- Text behind the head stays readable (most of it visible, first and last glyphs clear).
- One word per caption chunk (two only in CS-3), the tiers clearly 1× / 1.6× / 2.5×; the quiet spans readable on a phone;
  nothing clipped; no text over text by accident.
- Every title, photo, counter, numeral and type frame lands on its word; every F-B cut on a content word.
- Every number about the creator is theirs or spoken, in `figures.json`, and lands on its word; illustrations carry no
  labels; profiles show real facts.
- Names, tools and brands spelt exactly; the chip's count = the items or numerals; numerals ascend without gaps; the
  keyword on screen as spoken, long enough to read.
- The plates and stills are the creator's; fetched logos, screens and posts are the real ones, posts word for word;
  personal data blurred.
- The plates are silent under the voice. The file itself (1080×1920, 30 fps, −14 LUFS, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last
  word on the keyword) is the render's job; it checks it.

## §16 Build notes
- **Fonts:** Inter Tight (500–800) and Instrument Serif regular and italic; pre-load both before the first frame (the chip
  types at f0).
- **The chip** is one scene (`kind: "chip"`, z10) with the sub-line inside it; its width tracks the typed text.
- **Behind the person:** P-04, P-05, P-30 (on a face), P-35, P-45 and a face-beat P-08 set `behind: true` and need the
  cut-out; text among them also sets `exception: "behind_text"`.
- **Plates** are clip scenes `kind: "broll"` at z3 on a hidden stage, with their grade as a CSS filter; the rack focus and
  washes are filter ramps driven by `lt`.
- **Built-ins, not workarounds:** T-11 is `timeline.grades {t, blur: 14, dur, frame: true}`; T-02 is a `transitions`
  entry with `type: "blur-through"`; the CS-2 flicker is the caption swap itself; the camera presets carry their own blur.
- **Glass** is a flat fill + border, never stacked `backdrop-filter`.
