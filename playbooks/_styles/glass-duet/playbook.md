# Glass Duet Style Playbook (template v2)

## The feel

This reel feels expensive. Frame 0 is the creator in a room washed in one colour, violet or blue or teal, the shot still
settling as if the camera has just arrived, and under their chin the first word of the promise blurs into place in an
elegant copperplate script. Then a small clean word beside it. Then one word, heavy and huge. The caption isn't
subtitling them; it's typesetting them. Every sentence becomes a little piece of editorial design at chest height: the
soft words in script, the plain words small and quiet, the word that matters set bold and big. Watch it on mute and you
still hear how they talk.

Everything around them is glass, lit from inside in the room's own colour. When they count on their fingers, a glowing
diamond rises out of the counting hand and glides into place, and the camera leans in a breath to meet it. When they teach
a step, the face leans in, the frame cuts, and a glass card grows out of the dark holding the actual thing: the slide, the
setting, the before and after, a sparkle marking each part as they name it. Then a clean cut back, and the face lands
softly, a touch close, settling out. Show the thing, then the person. Show the thing, then the person.

Nothing here shouts. No punches, no shakes, no stickers, no gags. The energy lives in the type and the reveals, and every
motion is eased: blur in, glide, blur out. It moves like someone who knows exactly what they're teaching and has nothing
to prove. The face doesn't hide under the graphics: when the graphics need the screen, it floats into a card or a bubble
and joins the design. When they sell their own course, the same glass turns cinematic, a chrome
title flying at the lens, and it still never shouts.

One colour per reel, and every glow is that colour. The room and the graphics belong to each other, so the whole reel
looks like one premium product the creator built, not a template laid over a video.

**The test:** pause on any frame: the words look typeset, not subtitled, and every glow is the colour of the room; if
either fails, the frame is wrong.

## What this playbook is

You're editing one seated talking-head take of a creator teaching something (a method, a tool, N tips) or selling their
own course or program, and you have the authority to make it the most premium-looking reel in their niche. This playbook
is the style, pulled from five reels of a creator who teaches editing (v01 "this editing style is taking over", v02 "the
reason you still can't create a viral edit", v03 six techniques, v04 three reasons, v05 a course pitch) and measured frame
by frame at full resolution and full frame rate. Read it all, every time; take what fits the reel, invent where a moment
needs more, and never break the feel above.

**Who it's for and what it needs.** Teachers, coaches and course creators who sit and talk to camera in a set lit in one
saturated colour. One continuous take is enough; their exports, recordings, stills, result screenshots and the long
video's thumbnail make the glass cards real; a tool, post or page they name and didn't give is fetched from the web, and
anything else missing gets built (§12). The person cut-out is needed only for
the poster row behind the head (§8.3). Captions follow the creator's language in Latin script (English, or Hinglish
romanised as spoken), because the script voice has no Devanagari. Machine values live in `tokens.json`; where this text
gives a number tokens also holds, they agree.

**Two formats.** **F-A Tutorial explainer** (the default, 40–60 s): a promise with proof, "here are N…", one numbered item
after another, a recap, a link to the full video. **F-B Course sales** (90–125 s): "What is <product>?" as a chrome title,
the result claim, proof, what's inside module by module, the guarantee, a DM-or-comment keyword. They share the chest
captions, the glass language and the numbered markers; F-B adds italic caps captions, chrome titles, jump-cut reframes and
the colour-burn cut.

## §1 Procedure: how to approach a reel in this style

You watch, listen, plan, build and look at the storyboard yourself; the edit skill has the mechanics. These are the
decisions this style lives on, in the order that makes them easy.

1. **Pick the studio colour.** Look at the set around the head and shoulders (an early frame, the face excluded) and take
   the pack whose `primary` hue is nearest: violet, magenta or purple → TH-violet; blue → TH-blue; teal, cyan or green-grey
   → TH-teal; a dark room with neon bars or RGB practicals → TH-neon (§4.3). A neutral or white set takes the creator's
   brand colours if they have them, else TH-violet (F-A) or TH-neon (F-B). This is the reel's `theme`, and it never
   changes mid-reel.
2. **Pick the format and find the items.** Teaching → F-A: the promise, the "here are N" line, items 1…N, the recap, the
   CTA. Selling their own product → F-B: what it is, the result, proof, the re-hook questions, modules 1…N, the guarantee,
   the CTA. Count the items exactly as spoken; the diamonds must match.
3. **Write the hook** (§6): the promise lockup (8–10 candidates, the best by the stopper test, two alternates), the proof
   element that lands under it, the hero word. For F-B, the chrome title.
4. **Do the type-voice pass** (§5.3). Read every caption chunk the engine builds and set the two voices by hand where it
   guessed wrong: openers in script, numbers, brands and names never in script, one bold punch word per chunk at most, on a
   number, the topic noun or a name. This pass is what makes the captions look typeset.
5. **Pair every item with what it shows** (§7.3): R-A tutorial card when there's a process with stages to show, R-B chest
   window when one image or clip says it, R-C concept when there's nothing to show. Decide the exact content inside every
   window and card: which recording, which still, which bullets, which leader labels. The last item gets the richest
   picture.
6. **Feel the tone of each line:** `hype` (hook claims) · `awe` (reveals, results) · `explain` (steps) · `win` (the payoff)
   · `warn` (the mistake) · `cta`. The tone picks the camera breath and the colour (§10.2, §4.2).
7. **Plan the moves:** every world switch on a sentence start or an ordinal word, a push before each cut into a world, a
   landing after each cut back (§9.2).
8. **Fetch the third-party moments** (§12.4): the creator's files first, else the real thing from the web, source noted;
   **get the link-card art** (the long video's real thumbnail and exact title, from their files or their channel).
9. **Plan the sound** (§11): calm and sparse, a cue only where something lands.
10. **The cut-out only when it's needed.** The poster row behind the head needs it (`matte: optional`, so it runs once the
    scenes are written); make sure it's there before the storyboard.

## §2 Hard rules: the craft and the style's never-list

**Craft, by eye** (judge it on the storyboard, in context, the way an editor does):
- **Keep the person clear.** People come for the face, so keep the face, the hair and the room above the head clear of
  front layers, captions included, when the moment is about them. The framing that does it: on L-full the duet caption
  block starts 220 px under the face (anchor `chest`, `avoid_face`; fallback centre y 1120); top-band graphics either sit
  behind the head (`behind: true` with the cut-out) or end ≥ 40 px above the head top; nothing crosses the face card
  (L-card-pale) or the orbit bubble (L-pip-orbit). When the moment wants otherwise (a caption crossing the chin for a
  beat), that's editing; what's never fine is a head chopped or a face buried by accident. Behind the person is fair
  game, text included.
- **No text over text.** Captions hide under every z8 element (the promise build, the diamond marker and its title, chest
  windows, the checklist card, the chrome title, the video card, the keyword chip); a chest window and the duet caption
  never share the chest. A caption over a busy poster row or screen wall gets its scrim.
- **On the word.** Every marker, window, card, label and bullet starts 2 f before its trigger word and is fully settled
  within +5 f. Diamonds land on the ordinal word ("first", "two", "number three"). Captions show ≤ 0.15 s before the word
  (lead 1 f).
- **Say what was said.** A number or quote the creator says is shown exactly as said. A count, view number, rating,
  income figure or testimonial presented as the creator's is their real one, word for word. Illustrations (a mock
  outcome, a generic screen, a star-rating comparison) may use made-up but realistic numbers, with no label
  (`illustrative: true`); a five-star widget is a comparison device, never a real rating of someone else.
- **Promise integrity.** The diamonds or tiles equal the items spoken; the recap row shows all of them; "the full tutorial
  is on my YouTube" is paid off by the link or video card; the CTA keyword is on screen ≥ 1.5 s (F-B: to the last frame).
- **Spelling.** Brands, tools and names exact, and never set in the script face.
- **Readable.** Script ≥ 70 px (87 px standard), the small caption sans ≥ 54 px (56 standard); labels ≥ 40 px, or
  28–39 px only when the same words are spoken or shown larger (`exception: "E3"`, weight ≥ 500, contrast ≥ 7:1);
  the under-card caption 44 px (never under 40). No white text on the pale world (1.1:1). On footage, glass text sits on
  glass at ≥ 55 % fill.
- **No dead air, no visible jumps.** Pauses are cut on word boundaries ±1 f, each jump hidden under a caption blur swap or
  a world switch; never a visible jump in the face mid-sentence.
- **Audio.** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP, the bed 20 dB under the voice, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last
  word, no black tail.

**Never in this style:**
- Crash zooms, shakes, rotation snaps, whip pans or speed ramps on the face; in F-A, any punch-in at all. Dissolves or
  blur-throughs between worlds: the source always cuts.
- Comedy: meme sounds, stickers, stamps, marker roasts, freeze-frame gags.
- Coloured caption words. Emphasis is size, weight and family only. No caption boxes or pills on L-full.
- A second studio hue family in one reel, or a glow that fights the set (a teal glow on a violet set).
- Flat boxes without a rim or glow; hard offset shadows; sticker outlines; thick ink strokes.
- The script face on numbers, brand names or the bold punch word; script under 70 px.
- Look-alikes of YouTube, Instagram or app chrome presented as real (the link card is a generic glass card with the
  creator's own thumbnail and title).
- A raw full-bleed screen recording: recordings always sit in a glass frame (tutorial card, chest window, screen panel).
- Stock B-roll, AI stock scenes, emoji in captions, logos as decoration (generic app icons are `fx.icon` glyphs; a tool or
  brand the creator names gets its real logo, inside glass, §8.8).
- Effect packs: RGB split, lens-flare PNGs, film burns; the colour-burn cut belongs to F-B only (T-12). A new move is
  welcome when it's built in this style's language: glass, blur, glide, cut.
- Grading or tinting the footage: the set colour is the grade.
- A fade to black at the end.

### 3.5 Safe zones and bands
- **Meaning text** stays in x 64–1016, y 110–1500; nothing readable at x > 970 for y 900–1540 (Instagram's buttons), and
  nothing below y 1540.
- **The caption band:** CS-1 / CS-3 block top = face bottom + 220 px (typically y 980–1240), clamped inside y 110–1500;
  CS-2 sits 34 px under the card.
- **The top band** (y 180–720): the poster row, behind the head with the cut-out (y 200–686). Without the cut-out a seated
  head leaves no room above it for a row, so the row is skipped.
- **The chest band** (y 960–1300): chest windows, the checklist card, markers, duo icons, the video card.
- **The lower band** (y 1300–1500): sparkle bullets under a window, the diamond row (centre y 1400), the rating widget,
  the link card, glass slabs, the tile row.
- **The bottom** (y > 1500): only footage and decorative glow; never text.

### 3.6 The person
- **Framing the style assumes:** seated chest-up, head top y 180–360, chin y 700–860, face centre x 420–660. The head owns
  the top of the frame; the design lives at the chest.
- **The head region** is the face, the hair and the room above the head, from the cut-out when it exists. On L-full the
  caption starts 220 px under the face, so a 3-line block (≈ 245 px tall) still ends above y 1500; leader labels beside
  the head (y 820–880, x 700–950) stay clear of the hair and ear; top-band graphics go behind the head or stop 40 px above
  it.
- **The face goes into the graphics:** on W-pale it's the card (x 312–768, y 520–1331), in the orbit it's the bubble
  (Ø 300 at (540, 880)); stat cards, carousel cards and orbit tiles drift around them and pass behind rather than across.
- **People come for the creator,** so the face is on screen most of the reel and every absence ends with it back, in L-full
  or in a card. Returns from the tutorial and void worlds are a hard cut on a sentence start + the Z-0b landing (G-4);
  from a card, the G-2 grow.
- **The counting hand.** The creator counts on their fingers on ordinal words (v04 @0:06.1, @0:20.8; v01 @0:18.2) and the
  diamond emerges from that hand (P-DIAMOND-MARKER); when they don't count, it rises into place on its own.
- F-A never reframes the face beyond the Z-0…Z-3 moves; F-B jump cuts alternate wide and tight (T-14).

### 4.2 Meanings
- **The studio colour (`primary` + `accent`) is this reel's surface.** Every glass element carries it; it says "this is
  part of the lesson".
- **White** is what's said (captions) and what's shown (glass text).
- **Gold** is earned (a result achieved). Rating stars stay in the studio colour (violet fill and glow, outline only when
  empty), as in v04 @0:00–0:03. **Green** is money and the guarantee (F-B). **Rose** is the wrong state, rarely.
- The axis is **dim → lit**, not red → green: a wrong state is drawn dim or grey (an empty star, an inactive tile), the
  right state lit (the active tile glowing, the stars filling). Rose is a last resort.
- Brand colours appear only on the creator's own brand elements (their logo, their course art).

**How the two voices sing (the type-voice pass):**
1. **A chunk alternates voices:** script → small sans → script → **bold**. The measured targets at full resolution:
   "*This* editing *style* / is **taking over** / *In* **2026**" (v01 @0:02.3) and "*If* you *look* / at **your** *edits* /
   and you **think**" (v04 @0:01.5). Runs of 2–3 script words are lovely ("*And every single*", v01 @0:03.5); a whole
   3-line block in script is not. A chunk is never all one voice.
2. **Openers are script.** This, If, That, The, In, And open a sentence in script in every source reel. The engine puts
   function words in the sans, so promote the opener to tier 1 by hand when the chunk starts a sentence.
3. **Numbers, brands, tool names and the punch word are never script.** Content words go to script by default, so mark
   numbers, tool names and names `emph` (tier 2) or demote them to tier 0; check names are in the glossary.
4. **The punch word is the point of the line,** set bold and biggest ("taking over", "2026", "your", "think", "reason"):
   one per chunk at most (one per line in a long block), and rare enough that it always lands as a punch.
5. **Single-word chunks** are common in the body (v01 @0:20 "two", @0:44 "create"): use them for short emphatic
   sentences.

Fix the tiers with `captions.overrides` (`{i, emph: true|false}`, `{i, break: before|after}`), never with hand-built
caption cards.

## §6 Hook system

**The hook title** promises the viewer something: an outcome they want, a curiosity gap, or who it's for ("How to go viral
as a doctor creating content", never the label "Reels for Doctors"). It needn't repeat the spoken words; it must be true to
what the reel delivers. In F-A the title is the promise lockup, the creator's first sentence set in the duet; in F-B it's
the chrome product title under a spoken "What is…?". The shape is fixed (§5.2); the voice is yours to make irresistible.
Write 8–10 candidates, pick the best by the stopper test, keep the next two as alternates.

### 6.1 The stopper test
1. **Thumbnail:** frame 0 at 25 % scale shows a person teaching in a coloured room and the first caption word (the script
   word at 87 px still reads as a word at 22 px).
2. **Mute:** the first 3 s tell the promise without sound: the promise lockup + the proof element.
3. **Motion at f0:** the Z-0 landing from t 0 + the first word's blur-in.
4. **One read:** the promise's first chunk reads in ≤ 1.5 s; the F-B chrome title in ≤ 1.2 s.
5. **Payoff:** the first promise line reads by 0.7 s; a proof element lands by 2.5 s (the poster row, the link card, stat
   cards, the rating widget, the recursion window, a chrome title or the shrink-to-card collage).

The opening feels rich but unhurried: a word builds, then the next, the proof slides in under them, the hero word lands
big, and every new thing arrives while the last one settles.

### 6.2 HA-05 Promise + proof (default)

think…", "What is <product>?") while a proof element animates in within the first 0.5–2.5 s. Not result-first: the hook
promises, the proof makes the promise credible.

### 7.4 Open loops and re-hooks
- **The count loop:** the diamond row in the promise shows N; each item lights its number; the recap row shows all N.
- **The deliverable loop:** "the full tutorial is on YouTube" (the hook's link card) → paid by the video card at the end.
- **F-B re-hooks:** a question line set big ("HOW IS THIS DIFFERENT…?", "DON'T BELIEVE ME?", "AND LASTLY, THE SECRET") with
  P-SCREEN-WALL, P-TILTED-GLASS-SHOTS or a P-COLOUR-FLASH-WORD, wherever a two-minute pitch could let attention dip.
- The hook is over fast: the "here are N" line arrives while the promise is still fresh.

### 7.5 Rhythm by feel
- **Calm, steady, premium.** The energy is in the caption rhythm and the reveals, never in camera moves. Words land one
  by one on the speech; the punch word is the beat.
- **Face → show → face.** The worlds alternate: the face, then a tutorial card or a chest window, then the face. Two
  non-face spans back to back happen only in the F-B void runs.
- **It never sits still and is never busy.** Something new arrives when the words give it a reason: a word, a diamond, a
  bullet, a breath of the camera. Dense in time, never in space: one or two pieces of glass and the caption make a full
  frame; a third panel turns premium into a dashboard.
- **Holds are part of the design.** The tutorial card holds still while bullets land on their phrases; a marker holds long
  enough to read; the recap row sits under the closing sentence.
- **Variety comes from the moment,** never from a quota; the marker ritual is the one deliberate repeat.
- **Escalate to the end.** The last item gets the richest picture (an R-A tutorial card or the checklist card). Then the
  end goes quiet: the recap row, one sentence, the CTA card, a hard end.
- For reference, measured on the five reels (a description, not a target): F-A hard cuts are only world switches (v01 8 in
  50 s, median shot 6.1 s; v02 9 in 47 s; v03 1; v04 none in one 46 s take); within a take the changes are caption words
  (every 0.25–0.5 s), markers and camera breaths, a marker-push about every ten seconds; the tutorial world held its card
  still for up to 4.7 s while bullets landed every ~0.9 s (v01 @0:12.4–0:17.1); the face was on screen 45–80 % of an F-A
  reel. F-B (v05): 35 cuts in 122 s, median shot ≈ 3.4 s, two burn cuts.

### 8.5 Data and truth
- A number the creator says is shown exactly as said. F-B figures go through `plan/figures.json` (`formula: none`, the
  spoken word attached).
- What's presented as the creator's own (counts, views, results, testimonials, their posts) is real: 4 stat cards show 4
  real posts.
- Illustrations (a mock outcome, a recreated screen, a rating comparison) may use made-up but realistic numbers, with no
  label (`illustrative: true`).

### 8.6 Ink and annotation layer
Thin, precise lines that name the parts of what's shown, like a design spec (v01 @0:39–0:42, v03 @0:07–0:17, @0:24–0:28).

| Token | Value |
|---|---|
| Stroke | `paper` at 85 % (or `accent`), 2 px (3 px on footage), no wobble, square caps |
| Anchor dot | 5 px radius, `accent`, 8 px glow |
| Draw-on | 8 f (the dot 4 f first), no overshoot |
| Label | P-LEADER-LABEL type: Inter Tight 500, 30 px (`exception: "E3"`, redundant), ≤ 4 words on ≤ 2 lines |
| Max on screen | 3 marks |

| Mark | Recipe | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `leader_line` | A dot on the target → a straight line at 30–70° → the label; line length 80–220 px | Naming a part ("Edges of the screen", "Sans font", "hip below knee") |
| `bracket` | A thin 2 px bracket along one edge of a window (the height of the part) with the label at its centre | Naming a span (a margin, a range) |

| `underline` | A 3 px `accent` line under a title, drawn L → R in 10 f | The P-CHECKLIST-CARD header |

- Targets are points inside the style's own windows and cards, written as coordinates in the scene; no footage tracking.
- Marks stay off the head; labels stay inside x 64–970, y 110–1500.
- Every mark exits with its window: no orphan lines.
- Labels repeat words that are spoken or shown larger (`data-redundant`); otherwise they're 40 px.

### 8.7 Comedy: off
No stickers, stamps, meme sounds or roast marks. The style's confidence is the joke it doesn't need.

### 8.8 Assets
- The creator's own exports, recordings, stills and screenshots first (SH-1…SH-6).
- Built mocks are generic and unbranded (`fx.appUI`, `fx.device`, glass cards with `fx.icon`).
- No stock footage, no AI stock scenes, no logos as decoration; generic app icons are `fx.icon` glyphs in the studio
  colour. A tool or brand the creator names gets its real logo (the creator's file, else fetched from the brand's site),

  found.
- Third-party material: fetch the real thing, source noted (§12.4); private data in screenshots is blurred.

### 9.3 How the moves breathe
- **The cut into a world is earned.** The face leans in (Z-3), the frame cuts, the card grows: three beats that feel like
  one breath. Spend it on the steps that have something to show; a step that's only an idea stays on the face with a
  marker.
- **The cut back is soft.** Every return lands slightly close and settles, so the face arrives instead of snapping in.
- **The card morphs are a pair of brackets.** Shrink-to-card opens a "look at this" moment, grow-from-card closes it; use
  them when the face really steps aside for proof, not as a habit.
- **The orbit and the flash word are punchlines of the eye.** They're vivid because they're rare; save each for the line
  that deserves it.
- **The burn cut is F-B's chapter break:** it belongs on the section turns of the pitch (the source used two in two
  minutes), never inside a sentence, never in F-A.
- **Captions swap on their own soft blur** under everything; markers repeat on every item because the repeat is the
  structure.

### 10.2 Footage camera and zoom (zoom policy `slow_push`)
The camera breathes: it lands, leans in to meet a marker, leans back to listen.

| ID | Preset | Recipe | Tone / use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Z-0** | `land-settle` | Lands at 1.22 and settles to 1.00 over 45 f (`land: 1.22`, `ease: "expoOut"`; half the travel by f12); on f0 add `blur: {"kind": "defocus", "px": 10, "frames": 3, "shape": "decay"}` | Frame 0. - **The shape of a face span:** Z-0 (or Z-0b) lands → Z-1 leans in on the marker or claim → Z-2 leans back as the
  explanation starts → Z-3 leans in before the next cut. Every move runs ≥ 15 f and finishes before the next begins.
- **Only on the face.** Other layouts get no camera: the tutorial card animates itself.
- **It breathes; it never ticks.** Vary the moves so the camera feels alive, and let long explanations sit still between
  breaths.
- **No rotation, no shake:** the measured rotation is 0 (≤ 0.1° per 0.5 s apart from body motion).
- **Sharpness.** A 1080p source holds ≤ 1.12× sharp: Z-1…Z-3 stay ≤ 1.12×; the Z-0 / Z-0b landings start above that and
  read slightly soft for their first ≈ 10 f while moving, as in the source. A soft source may lower `land` to 1.12.
- **No punches in F-A;** the only fast camera change is the F-B jump-cut reframe (T-14). The built-in `whip` is never used:
  the source never whips.

### 10.3 Layer order (back to front)
1. The world background (W-studio footage, W-tutorial, W-pale, W-void, W-grid, W-flat)
2. Glows (the world glows, P-FALLING-ICONS at z2)
3. Cards and collages (P-POSTER-ROW, P-STATCARDS, the P-CARD-CAROUSEL side cards); P-POSTER-ROW is `behind` the cut-out
4. The presenter (the footage, the card, the bubble)
5. Labels, outline words, glass slabs, the link card (z5–z6)
6. Markers and rows (z6)
7. Auto-captions (z7)
8. Big type and chest windows (z8: P-PROMISE-DUET, P-DIAMOND-MARKER, P-PREVIEW-WINDOW, P-CHECKLIST-CARD, P-CHROME-TITLE,
   P-VIDEO-CARD, P-KEYWORD-GLASS); captions hide under them
9. Light passes (z11): the chrome flare, the P-WINDOW-WIPE light line

### 10.4 Finishing
- No grain and no vignette added; the set's own look is kept.
- Glow only on glass rims, markers, the chrome title and the money figure; captions get a soft dark shadow only.
- Card radius 14 (windows), 18 (the link card), 22–26 (tutorial and face cards); rims 2 px; no hard offset shadows.

## §11 Sound

| Line | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Cue moments** | The hook (f0 and the proof landing), reveals (cards, windows, the chrome title, the tutorial card), the list cue (the diamond or tile entry on every item), the transitions that move the stage (T-02, T-03, T-04, T-09, T-10, T-12, T-13), the CTA (the keyword chip or video card landing) |
| **The list cue** | One file on every diamond or tile, so the count becomes a sound; the other cues vary |
| **Meme sounds** | Off |
| **Music bed** | On; enters after the hook (on the "here are N" line or the first marker) |
| **Ducking** | The bed sits 20 dB under the voice while it speaks |
| **Before the CTA** | Silence: no cue in the 1.0 s before the first CTA word |
| **Loudness** | −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP; a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word |

The palette is calm and premium (`tone.energy: calm`): soft whooshes, UI ticks, glassy shines; nothing louder than the
pack's role defaults. Sound only marks a landing; if in doubt, leave it out.

### 12.3 Props, cut-out, resolution
- Props: none. Reaction bank: none (no comedy).
- The cut-out: needed when the hook uses the poster row behind the head (the row sits behind the hair, v01 @0:01); nothing
  else needs it, so it runs only then.
- Resolution: 1080×1920 is enough (the camera ≤ 1.12× when held; the landings start at 1.15–1.22 and settle); a 720p
  source is upscaled once and gets no push.

### 12.4 Third-party inserts: fetch the real thing
When the creator names another creator, a course, a tool, a post or a brand, the viewer should see the real one.
1. **Find the moments** in the transcript that call for third-party material: another creator's profile or reel
   (P-PROFILE-TILE, P-STYLE-CARD), competitor courses (P-COURSE-STACK), a tool's UI (P-SCREEN-PANEL), a third-party post
   (P-TILTED-GLASS-SHOTS), a brand or tool logo.
2. **The creator's own files come first;** otherwise search the web and fetch it: the real profile, the real course page,
   the tool's real screen, the real post, the real logo (captured and framed on the part that matters). Note where it came
   from.
3. **Use it as it is,** framed in glass, cropped, private data blurred, never altered to say something it doesn't; a post
   is shown word for word.
4. **Nothing usable to be found → rebuild it from its exact words:**
   | Pattern | Rebuilt as |
   |---|---|
   | P-PROFILE-TILE | The generic profile tile (initials, the name as spoken, two decorative lines) |
   | P-STYLE-CARD (someone else's style) | A type-set card in the studio colour with the style's name; no imitation of their footage |
   | P-COURSE-STACK (other courses) | Type-set glass cards with generic names ("Course A / B / C") or the names as spoken |
   | P-SCREEN-PANEL (a tool UI) | `fx.appUI` (a list, settings or browser) recreated generically |
   | P-TILTED-GLASS-SHOTS (posts) | `fx.quoteCard` with the post's exact words |
   | Brand or tool logos anywhere | `fx.logoPlate` (the name set in type) |

### 12.5 Frame rate and audio
30 fps CFR output, 1080×1920, BT.709. The voice chain: high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, light compression, −14 LUFS.

## §13 What your plan should settle

Your `ideas.md` is a page or two, for you. In this style it has decided, before any code:
1. **The format and the theme:** F-A or F-B, the studio pack and the backdrop colour it was picked from (`theme`).
2. **The hook:** the promise lockup with its two alternates, the archetype (HA-05, HA-02, HA-07 or HA-18), the proof
   element and when it lands, the hero word; for F-B, the chrome title.
3. **The count:** N items as spoken, the diamond row on the count word, the recap row.
4. **The ritual per item:** R-A, R-B or R-C (`ritual` on the beat that opens each item), and exactly what each window or
   card holds: which recording or still, which bullets, which leader labels (`ink` marks with their targets).
5. **The type-voice pass:** every chunk whose tiers were forced (`captions.overrides`), and why.
6. **The moves:** every world switch on its word, the push before each cut in, the landing after each cut back.
7. **The scene fields this style uses:** `exception: "E3"` on leader labels and CS-2 spans, `exception: "E4"` on falling
   icons, `figure_id` on F-B figures, `illustrative: true` on built illustrations; which inserts are the creator's, which
   were fetched (with the source) and which rebuilt, and the fallbacks used.
8. **The sound:** the few cues, the list cue, the bed's entry, the silence before the CTA.
9. **The moments you'll look at hardest on the storyboard:** f0, the proof landing (≈ 1.0 s), one marker with its
   diamond, one tutorial card or chest window, the recap row, the CTA card.

## §15 Your look at the storyboard: the checklist

Watch it once as a stranger with a thumb over the next reel, then once as the editor whose name is on it. Fix what
bothers you, in one pass.

**The style (does it feel like §The feel?)**
- Frame 0: the face is landing and settling in the room's colour, the first script word blurring in; the proof is in by
  2.5 s.
- The captions look typeset: script openers and content words, small sans function words, one bold punch word, lines
  nearly touching; never all one voice.
- Every glow is the room's colour; one pack for the whole reel; gold, green and rose only for their meanings.
- Every graphic is glass with a rim and a glow; never a flat box.
- Motion is eased everywhere: blur in, glide, blur out; worlds change by cut, never by dissolve; the camera lands on every
  face entry, leans in on markers, leans back, leans in before a cut.
- Face → show → face; the last item is the richest; the end is quiet.
- Start to end, it looks like the creator's own premium course, not a template.

**Craft (by eye, in context)**
- The creator's face reads whenever the moment is about them: the caption block under the chin, the poster row behind
  the hair, leader labels off the head, nothing across the face card or the bubble; nothing chops the head or buries the
  face by accident.
- No text over text by accident: captions out of the way under the z8 elements; a chest window and the caption don't
  fight for the chest.
- Every marker, window, card, label and bullet lands on its word; every diamond on its ordinal word; nothing lingers
  after its point.
- Numbers as said; the creator's counts, results and testimonials real; fetched posts word for word; illustrations
  unlabelled.
- Promises paid off: the diamond count = the items spoken; the recap shows all; the link or video card pays off "full
  tutorial"; the keyword readable (F-B to the end).
- Names, brands and tools spelt exactly, never in script; script and labels big enough to read on a phone; no white text
  on the pale world.
- No visible jump in the face mid-sentence. The file itself (1080×1920, 30 fps, −14 LUFS, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the
  last word) is the render's job; it checks it.

## §16 Build notes

- **Fonts to pre-load:** Jost 500/700, Pinyon Script 400, Inter Tight 400–900, Montserrat 400–900 with its true italic.
- **Glass cost:** at most one element with `backdrop-filter` per frame (blur 14 px; the slab or the keyword chip); glass
  frames stay ≤ 30 ms per frame (check with `veos measure`); one 3D chrome scene on screen at a time (≈ 50–85 ms per
  frame), with the flat fallback when another heavy scene is up.
- **Determinism:** every frame is a pure function of its index; drifts, floats and falling icons use `ctx.rng(seed)` /
  `rngStable`.
- **Events:** declare `events` for every word built inside a z8 type scene, and for every bullet, label, star and tile, so
  each lands on its word.
- **The take as an asset:** the carousel and FB-2 show the creator at other moments by registering the take
  (`veos asset add <take> --name take`) and reading it with `ctx.videoFrame("take", t)`.
