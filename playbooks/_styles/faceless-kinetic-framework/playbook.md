# Faceless Kinetic Framework Style Playbook (template v2)

## The feel

This reel has no face, and it never feels empty. The voice is the presenter and the type is its body. Every phrase the
creator says flies up from the bottom of the frame and locks into place under the last one: a heavy bold line, then a thin
italic one, then bold again, until a stack of short lines reads like a sentence a magazine designer set live, to the
voice. You read it at the speed you hear it. That's the hold: listening and reading become one act.

The idea always has a shape. Black bars around a dark hexagon, every label redacted, and the viewer wants to know what
they hide. Then the camera snaps in, so fast it smears, and the first bar types out its secret letter by letter. A hard
cut to the proof, a hard cut back, and the hub spins the next bar to the top. Or a giant grey letter fills a black screen
with the rest of its word tucked into its shoulder. Or a proof card sits above the stack like evidence on a desk. Every
part of the idea is a place, and the camera goes to visit it.

Nothing fades. Sections slam from warm graph paper to dark umber to charcoal ink and back on hard cuts, the new words
already there on the cut frame. Cards arrive with a smear and keep drifting. It's calm, never loud: no jokes, no shakes,
no stickers. Colour is a whisper, one burnt orange on the one thing we're on. The energy comes from precision: the snap,
the type landing exactly on the word, the cut exactly on the breath.

The rhythm is a ritual the viewer learns by the second visit: snap, type, proof, back, spin. Every part gets the same
respect, so the viewer relaxes into it and keeps watching for the next bar. It doesn't end bigger; it ends complete,
pulling back to the whole shape, then cutting to a dark card that holds dead still with the word to comment. It feels like
being handed a file.

**The test:** pause on any frame and you can read exactly what was just said, and see which part of the shape we're in.

## What this playbook is

You're editing a voice-over reel with no camera and no presenter: every frame is built from the creator's voice, their
script and whatever proof they hand over. You have the authority to make it the clearest, most beautifully set explainer
in their niche. This playbook is the style, pulled from three peter.visuals reels (v01 a proof stack, v02 a hub tour, v03
an initial ladder), measured frame by frame at full resolution and full frame rate. Read it all, every time; take what
fits this reel, invent where a moment needs more, and never break the feel above.

**Who it's for and what it needs.** Creators who teach one idea at a time: N ways, a named formula or acronym, "this is the
X style", do this not that. Input: one voice-over file and its script (one format, F-A Kinetic framework, 18–30 s). Nothing
is filmed; no matte. The creator's own reels, screenshots and lead-magnet screenshot make the proof cards real; a real
post, profile or product the voice names is fetched from the web; anything else is built (§12). Captions follow the creator's language (English, Hinglish or Hindi, set in their copy) and
are the main picture, always on. Machine values live in `tokens.json`; where this text gives a number tokens also holds,
they agree.

## §1 Procedure: how to approach a reel in this style

You listen, plan, build and look at the storyboard yourself; the edit skill has the mechanics. This style lives or
dies on one craft step, turning a spoken idea into a shape with named parts, and on the moves that carry the viewer through
that shape. Decide these, in this order.

1. **Listen to the voice.** Where it speeds up on a list, where it breathes before a part, which word it leans on. That
   breathing is your rhythm: lines fly in on phrases, labels type on part names, cuts land on breaths.
2. **Extract the framework (the craft step).** Before any scene, write:
   - `framework`: its name in ≤ 4 words ("6 ways to hook viewers", "the HRST formula").
   - `shape`: an acronym in the script ⇒ **SM-2** initial ladder (3–6 letters, or a word whose letters are the parts);
     3–8 parallel items ⇒ **SM-1** hub tour; one thing shown with proof ("this is the X") ⇒ **SM-3** proof stack.
   - `parts`: each part's label, ≤ 18 characters and ≤ 4 words, as the voice says it.
   - `proof`: per part, what proves it: a creator clip, a card you build, or nothing (then the label plus a header line or
     a centre stack carries it).
3. **Feel the tone of each line:** `explain` · `awe` · `warn` · `win` · `cta`. There's no `mock`: this style never jokes.
   Explain is ink on paper; awe goes dark and lets size do the work; warn strikes the wrong way in red; win ticks the right
   way in green; the CTA is ink on paper and still.
4. **Write the hook** (§6): the archetype follows the shape (HA-06 for the hub, HA-12 for the ladder, HA-02 for the proof
   stack). Write 8–10 title candidates, pick by the stopper test, keep the next two as alternates.
5. **Plan the worlds** (§4.3): which sections stay on paper, which go to umber or ink, and the exact frame of each flip.
6. **Plan the camera path** (§10.3): the canvas nodes, the one snap push, every hub spin, the drift on a held hub, the
   pull-out if the voice recaps. These moves are the edit; write each one in frames.
7. **Plan the type per span** (§5.3): a low stack under a card (CS-1), a centre stack on its own (CS-2), a header line over
   a card, clip or zoomed hub (CS-3), or built text with the captions hidden under it (title, labels, ladder, CTA).
8. **Gather the proof** (§12.3): the creator's own clips and screenshots in their folder first; a real post, profile or
   product the voice names, fetched from the web (source noted); the lead magnet as the voice names it (a size only when
   they state one, their screenshot when there is one). Build what's still missing.
9. **Plan the sound** (§11): a calm bed and a few cues; the list cue on every visit.

## §2 Hard rules: the craft and the style's never-list

**Craft, by eye** (judge it on the storyboard, in context, the way an editor does):
- **The voice and the picture stay in sync.** Every spoken phrase is on screen within ±1 f of its onset (captions lead
  1 f). Every node label, card, letter and count starts 2 f before its trigger word and is fully landed within ±5 f; the
  camera push starts 6 f before the item word so the node is framed on the word. Cuts land on word boundaries.
- **Every word is read, once.** Each phrase is an auto-caption (CS-1/2/3) or sits inside a built text scene carrying the
  same words (title, node label, ladder, CTA line), never both at once (hide the captions under built text).
- **Nothing important hides under Instagram's buttons.** Meaning text stays inside x 64–1016, y 110–1500; nothing meaningful
  in the top 110 px, below y 1540, or in the right 110 px between y 900 and 1540 (the hub's right-hand node slides left
  for this, §10.4).
- **If a face ever appears** (a person inside a proof clip), keep it clear of header lines, chips and stacks when the
  person is the point of the proof; what's never fine is a face buried by accident. Behind it is fair game.
- **No text over text.** One kinetic stack at a time, never under a card; the header line never over a card's own text; a
  view chip only on its card.
- **Say what was said.** A number or quote the creator says is shown exactly as said; quote cards are word for word.
  Illustrations may use made-up but realistic numbers and names (a "1.2M" chip on a built example reel), with no label.
  Facts about the creator, a named real account or the lead magnet (their views, followers, the resource's size) are shown
  only as the creator stated them.
- **Promise integrity.** The title's count equals the nodes, letters or items shown; every node of the hub is typed and
  visited; every letter of the acronym appears; the CTA keyword on the card is the keyword in the voice; the resource on
  the lead card is the one the creator names.
- **Spelling.** English words, tool, brand and person names exact in captions and on cards.
- **Readable.** Stack lines sans 80 / serif 112, the header line 76, labels ≥ 40 px (node labels 40 px in world space, so
  ≈ 92 px at the 2.3× push and never below 40 at home). Titles hold ≥ 10 f after their last word lands; text holds ≥ 0.25 s
  per word. Contrast: ink on the light worlds, white on umber and ink; never ink text on a dark world, white text on paper,
  `accent` on paper (1.55:1) or the signal `#AE3F18` as text on umber (2.19:1). Text the canvas camera moves is never
  pushed into the header band while captions show.
- **Audio.** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP, the bed ≥ 18 dB under the voice, creator clips muted, a hard end
  ≤ 6 f after the last word, no black tail.

**Never in this style:**
- A face, a hand, a webcam frame, an AI avatar or a stock person as the presenter. A creator's clip may contain people (it's
  proof), never as a stand-in for the presenter.
- Stock media standing in for proof: stock footage, AI images of real people, a generic clip passed off as the thing
  named. Proof is the creator's file, the real thing fetched, or a card you build.
- Fades: a fade-in from black, a dissolve, a crossfade, a black tail. The cut is this style's transition.
- Loud colour: gradients, neon glows, bloom, colour on a whole stack line (the signal touches one word or one element),
  more than the signal plus one of red/green in a frame.
- Emoji, stickers, meme sounds, stamps, shakes, crash zooms, freeze-frames: comedy is off.
- Effect packs: whip pans, light leaks, glitches, film burns, spins of the whole frame, page curls. A new move is welcome
  when it's built in this style's language: a smear, a snap, a morph, a hard cut with the new thing already there.
- Ken Burns on built cards (a creator clip may push 1.00 → 1.04 inside its card, nothing more).
- Two stacks at once, a stack under a card, or a stack line longer than 3 words or 16 characters.
- A dead diagram: when a diagram holds, the camera breathes on it (C-3 drift).
- A bare world: a ground with nothing built on it, beyond the 0–2 empty frames a clearing cut (T-03) leaves on purpose.
- A built card styled as a real platform: no platform logos, no verified ticks, no pixel-for-pixel copy of a real app.
- A lead-magnet promise the creator hasn't made (resource name, size, delivery): only what the voice, script or brief
  states.
- Decoration. Every card, node and letter shows the thing being said.

## §3 Worlds, layouts, stage moves, safe zones

No presenter: every frame is built. The stage is the canvas and its worlds; the camera that travels it lives in §10.3.

### 3.1 Worlds
| ID | Kind | Look | Carries | Enter / exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### 3.3 Stage moves (G-1…G-9): how elements change places
| ID | Move | Recipe (30 fps) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G-1** | **Stack fly-in** | A new line appears below the last one, near the frame bottom (≈ 450 px under its slot, 420–500), and decelerates up into the slot: exponential out, ½ by f5, 90 % by f12, settled by f20; vertical motion blur 16 px → 0 over the first 3 f only; older lines stay exactly where they are (`anchor: "top"`, no glide). Measured v01 @ 0:01.29–0:01.92 ("style": 507 → 0 px from its slot, × 0.8 per frame) | Every new phrase in a stack |
| **G-2** | **Stack clear** | The whole stack vanishes on a hard cut (the next section's first frame); only inside one section, when a 6th line is due, do the lines blur 0 → 8 px and lift 44 px over 6 f | A section change (cut), or a full stack |
| **G-3** | **Card slide-in** | 4 f after a flip, a card enters from the right frame edge: a horizontal smear (`ctx.blur(12 → 0, 0)`) on its first 4 f, 80 % of the travel in 6 f, then the last ≈ 140 px continue as a slow drift (≈ 50 px/s) through the whole hold | A reference card arriving after a world flip (v01 @ 0:02.63–0:05.9) |
| **G-4** | **Proof flurry** | Hard cut to the next card set (profile bar + card) on its word; every card sits at a random tilt −5…+5° (`ctx.rngStable`, never 0 twice running); 0.25–0.5 s per set (measured: six sets in 2.0 s) | A run of examples ("…just like these creators", v01 @ 0:06.21–0:08.13) |
| **G-5** | **Push-and-type** | Canvas camera C-1 snap to the node (14 f, §10.3); 5 f after it lands the node's redaction bar is consumed from the left by its label typed at 45 cps, the caret blinking every 15 f after | The first hub node visit (SM-1) |
| **G-6** | **Letter cut** | The giant initial and its tucked word are on screen whole on the cut frame (rest x 64); the first letter only de-blurs 8 → 0 px over 3 f; later letters have no entry motion at all | Every SM-2 letter (v03 @ 0:05.20, 0:06.48) |
| **G-7** | **Morph hand-off** | `fx.morphShape`: the outgoing object becomes the incoming one (card rect → hub hexagon, letter → recap word) over 0.5 s | A section change inside one world |
| **G-8** | **Card rise-in** | 0–1 f after a cut to paper, a card rises from ≈ 450 px below its rest with scale 1.12 → 1.0, vertical motion blur on the first 3 f (`ctx.blur(px, 90)`), settled in 12 f (expo-out) | The first proof after an ink punch or a header change on paper (v01 @ 0:08.22, 0:13.89) |
| **G-9** | **Hub spin** | Camera held at the push framing; the hub (hex, bars, leaders, labels) rotates −360/N about its centre in 10 f, ease-out, so node k+1 comes to the top; the cut back from a proof lands on the spin's 3rd–4th frame; the next label starts typing on the spin's last 3 f | SM-1 node → node (v02 @ 0:06.37, 0:11.00) |

### 4.2 Meanings
- **Ink on paper** = the explanation. **White on dark** = a new section: context or a reveal.
- **The signal** (`primary` on light, `accent` on dark) = "this one": the node being visited, the word being typed, the
  keyword to comment. One element per frame.
- **Red → green** only for a wrong-way / right-way pair (P-16); never decorative.
- **Ghost grey** = the structure behind the explanation (giant letters, tucked words): big, quiet, never the words you read
  first.

and rare enough that each one feels like a new chapter (the reference flipped 3.3 s apart at the closest, v01 2.5 → 5.8 s).
Write every flip twice, at the same frame: `timeline.theme_flips: [{t, theme}]` and `timeline.world: [{t, world}]` (the
renderer and the caption colour read the second).

### 4.4 Rules
- No footage grade: a creator's clip is shown as delivered (no LUT, no B&W).
- Coloured text on paper only in `primary`, `bad`, `good` or `mute`, at ≥ 40 px. Never `accent` on paper.
- On umber and ink, text is paper; the only coloured text is `accent`.
- No gradients, glows, bloom or vignette on any world. Most frames carry no bright hue at all; a few carry the signal.

## §6 Hook system

**The hook title** promises the viewer something: an outcome they want, a curiosity gap, or who it's for ("How to go viral
as a doctor creating content", never the label "Reels for Doctors"). It needn't repeat the spoken words; it must be true to
what the reel delivers. In this style the title is the stack or the title-mix: its shape is fixed (§5.2), its voice is
yours to make irresistible. Write 8–10 candidates from the formulas in §6.5 and the proven patterns ("How to X as a Y",
"Why your X isn't working", "The X nobody tells you", "Stop doing X", a number or a contrast), score them on outcome,
curiosity, who it's for and brevity, check the best against the stopper test, and keep the next two as alternates.

### 6.1 The stopper test (frame 0 and the first 3 s)
1. **Thumbnail:** frame 0 at 25 % scale shows the light world and the shape already starting; at 1.0 s (the cover frame)
   the title reads at 25 % (72 px → 18 px).
2. **Mute:** with the sound off, the first 3 s tell the promise: the title or the stack carries every word.
3. **Motion at f0:** something is already moving on frame 0: the hub dot growing, the thumb field turning, the first word
   rising, the hero card settling.
4. **Payoff:** the framework's shape (the hub skeleton, the condensed claim, or the proof card with its full stack) reads by
   about 2.5 s (3.0 s for HA-12).

The opening feels dense and certain: the hub grows its bars while the title builds word by word, every element landing
while the last one settles, then one decisive move (the snap, the flip) carries the viewer into the first part. For
reference, the three reels measured about 7–10 changes in their first 3 s (a description, not a target).

## §7 Structure and rhythm

### 7.1 Structure: a framework
`HOOK` (title + shape) → `CONTEXT` (optional; umber when it carries a reference card, ink in SM-2) → `ITEM-1…N` (the marker
ritual) → `RECAP` (optional: the hub pulled out, the acronym, or the equation) → `CTA` (the still lead card, 3.0–4.0 s).

A typical 24 s reel, as a feel for the proportions: HOOK 0–2.5 · CONTEXT 2.5–6.0 · ITEMS 6.0–18.5 · RECAP 18.5–20.5 ·
CTA 20.5–24.0.

### 7.2 Markers: one shape per reel
| ID | Marker shape | Numbering | Recap | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SM-1** | **Hub tour**: a hexagon hub + N redacted bars; each item is a camera visit to its node, and the label types | clockwise from the top | the pull-out shows every label (P-14) | v02 @ 0:00–0:33 |
| **SM-2** | **Initial ladder**: one giant initial per item on W-ink, the tucked word, the explanation ladder | the acronym's letter order | "That's [ACRONYM]" on paper (P-23) | v03 @ 0:04–0:13 |
| **SM-3** | **Proof stack**: a hero card + the stack below it; the items are proofs, not numbered | none (spoken only) | the equation (P-05) | v01 @ 0:00–0:21 |

No numbered badges, no "Step 1" chips: the shape is the marker. One shape per reel: a second kind of diagram never appears.

### 7.3 The unit rituals (identical for every item of a reel)
The ritual is the one deliberate repeat, and it's what the viewer learns to trust: by the second visit they know the dance
and lean in for what the next bar hides.

**SM-1 node visit** (a few seconds per item, as long as the part and its proof take to say):
1. f −8 (before the item word): item 1 is the **C-1 snap push** home → node (14 f, 2.3×); every later item is the **G-9 hub
   spin** (−360/N, 10 f, ease-out, camera held), entered by the T-02 cut back from the previous proof on the spin's 3rd–4th
   frame.
2. f 0 (the item word): node k's bar is typed over at 45 cps (≤ 18 characters ≈ ≤ 12 f), the caret blinking after.
   Visited labels stay full ink (no dimming, v02 @ 0:06.50); unvisited nodes stay black bars, still keeping their secret.
3. The label done + 10–25 f, either
   - **with proof:** a T-02 hard cut to the proof, held while the voice proves it (v02's full-bleed examples ran 3.0–4.6 s
     each): P-33 full-bleed clip or P-32 clip card, P-26 large card or P-27 quote card, with the CS-3 header line; then a
     T-02 hard cut back into the next spin; or
   - **without proof:** the CS-3 header line runs over the zoomed hub (the header band y 270–400 is clear, because the node
     is framed at y ≥ 560) with a C-3 drift of 30 px.
4. After the last item the reel goes straight to the CTA (v02 ends on its last proof), or, when the voice recaps, a **C-2
   pull** to home (18 f) under the recap line (P-14).

**SM-2 letter** (a second or two per letter: as long as its ladder takes to say):
1. f −2 (before the part's word): a T-09 hard cut: the previous letter, tucked word and ladder vanish together (declared
   `cuts`).
2. f 0: **G-6** the giant initial and its tucked word are on screen whole (the first letter only de-blurs 8 → 0 over 3 f).
3. f 6 on: the ladder words pop on their onsets: helpers hard, the key word at 110 % settling to 100 % over 4 f (v03 @
   0:06.08).
4. Hold ≥ 10 f after the last ladder word; the next letter cuts in on its word.
5. After the last letter: a T-01 flip → W-paper and the P-23 acronym recap (the letters slide in like P-24), then a T-11
   blur-push cut into the CTA.

**SM-3 proof** (one section per idea):
1. A section opens on a hard cut (a world flip, or a header change on paper) with its card entering by G-3 (from the right)
   or G-8 (from below); the card then holds while CS-3 header words swap above it.
2. A run of examples is a **G-4 proof flurry** (hard cuts every 0.25–0.5 s, tilted cards).
3. One emphatic line gets the **P-08 ink punch**; the recap is the equation (P-05) on W-paper, entered after a hard cut
   that empties the frame (T-03).

### 7.4 Open loops
- **The redacted bars are the loop.** Every black bar is a promise the viewer can see; the title's N is paid by N visited
  nodes, N letters or N proofs.
- **The deliverable loop:** a resource named in the hook (optional) is the one on the lead card.
- **The hook is over fast,** so the first part arrives while the promise is fresh: the hook ends when its payoff has
  landed and held 10 f; any longer hold belongs to the context. In a longer reel (past 30 s, a 6–8-node hub) give the
  middle a teaser: the hub pulls out to show "[k] of [N]" with the visited nodes dimmed (scene `kind: "teaser"`).

### 7.5 Rhythm by feel
- **Calm, with one peak.** Information only, no comedy beats. The energy sits level and confident, and the reel spends its
  one big moment early: the first snap push (SM-1), the first giant letter (SM-2) or the umber flip (SM-3).
- **Even items.** Every part gets about the same time and the same ritual; the last item never escalates in size.
  Completion is shown by **pulling back** to the whole framework, not by a bigger effect. This is the one style where the
  ending is quieter than the middle, and that's what makes it feel finished.
- **It never sits still, but it is never random.** Something arrives when the words give it a reason: a line flies in on a
  phrase, a label types on a part name, a header swaps on every word or two. A held diagram breathes (a slow drift); only
  the CTA is allowed to stop dead.
- **It follows the voice.** A fast list gets the stack filling fast; the breath before a part is where the snap or the cut
  lands; a pause holds the current stack instead of clearing it.
- **Hard cuts carry the edit.** World flips, proof changes, letter changes and the CTA are hard cuts with the new content
  already on the cut frame; a card that arrives after a cut does so 4 f later (G-3, G-8).
- **The end feels filed:** the lead card holds still while the CTA line reads.
- For reference, measured on the three reels (a description, not a target): motion onsets 9.0–11.8 per 10 s; the longest
  still stretch in the body 0.67–1.13 s; only the CTA holds still, up to about 2.0 s (v01 2.05 s); hard-cut shots ran a
  median 2.5 s (v01), 1.3 s (v02, the clip-heavy hub) and 3.6 s (v03, plus the 1.0–1.3 s letter cuts).

### 7.6 The hub motif (continuity)
- **Motif `M-hub`:** the hexagon hub (r 160, `hub` fill). Its states: `dot` (f0, r 12) → `skeleton` (bars redacted) →
  `labelled` (bars typed) → `visited` (the active node's end dot in `primary`, the rest at 40 %) → `recap` (every label,
  none active). In SM-2 and SM-3 reels the motif is absent: one shape per reel.
- **The same map, every time.** After every proof the hub returns at exactly the framing it left (the camera never moves
  while a proof shows), so the viewer re-enters a place they already know.
- **Morph hand-offs** (P-19, `fx.morphShape`, 0.5 s): a CONTEXT card becoming the hub hexagon, or a proof card becoming the
  next station's first node. It's a special move, worth one moment in a reel; every other section boundary is a hard cut
  (T-01, T-02). No bookend.

## §8 Visual system: B-roll and patterns

The type carries the words; the shape and the proof carry the meaning. **Numbers become pictures** the style's way: a
spoken count becomes N nodes or N letters; a view or follower count becomes a view chip on its card; a spoken stat ("6
months", "4 clients") becomes a P-36 number card. Never a bare number floating on paper. Variety comes from the moment,
never from a quota; the marker ritual is the one deliberate repeat.

         + `border-radius:18px;background:${ctx.col("grid")};opacity:${op};${near ? "filter:blur(6px);" : ""}`
         + `box-shadow:0 10px 30px rgba(0,0,0,.12)"></div>`; }
    const q = 1 - Math.pow(1 - ctx.clamp(ctx.t / 3.7), 2), rot = -3 * (1 - q);
    return ctx.html(`<div style="position:absolute;inset:0;transform-origin:540px 960px;transform:rotate(${rot.toFixed(2)}deg)">${h}</div>`); } });

// P-20/21/22 giant initial + tucked word + ladder (one scene per letter, W-ink)
function initialScene(id, t_in, t_out, L, rest, ladder /* [{text, at, key}] */, first /* true only for the first letter */) {

    text_content: [L + rest, ...ladder.map(w => w.text)].join(" "), box: { x: 64, y: 500, w: 940, h: 945 },
    events: ladder.map(w => w.at - t_in),

      const tCol = tSize >= 110 ? g : ctx.col("paper"), tp = 1;

        const sz = w.key ? Math.min(104, Math.floor(104 * 484 / ctx.measure(w.text, `700 104px ${ctx.fam("display")}`))) : 44;
        lad += `<div style="font:${w.key ? 700 : 500} ${sz}px/1.05 ${ctx.fam("display")};color:${ctx.col("paper")};transform:scale(${k.toFixed(3)})">${ctx.esc(w.text)}</div>`; }
      return ctx.html(`<div style="position:absolute;left:64px;top:${(500 - 0.064 * 1100).toFixed(0)}px;opacity:${op.toFixed(2)};font:400 1100px/1 ${ctx.fam("numeric")};color:${g};transform:scaleX(${sx.toFixed(3)});transform-origin:left top;${bl > 0.4 ? `filter:blur(${bl.toFixed(1)}px);` : ""}">${ctx.esc(L)}</div>`

        + `<div style="position:absolute;left:520px;width:484px;top:1000px;transform:translateY(-50%);text-align:center">${lad}</div>`);
    } });
}

### 8.4 Data and truth
- Every number the creator claims is spoken, in the script, or supplied by them (view counts, follower counts, the lead
  magnet's size). When a reel shows a number that isn't spoken (a view chip, a follower count, the resource's size), record
  it in `plan/figures.json` with `from: "creator"`.
- Illustrations may use made-up but realistic numbers and names with no label (`illustrative: true` on the figure or
  scene): a "1.2M" chip on a built example reel, "212 views" on a built post.
- Quantities are countable in the shape (N bars, N letters); comparisons use the same card size side by side (P-36 pairs,
  P-16).

### 8.5 Assets
- **Real captures first:** the creator's own clips, screenshots and resource screenshots, as delivered, framed by P-25 /
  P-26 / P-32 / P-40.
- **Then the real thing from the web** for what the voice names: the post, the profile, the product's logo or page,
  captured and framed in the same cards, shown as it is, source noted.
- **Built substitutes** only when nothing usable turns up: `fx.quoteCard` (posts), a built profile card (generic, no
  platform UI), `fx.appUI({kind: "video", caption})` (a reel stand-in: a dark 9:16 frame with the reel's spoken hook line
  as its caption), `fx.logoPlate` (a product named in type), `fx.silhouette` (a person).
- **Never:** stock footage, AI images of real people, or a built card dressed as a real platform (platform logos,
  verified ticks, its layout copied pixel for pixel). A real post captured from the web is the real thing, not a copy.
- P-34 tiles are the creator's own reel thumbnails, or blank `grid` tiles.
- No comedy layer: no stickers, stamps, meme cues or freeze-frames.

## §9 Transition system

### 9.1 Library (30 fps)
| ID | Transition | Frames | Recipe | Cue role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T-01** | World flip | 0 | A hard cut of the whole ground (paper ↔ umber ↔ ink) on a section's first frame; the new header or text is already on the cut frame; a card follows 4 f later (G-3 or G-8) | transition (a soft hit or swish) |
| **T-02** | Proof cut | 0 | A hard cut between the hub (camera held) and a screen-pinned proof, and back | none (the card's settle carries it) |
| **T-03** | Clearing cut | 0 | A hard cut that empties the frame (the stack and card vanish; 0–2 empty frames) before a new build: the equation, the recap (v01 @ 0:19.06, v03 @ 0:12.52) | none |
| **T-04** | Snap push | 14 | The title blurs out in place (6 f), then the C-1 snap push (`ease: "expoInOut"`, `p.snap: true`; the canvas camera's own motion blur on its middle 2–3 f) | transition (whoosh) |
| **T-05** | Zoom-through | 18 | C-4 into a node, scale 4, then the cut to `then` (P-18) | transition (whoosh) |
| **T-06** | Morph hand-off | 15 | `fx.morphShape` rect → hex (P-19) | reveal (soft) |
| **T-07** | Flurry cut | 0 | G-4: a hard cut to the next tilted proof set | none (the list cue may tick the first three) |
| **T-08** | Ink punch | 0 + 0 | A hard cut to black, a hard cut back (P-08) | none |
| **T-09** | Letter cut | 0 | A hard cut of the whole letter group; the next is whole on the cut frame (G-6) | list cue (one sound for every letter) |
| **T-10** | Hard end | 0 | The last frame ≤ 6 f after the last word; no black tail | none |
| **T-11** | Blur-push cut | 6 + 0 | The outgoing picture punches 1.0 → 1.1 and blurs over 6 f, then a hard cut: the built-in `{"t": <cut>, "type": "zoom-blur", "frames": 6, "pre": 6, "punch": 0.1, "amount": 0.06, "at": "centre"}` (v03 @ 0:04.96–0:05.20 line-up → giant H; 0:13.12–0:13.60 recap → CTA) | none |
| **T-12** | Hub spin | 10 | G-9, entered by the T-02 cut back from a proof | none |

### 9.2 Grammar
| Boundary | Use | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Frame 0 | the hook's first element already moving | a fade-in from black |
| HOOK → CONTEXT | T-01 to umber | a dissolve |
| HOOK → ITEM-1 (SM-1) | T-04 snap push | a cut that loses the hub |
| HOOK → ITEM-1 (SM-2) | T-01 to ink, the P-24 line-up, T-11 into the first letter | — |
| CONTEXT → ITEMS | T-01 back to paper, or T-06 card → hub morph | — |
| Node → node | T-02 cut back into the T-12 hub spin | a second push; a camera dolly |
| Node → its proof → node | T-02, T-02 | a camera move while the proof shows |
| Letter → letter | T-09 | a crossfade |
| Proof → proof | T-07 flurry cut (SM-3), or T-01 / T-03 + a G-8 rise for a new section | a crossfade; a slide in the opposite direction |
| Any → RECAP | T-01 (SM-2), T-03 + P-05 (SM-3); SM-1 has none (or the C-2 pull) | — |
| RECAP → CTA | a hard cut (SM-3) or T-11 (SM-2); the whole CTA is on the cut frame | an entry animation on the CTA |
| The last word | T-10 | a black tail, an outro sting |

### 9.3 How the moves breathe
The hard cut is the backbone: it carries every world flip, every proof, every letter and the CTA, and it's what makes this
calm style feel sharp. The snap push is the one big gesture, so it happens once, at the hook's release (the engine allows
one snap per reel). The zoom-through, the morph and the ink punch are special moves: each one is a moment, worth spending
on the place that earns it, and a reel that uses all three at once has stopped being calm. The rituals repeat on purpose
(the hub spin on every node, the letter cut on every letter); everything else changes from one boundary to the next so no
move becomes a tic.

| Equation element | from the right edge, 8 f expo-out (½ by f2), blur 12 → 0 on f0–3 |

### 10.2 Footage camera
None: there's no footage, so no zoom presets (`zoom_policy: none`). A creator's clip may push 1.00 → 1.04 inside its card
(P-32) and nothing else.

### 10.3 The canvas camera
The camera moves over the graphics world (z1–6), never over the captions (z7) or screen-pinned proof scenes
(`parallax: false`). It looks at world point (x, y) at zoom s; home is (540, 960, 1). It's the presenter's eyes in a reel
with no presenter: where it goes, the viewer goes.

| ID | Move (timeline `move`) | Scale | Frames | Ease | Use in this style |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C-1** | `push` to a node (snap) | 1.0 → 2.3 (2.2–2.4; `fill: 0.7`) | **14** (14–15; `dur: 0.47`, `p.snap: true`: the reference settles in 13–14 f with ≈ 80 % of the scale in 3 f) | **expoInOut** (snap); inOut otherwise | the first node visit (P-12; the reel's one snap); a station's key node (P-17: `dur` 0.5, `inOut`, no snap); the hub growth at f0–1.2 s as a `push` 0.8 → 1.0, `ease: out` |
| **C-2** | `pull` / `settle` | → 1.0 | 18 (12–21) | inOut | the hub recap (P-14); leaving a station |
| **C-3** | `dolly` node → node; `pan`; `drift` | keeps the zoom; dolly `arc: 0.10` | dolly 30, pan 24 (24–36), drift 90 | inOut (drift sine) | list rows (P-15); a held zoomed hub (drift 30–60 px); node → node only as the fallback for the G-9 spin |
| **C-4** | `zoom-through` | × 4 into the node (3–5), then `then` | 18 (18–24) | in | the HOOK → stations hand-off (P-18) |
| **C-5** | `orbit` | radius 40 px (30–50), 2° roll, returns | 60 (45–60) | sine | not in the reference: it reads as a template move here, so leave it out |

The engine's camera limits (they keep motion smooth): known moves only; every move ≥ 0.5 s (0.25 s for the single
`p.snap` push), eased, with ≥ 0.4 s between moves; travel ≤ 110 px and zoom change ≤ 12 % per frame (the snap: 220 px and
35 %); zoom 1.0–4.0 (5.0 inside C-4); text the camera moves meets its size floor at zoom 1 (node labels 40 px, the hub
name 44 / 72 px; at 2.4× labels read at 96 px). During a held zoom the header band (y 270–400) stays clear: push targets
are framed with `fill` 0.6–0.8 so the node sits at y ≥ 560 on screen. The light grounds follow the camera at 0.7
(`fx.ambient follow: 0.7`) for depth; umber and ink grounds don't move.

### 10.4 Hub geometry
World px = screen px at home, for N parts (3 ≤ N ≤ 8):
- the hub `{x: 380, y: 800, w: 320, h: 320}` (hex r 160, centre 540, 960);
- node k (k = 0…N−1) centre = (540 + 330·cos θk, 960 + 330·sin θk), θk = −90° + k·360°/N; its bar rect
  `{x: cx − 115, y: cy − 20, w: 230, h: 40}`;
- **clear of the button column:** a node on the right whose rect (the 230 px bar, or its typed label if wider: ≈ 22 px per
  character at 40 px) has any part in y 1100–1700 slides left until its right edge is ≤ 960 at full hub size (left of the
  buttons at x 970). For N = 6 that's n3 (k = 2, θ 30°): centre (826, 1125) → (≤ 760, 1125) with an 18-character label, its
  leader line ending at the moved bar. The hex, the other nodes and the 330 px ring stay as measured (also N = 3 k = 1,
  N = 8 k = 3);
- labels ≤ 18 characters at 40 px in world space (Inter Tight 500), centred on the bar.

### 10.5 Layer order (back to front)
1. z1 the world ground (`fx.ambient` paper or mist, the flat umber fill, the ink dots)
2. z2 the P-34 thumb field; the P-08 ink-punch ground
3. z3 diagrams (hub, stations, lists), morphs
4. z4 proof cards, clip cards, engine cards, the lead card
5. z5 built text: titles, the ladder, giant initials, the condensed claim, the CTA line
6. z6 view chips and marks that sit on cards (declared `overlaps` with their card)
7. z7 auto-captions (CS-1/2/3)
8. z8 none (avoid it: z8 hides the captions)
9. z9–11 unused (no comedy, no banner, no light passes)

Parallax: the z1 light ground follows the camera at 0.7; the z3–6 scenes that belong to the canvas follow at 1;
**screen-pinned scenes during a held camera (proof cards and their chips) set `parallax: false`**.

### 10.6 Finishing
- No grain, no vignette, no glow, no bloom, no colour grade.
- Soft drop shadows only under cards; type never has a shadow or a stroke.

## §11 Sound

Calm and sparse: the type and the cuts are the drama, and sound only marks a landing. (The reference reels' sound couldn't
be measured, so this is a decision, not a measurement.) Cues come from the bundled pack by catalogue id.

| Line | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Where sound goes** | Transitions (world flips T-01, the snap push T-04, a zoom-through T-05); reveals (the hub landing, the condensed claim, a proof card landing); the list cue (one file for every node visit or letter: the one sound allowed to repeat); the CTA (the hard cut to the card). The hook carries one cue, on its reveal. **Stack lines, typing and drifts are silent** |
| **Meme sounds** | None |
| **The bed** | On from f0: calm, minimal, ≤ 100 BPM; no drop, no riser |
| **Ducking** | The bed sits ≥ 18 dB under the voice while it speaks; creator clips play **muted** (their sound isn't used) |
| **Loudness** | −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP; a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word |

No file more than twice in a reel, except the list cue.

## §12 Footage handling

No camera: the input is a voice and whatever proof the creator owns. Props, a reaction bank and a matte don't exist here.

### 12.1 The voice and the script
| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Voice-over | one voice, a cardioid or lav mic 15–20 cm away, a quiet soft room; 48 kHz WAV (mp3 / m4a accepted); peaks around −6 dBFS; no music or effects baked in (if music is baked in, use a dry voice from their folder when there is one; otherwise keep it and switch the bed off) |
| Pace | 150–170 words a minute (2.5–2.8 words a second); sentences ≤ 12 words; one breath per part |
| Script shape | 45–80 words for 18–30 s: a hook line (≤ 10 words) → optional context (one sentence) → N parts, each "[label]. [one explaining phrase]" → an optional recap ("That's [name]") → the CTA ("Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send you [resource]") |
| The cut | the voice isn't re-cut except to remove breaths over 0.35 s and false starts; a pause up to 0.8 s holds the current stack (the stack clears only after a pause ≥ 0.8 s or a full stack); a pause over 1.5 s is trimmed to 0.5 s |
| No camera | nothing is filmed; no presenter footage enters this format |

### 12.2 The creator's optional files, and what you build without them
| Optional file | Used by | Without it, build | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| The creator's own reels or B-roll (9:16, ≥ 720 × 1280) | P-25, P-26, P-32, P-33 | `fx.appUI({kind: "video", caption})`: a dark 9:16 frame with the reel's spoken line as its caption | proof looks illustrative, not lived-in; it holds |
| Screenshots of posts, profiles or reels the creator holds | P-27, P-28 | the real one captured from the web (§12.3); else `fx.quoteCard` with the verbatim quote, a built profile card with initials (no stats) | a rebuilt card has no real avatar or counts; it holds |
| Thumbnails of the creator's own reels | P-34 | blank `grid` tiles | the field reads as abstract; it holds |
| A screenshot of the lead magnet | P-40 | the P-39 built lead card (name + skeleton rows) | no real rows; it holds |
| The lead magnet's real size ("1,500+ hooks") | the P-39 count line | a count-free line ("The free [resource].") | a weaker promise; it holds |

Minimum sizes: clip cards (P-32 at 540 wide) ≥ 720 × 1280; full-bleed clips (P-33) ≥ 1080 × 1920; screenshots for P-26 /
P-40 ≥ 1080 px on the long side. Smaller files go in the smaller hero card (P-25, 432 wide) or are replaced by the built
substitute.

### 12.3 Third-party moments: fetch the real thing
When the voice names a real post, another creator's reel, a profile, a product or a headline, the viewer should see the
real one.
1. **Find the moments:** a post read aloud, another creator's reel, a profile, a product, a headline.
2. **The creator's own files first:** clips and screenshots in their folder, and their lead-magnet screenshot.
3. **Otherwise fetch it from the web:** the real post, profile, logo or headline page, captured and framed on the part
   that matters, in this style's cards (P-25 / P-26 / P-27 / P-28). Note where it came from. No URLs in scenes: download
   it into the project and use it as an asset.
4. **Use it as it is** (crop, frame, card), never altered to say something else; a post or headline is shown word for
   word.
5. **Nothing usable to be found:** rebuild it from its exact words, with no labels and no credit lines:

| Moment | Built substitute | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| A post or tweet read aloud | `fx.quoteCard({quote: the script's exact words, name})` | P-27 |
| Another creator's reel as an example | `fx.appUI({kind: "video", caption: "[the reel's hook line, as the voice says it]"})` in the card rect | P-25 / P-26 / P-32 |
| A creator or account as proof | a built profile card (initials avatar, name or handle only if spoken, no stats) | P-28 |
| A product, app or tool | `fx.logoPlate({name, kicker})` (the name set in type) | instead of P-37 |
| A person | `fx.silhouette({name, role})` | instead of P-27 |
| A news headline | `fx.headlineCard({masthead, date, headline: exact})` on a light card at x 90, y 450; the beat carries `source {masthead, date, headline}` | instead of P-27 |

### 12.4 Frame rate and audio
1080 × 1920, 30 fps CFR. The voice chain: high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, light compression, −14 LUFS integrated, true peak
≤ −1.5 dBTP. Every frame is a pure function of its index: the thumb field and the flurry tilts are seeded
(`ctx.rngStable`).

## §13 What your plan should settle

Your `ideas.md` is a page or two, for you. In this style it has decided, before any code:
1. **The framework line:** its name, the shape (SM-1, SM-2 or SM-3), the N parts (≤ 18 characters each), the proof per
   part.
2. **The hook:** the archetype, the title with its two alternates, frame 0 and the payoff moment.
3. **The worlds:** the pack per section and the exact frame of every flip (written in both `theme_flips` and `world`).
4. **The camera path:** the `canvas_nodes`, every move (C-…, its target, `fill`, frames, ease), the one snap, every hub spin,
   every drift, the pull-out if there's a recap.
5. **The type per span:** CS-1, CS-2, CS-3 or hidden under built text (`captions.overrides`, `captions.hide`), with every
   built text scene's words.
6. **The proof per beat:** the pattern; the creator's file, the real thing fetched (with its source) or the built
   substitute; every number's source.
7. **The CTA:** the keyword, the resource's name and size as stated (or none), P-39 or P-40, and the still hold.
8. **The sound:** the reveal cue, the list cue, the flips, the CTA cut, the bed.
9. **The moments you'll look at hardest on the storyboard:** f0, 1.0 s (the cover), the first node visit or letter (the
   label mid-type, the bar framed under the header band), one proof card, the recap, the CTA; and a right-hand hub node at

## §15 Your look at the storyboard: the checklist

Watch it once as a stranger with a thumb over the next reel, then once as the editor whose name is on it. Fix what
bothers you, in one pass.

**The style (does it feel like §The feel?)**
- Frame 0: the shape or hero card is already moving and the first word is rising; the title reads within a second.
- The voice is visible: every phrase flies in or swaps in exactly as it's said, and the stack reads like a designed
  sentence.
- One framework, one shape; the camera visits it; the same ritual for every node or letter; the hub returns at the same
  framing after every proof.
- Nothing fades: flips, proofs, letters and the CTA are hard cuts with the new content on the cut frame.
- Calm: no jokes, no shakes; colour is a whisper, the signal on one element at a time.
- It ends complete, not bigger: the pull-back or the recap, then the still lead card.
- Start to end, without sound: you could say the framework and its parts afterwards.

**Craft (by eye, in context)**
- No presenter anywhere: the stage is `L-canvas` (hidden) for the whole reel. A person inside a proof clip reads when
  they're the point; nothing buries a face by accident.
- Every spoken phrase is on screen once (an auto-caption or built text, never both), as it's said; every label, card,
  letter and count lands on its word; the push frames its node on the word.
- No text over text by accident: one stack at a time, never under a card; the header line clear of card text.
- The stack alternates sans / serif without a break, ≤ 5 lines, ≤ 3 words and ≤ 16 characters per line; header lines 1–2
  words; ink on light worlds, white on umber and ink; text big enough to read on a phone (sans 80, serif 112, header 76,
  labels 40 in world space).
- Meaning text clear of Instagram's buttons; the right-hand hub node clear of the button column; nothing the camera moves
  sits in the header band while captions show.
- Every number the creator claims is spoken, scripted or stated (and in `figures.json` when not spoken); illustrations
  carry no labels; quotes and fetched posts word for word; built cards never dressed as a real platform.
- The promised count = the nodes, letters or proofs shown; every node typed and visited; the CTA keyword in the voice =
  the keyword on the card; the lead magnet's name and size as stated; the keyword readable on the card.
- Names, tools and brands spelt exactly.
- The file itself (1080 × 1920, 30 fps, −14 LUFS, the bed under the voice, creator clips muted, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the
  last word, no black tail) is the render's job; it checks it.
