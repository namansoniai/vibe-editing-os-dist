# Evidence Explainer Style Playbook (template v2)

## The feel

This reel is a case being made, and the viewer is the jury. It opens on the creator's face, close and out of focus,
pulling back and snapping sharp as the first sentence starts: someone has something serious to tell you. Then, on the name
of the source, the frame splits. The evidence slides down from the top behind a thin line of the topic's colour, the
creator drops into the bottom half, the article blurs in and snaps sharp, and red bars wipe across the headline exactly as
the words are said. You aren't being told. You're being shown the receipt.

That's the engine: claim, then proof, in the same breath. Every outlet named gets its headline, word for word. Every date,
name, place and number spoken lands on screen as a plate, because there are no captions here: the screen carries only
what proves the point. Nothing on it is a transcript; all of it is evidence. And when a run of proof is done, the verdict
comes down like a rubber stamp: SCAM, FAKE, TRUE.

The creator stands on a mottled plate of colour that tells you the topic before a word lands: crimson for injustice,
ember for war, gold for money. They never leave for long. People believe a person holding the documents, so the face stays
in the bottom of the split, in the ring over a long read, in the glowing card beside the numbers, and it comes back full
frame for every conclusion.

The voice is calm; the proof is dense. Nothing decorates. The camera is locked and earns one eased punch on the word that
matters. Between chapters a warm light leak burns across the cut like a page turned under a lamp. It builds like a closing
argument: each beat opens on a new kind of proof, the strongest document is saved for last, there's a breath before the
stamp, then the last line, and it ends.

**The test:** pause on any frame and everything on screen is either the person making the case or the evidence for it.

## What this playbook is

You're editing one talking-head take of a creator making claims about a news story, a policy, a price, a myth or an
incident, and you have the authority to make it the most convincing explainer in their niche. This playbook is the style,
pulled from four reels of the original creator (v01–v04, about 130–179 s each), measured at full frame rate, then
sharpened. Read it all, every time. Use it the way a great editor uses a reference: take what fits this reel, invent when a
moment needs more, and never ship a frame that breaks the feel above.

**Who it's for and what it needs.** Explainers, news and myth-busting creators, fact-checkers: anyone whose reel makes
claims and proves them. Input: one talking-head take shot against a plain, flat background (for the cut-out), plus
whatever screenshots, clips and photos the creator has; the cited articles, posts and photos they don't have are fetched
from the web, source noted; anything still missing gets built (§12). The cut-out is required (the
backdrop plate replaces the room) unless the reel is shot in a tidy real room (TH-room). There are no speech captions in
any language; plates and chips are written in the on-screen language (English by default; Hinglish, or Hindi in
Devanagari, when the creator's copy says so). The style suits long reels (100–170 s); a shorter take simply holds fewer
evidence beats. Comedy is off. Machine values live in `tokens.json`; where this text gives a number tokens also holds,
they agree.

## §1 Procedure: how to approach a reel in this style

You watch, listen, plan, build and look at the storyboard yourself; the edit skill has the mechanics. This style's
craft is two passes: **claim → card pairing** (what on screen proves this?) and the **plate pass** (what was named, and is
it on screen?). Do them carefully and everything else follows.

1. **Find the case.** Write the reel's verdict in one line ("petrol prices are a scam", "hot water doesn't cure dengue").
   Then cut the talk into evidence beats EB-1…EB-n (§7.1): each is one claim, its proof (one to three items), optional
   counter-evidence and optional mini-verdict. Mark where each verdict lands.
2. **Pick the theme pack** by the topic of the verdict (§4.3) and say why in one line.
3. **Pair every claim with its card.** For every claim beat, capture the citation: the masthead (outlet name), the date,
   the **exact** headline (from the creator's screenshot, the real article fetched from the web, or word for word from the
   script), and the highlight spans (the words the creator says while the card is up). One card per claim. A claim with
   no source in the script, the creator's files or a web search is flagged ("unsupported: needs a source or a rewrite") so the creator hears about it; you never invent
   a headline to make a point.
4. **The plate pass.** List every spoken date, proper name, place and number with its word time. Each becomes a plate, a
   chip, a card highlight or a figure. Numbers that are compared, or derived by arithmetic, go to `plan/figures.json`
   (§8.5); a one-off number becomes P-NUMBER-CHIP. A name already on a card's highlighted phrase is covered by the card.
   Translate or transliterate only what becomes a plate: names keep their real spelling; Hindi number words become digits.
5. **Feel the tone of every line:** `claim` · `evidence` · `context` · `warn` · `verdict` · `cta`. The tone picks the
   camera move and the role colour (§10.3).
6. **Write the hook** (§6): the archetype and its variant from the first sentence, the first label, the post title (8–10
   candidates, the best by the stopper test, two alternates).
7. **Get the real evidence** (§12.5): for every third-party moment, the creator's file first, else the real article,
   post or photo fetched from the web (source noted); rebuild from exact text only when nothing usable turns up.
8. **Plan the stage.** One split orientation for the reel (top for news and people, bottom for money and objects); every
   split on its source word; the ring for long reads and vertical clips; the data stage for comparisons (§3).
9. **Plan the punctuation:** burns at chapter turns and evidence swaps, blur-in arrivals, one camera move per presenter
   stint at most, the stamps, the breath before the verdict (§7.5, §9, §10.2).
10. **Count on the cut-out** (unless TH-room): `matte: required`, so it starts right after the cut; make sure it's there
    before the storyboard. Look at the hair edge on three frames (the start, the middle, a gesture); a fringe of the old
    background wider than 2 px, or a green tee on a green screen, means TH-room (FB-1), and the creator hears why.

## §2 Hard rules: the craft and the style's never-list

**Craft, by eye** (judge it on the storyboard, in context, the way an editor does):
- **Keep the person clear.** People believe the face, so keep it and the hair clear of front layers (plates, chips,
  stamps, glyphs, cards) when the moment is about them; the geometry is in §3.6. The framing that does it: in L-full the
  plate band at chest height, its top ≈ 40 px below the chin; in a split the face's band free of text; nothing drawn over
  the ring or the rim card. When the moment wants otherwise, that's editing; what's never fine is a head chopped or a face
  buried by accident. Behind the person is fair game, text included (`behind: true`): the backdrop plate always is.
- **No text over text.** One plate or chip at a time on the presenter; a card's highlight bars sit under its own words; a
  stamp lands on evidence, never across a plate.
- **On the word.** Every card, plate, chip, figure and stamp starts 2 f before its trigger word and is fully on within
  ±5 f; highlight bars start on the first word of their phrase and travel with the speech; a split opens within ±0.25 s
  of its source word. Cuts sit on word boundaries ±1 f; no dead air (the one deliberate pause is a breath of ≤ 0.4 s
  before a verdict stamp).
- **Citations exact.** Every source beat shows the masthead, the date and the headline. Headlines and quotes are word for
  word (case and edge punctuation aside), never paraphrased, never re-ordered, always attributed; every highlighted span
  occurs in the headline or excerpt text and is spoken while it's highlighted. A created headline quotes only what the
  script, the transcript or the creator's own texts say.
- **Numbers right.** Every number shown is from the script, a cited source or the creator; compared numbers are figures
  in `plan/figures.json` and recompute; a number the creator says is shown exactly as said. Illustrations may use
  made-up but realistic detail with no label (`illustrative: true`), but in this style anything that reads as evidence (a
  figure, a name, a date, a headline) is real, because here the viewer takes every number as a claim.
- **Promise integrity.** Every "I'll show you", "here's the proof", "let's check" is paid on screen in the same beat; a
  spoken count ("three cases") equals the cases shown; a CTA keyword is on screen ≥ 1.5 s.
- **Spelling.** Names of people, outlets, places and laws exactly as in the creator's sources or script; add them to the
  glossary.
- **The real source.** Every third-party moment is the creator's file, the real thing fetched from the web (the article
  page, the post, the photo; source noted), or, when nothing usable turns up, a card rebuilt from its exact words (§12.5).
  A fetched page is shown as it is, never altered to say something it doesn't.
- **Privacy.** Personal identifiers in screenshots (phone numbers, emails, addresses, private people's account IDs) are
  blurred for their whole time on screen.
- **Readable.** Plates and chips ≥ 1.0 s on screen and ≥ 0.25 s per word; titles hold ≥ 10 f after they finish building;
  a highlighted phrase and a headline read at ≥ 40 px after framing (crop tighter if not). Meaning text stays inside
  x 64–1016, y 110–1500 and out of Instagram's right-hand column (x > 970 between y 900 and 1540).
- **Audio.** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP, the bed ≥ 18 dB under the voice, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last
  word, no black tail.

**Never in this style:**
- Speech subtitles of any kind, karaoke words or kinetic captions. Text on screen is evidence, plates, figures or verdicts.
- A created card made to look like a real outlet's page: no outlet fonts, colours, logos or layout copied.
- A highlight on words the creator doesn't say; a paraphrase presented as the source's words.
- Stock clichés: globes, newsroom stock loops, "breaking news" wipes, hooded hackers, gavels. Show the actual document,
  place, number or person.
- Meme sounds, emoji, stickers or comedy marks. A stamp is a verdict, not a joke.
- Red text on the crimson plate, yellow text on the gold plate, white plate text without its dark plate on a light photo.
- A backdrop that clashes with its rim colour (change both together, with the theme).
- Effect packs: RGB-split packs, film burns, glitch packs, any light leak that isn't the T-06 burn; a 1-frame jump re-crop
  every few seconds (not this style: the presenter's camera is locked between punches). A new move is welcome when it's
  built in this style's language.
- A burn that isn't this burn: much longer than its 16 f, more than two bright peaks, colours outside its warm ramp.
- Graphics that only decorate. Every plate names something spoken; every card proves something said.
- A matte shadow faked with a dark blob behind the head (the engine has no matte shadow yet; render none).

**Ring positions** (the inline `corner` on the stage entry): the default **bottom-left-centre (370, 1440)** (v02);
**top-right (796, 370)** when the card's text block sits low (v03 @0:06); **bottom-right (796, 1400)** when the card's
text is left-aligned and short (v03 @0:46); **bottom-left (290, 1490)** for a full-bleed clip whose subject is on the
right (v03 @1:30). The ring never moves while it's on screen; a new position needs a new evidence item.

**How the layouts behave.**
- **L-full** holds a claim for as long as the claim lasts. The framing is locked (measured < 3 % scale drift over 4 s,
  v01 @1:29.2–1:33.3): a stint gets one move at most, a Z-2 punch on its key word or a Z-1 slow push, never both; live
  gestures carry the rest.
- **The split opens on the source word** (the outlet's name, "report", "according to", "this video", the person's name)
  within ±0.25 s, and holds one evidence item at a time.
- **L-pip-ring** is for a card that must be read full-frame for longer than the split's top band can hold it (about 4 s
  or more), or a vertical clip.
- **An absence is short.** L-hidden and L-band run only as long as the proof needs; in the original reels the longest
  stretch without the face was about 7 s of full-bleed clip (a 12.5 s TV clip in v01 was not copied). After an absence the
  presenter returns in L-full or a split, never straight into another full-bleed card.
- **One orientation per reel.** Pick L-split-top (news and people) or L-split-bottom (money and objects) and keep it;
  never alternate the two inside one beat.

### 3.5 Safe zones and bands
- Meaning text: x 64–1016, y 110–1500. Nothing readable in y > 1540, or in x > 970 between y 900 and 1540.
- **Plate band (L-full):** centre y 1380 (v03 measured slab y 1342–1430, cy 1386; v02 sat higher, at ≈ 1240). A plate's
  top never rises above y 1180 in L-full, where it would meet the chin.
- **Plates in a split** go in the evidence band, never in the face's band: in L-split-top the plate's bottom edge sits
  70 px above the seam (y ≤ 880) or 70 px above the card's bottom; in L-split-bottom they sit in the bottom band, above
  y 1500.
- **Card column:** x 96–984; the top band y 150–900 (L-split-top); the full frame y 230–1200 (L-pip-ring, L-hidden). With
  the ring at its default corner its top edge is at y 1200, so the card's last text line ends by y 1160 (40 px clear).
- **Stamp box:** x 140–940, y 560–1100 on full-frame evidence (L-hidden, L-pip-ring over a card, the data stage). In a
  split the stamp centres in the evidence band (L-split-top y ≈ 300–900; L-split-bottom y ≈ 1000–1500). Over the presenter
  in L-full it lands in the chest band, its top ≥ 40 px below the chin.

### 3.6 The person
The creator is the witness everyone came to hear. Keep their face and hair clear of what's in front of them unless the
moment wants otherwise, judged by eye; these measures are how the layouts do it.
- **L-full:** setup A puts the head top at y 140–260 and the chin at y 760–1000 (measured head top / chin: v03 @0:00.3
  134 / 768, v01 @0:00.5 180 / 1005, v02 @0:01 150 / 930). The date plate at cy 1380 starts ≥ 320 px under the lowest
  chin, the keyword chip (measured y 1266–1414) ≥ 260 px. A Z-2 punch keeps the chin above y 1180 (else use 1.12).
- **L-split-top:** the presenter band is y 950–1920, framed with face 0.3 and eye 0.4: the face box is about 290 px tall,
  centred at y ≈ 1338 (box ≈ y 1192–1484), so the head top sits at y ≈ 1090 and the hair rises toward the seam. That band
  is the face's: plates, chips and stamps go in the evidence band. (`layout.plate_cy_split` 820 is this evidence-band
  position: a plate's bottom edge at y 880.) A year chip straddling the seam (≈ y 890–1010) stays ≈ 40 px above the head top; look at
  it on the storyboard.
- **L-split-bottom:** the presenter band is y 0–960 (face 0.3, eye 0.42): the face box ≈ 288 px tall, centred at
  y ≈ 403, head top ≈ y 158, chin ≈ y 547. Graphic text lives in the bottom band (y 964–1500).
- **L-pip-ring:** the circle (y 1200–1680, x 130–610 at the default corner) is the window; the face fills about 60 % of
  it (face 0.6, eye 0.48) and the head is clipped by the ring, never drawn over it. Nothing sits on the ring; the card's
  text ends 40 px above it.
- **L-pip-card:** the card (x 64–404, y 1180–1600) frames the face at about 55 % of its height; the data rows end by
  y 1120, 60 px above the card's top edge.
- **L-hidden / L-band:** no presenter. A third-party speaker's face in a clip gets the same courtesy: a quote overlay
  sits above or below it rather than across it.
- **Behind the head:** the backdrop plate lives there (`behind: true`). Text may sit behind the presenter too, but
  nothing in this style needs it.
- **Crops:** the face is about a third of the frame height in L-full, so a wide desk take needs a 1.35–1.6× base reframe;
  the Z-2 punch (1.20, measured 1.12–1.23) goes on top of it.

### 4.2 Meanings
- **Red is evidence and alarm:** the highlighted phrase, the false claim, the higher price. Red is never decoration.
- **Green is verified or better:** a TRUE stamp, the lower or older price.
- **Yellow is "look here":** a keyword chip, a number chip, a place on the map, the route.
- **The rim colour is the theme:** it frames the presenter (hairline, ring, card border) and nothing else.
- **Black and white is archive:** photos of public figures and old clips may run in B&W (§4.4).
- Outlets' brand colours never appear on created cards.

### 4.4 Clip treatments (GR-…)
The presenter's footage is **never regraded**. Treatments live inside the scene that shows the clip or photo
(CSS filters; the engine's grade module isn't needed).

|---|---|---|---|

### 4.5 Rules
- At most three bright roles in a frame (`max_bright_per_frame` 3): the rim, the red and one of yellow, green or cyan.
- Yellow chips always carry `ink` text; red bars always carry white text; plates always carry white text on `plate`.
- On TH-gold, yellow chips get a 3 px `ink` outline (yellow on gold is otherwise unreadable).
- The `flash` role is a light pass, never a fill for text.

### 5.5 Language and numbers
- **On-screen language:** plates and chips are written in the on-screen language. English (Latin) is the default; Hindi
  (Devanagari) uses the `deva` slot, no ALL CAPS (Devanagari has no case), plate size +8 px.
- **Names:** the real spelling from the source ("SMIT MACHCHHAR"), never translated.
- **Dates:** `D MON YYYY` in caps with a 3–4-letter month ("8 JAN 2026", "30 SEPT 2026"); Devanagari "8 जनवरी 2026".
- **Numbers:** English reels default to `$`, international grouping and K / M / B with up to 2 decimals. Hinglish and Hindi
  reels use `₹` (pre-painted), Indian grouping (₹1,25,000) and lakh / crore on plates longer than 7 characters (₹1,25,00,000
  → ₹1.25 Cr), up to 2 decimals for prices (₹32.98); dollars from a dollar source stay `$` with international grouping
  ($105). Ranges take an en dash without spaces (₹60–₹65). Units in brackets on the data stage ("(PER LITRE)"), inline on
  chips ("₹32.98 PER LITRE"). In text you write, never "Rs" or "INR".
- **Word for word beats format.** A headline or quote that writes "Rs", "INR" or international grouping is quoted as
  written. Show it as the real page (the creator's screenshot or the article captured from the web, P-CITE-SHOT) whenever
  possible; if it must be a created card, keep the words exactly, even if a number-format check flags it. Plates and chips you write always follow the reel's number format.
- **The script stays untouched:** text inside the creator's screenshots and fetched pages stays as it is (Devanagari headlines
  included); created cards quote the script in the language it was spoken or written in.

## §6 Hook system

**The hook title is a promise.** This style has no on-screen title: the hook is a claim and its first proof, labelled by
a plate. The promise lives in the **post title** (the caption field and the cover), and it promises a verdict the reel
delivers: "Petrol prices SCAM in 2026!", "Does hot water really cure dengue?". It doesn't have to repeat the spoken words;
it has to be true to what the reel proves. Write 8–10 from the formula (§6.5) and the proven shapes ("Why your X isn't
working", "The X nobody tells you", "Stop doing X", a number or a contrast), score them on outcome, curiosity, who it's for
and brevity, pick the best, and keep the next two as alternates.

### 6.1 The stopper test
| Test | This style's version |
|---|---|
| Thumbnail | Not the job of f0 (a blurred face on a coloured plate); the cover is chosen separately from the hook's strongest frame (the split with its first label, or the stamp) |
| Mute | Not required (`sound_on`), but the plates make the first 3 s partly legible anyway |
| Motion at f0 | Live footage under the Z-0 pull-open and its focus pull (measured v01, v02: scale 1.23–1.33 → 1.00 by 0.45–0.52 s, sharp by 0.38 s); in H9-B and H9-C a live gesture |
| Dense opening | The first three seconds feel full: the face resolves, the panel slides, the evidence snaps sharp, the first label lands (v01: blur-in, slide-down, the TV arrives, the TV sharpens; v04: slide-up, red tint, stamp, flare). Each lands while the last one settles |
| Payoff | The first evidence panel is readable by 1.5 s and the first label (a plate, a highlight bar or a stamp) lands by 2.0 s with `payoff: true` (measured v01 1.33 / 1.83, v02 1.33 / 2.0, v03 0.17 plate / 1.5 route label, v04 0.67 / 1.83) |

### 6.5 Plates and titles
You write two things: the **plates** (the exact spoken items: names, dates, places, numbers) and the **post title**.
- **Plate formula:** the spoken item, nothing added: "8 JAN 2026", "SMIT MACHCHHAR", "₹32.98 PER LITRE", "HIJACKING". ≤ 4
  words (chips ≤ 3). No verbs, no emoji, no adjectives.
- **Post title formula:** `[claim or accusation] + [the object] + [! or ?]`, ≤ 8 words, sentence case with one CAPS word:
  "Petrol prices SCAM in 2026!", "Don't travel to X! You can be arrested", "X has been destroyed… by Indian media". Write
  8–10; pick the one that names the specific object, promises a verdict the reel delivers, and fits 8 words; keep two
  alternates.
- **Never:** "shocking truth", "you won't believe", "exposed!!!", more than one "!" or "?", a verdict the body doesn't
  prove.

### 6.6 Hook sound
The hook may carry a soft hit on f0, a whoosh on the slide, a pop on the card, a tick on the first label and an impact on a
stamp (H9-C). The bed enters after the hook (§11).

### 6.7 CTA
The default is **no CTA**: the reel ends on the presenter's last line, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word (v01, v02,
v03). When the creator wants one:

| Device | Spoken pattern | On screen | Hold | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cross_promo` | "The full story is in my long video" | **P-CROSS-PROMO:** the long video's thumbnail (SH-6) on a dark card in the split's graphic band, its title line under it in `display` 700 40 px; fallback FB-6 | 2.0–3.5 s | The last 4 s |
| `comment_keyword` | "Comment KEYWORD and I'll send you the sources" | **P-CTA-PLATE** "COMMENT KEYWORD" (kind `cta-keyword`) at cy 1380 in L-full; the date-plate look, the keyword in `accent` | ≥ 1.5 s | The last 3 s |
| `link_bio` | "Sources are linked in my bio" | **P-CTA-PLATE** "SOURCES · LINK IN BIO" | ≥ 1.5 s | The last 3 s |

No silence is needed before the CTA; the voice runs on. No end card, no follow-and-save stack.

**Series and sponsors (only when the creator's copy turns them on).** A series shows a "PART {n}" tag in the date-plate
look, top-left (x 64, y 150), for the first 2.0 s after the hook. A paid integration gets P-SPONSOR-PLATE: the sponsor's
name set in type on a plate with "Paid partnership" held ≥ 2 s, never over evidence.

## §7 Structure and rhythm

### 7.1 Structure: evidence
**The arc:** the hook (a claim and its first proof) → **EB-1…EB-n** (claim → evidence → counter-evidence) → **the
verdict** → (the CTA). A long reel holds several evidence beats; the original reels ran four to seven.

| Unit | Content | How it feels |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | An HA-09 variant or an alternate | Over in a few seconds: the claim and its first proof land before the viewer can doubt it |
| EB (evidence beat) | One claim, its proof (one to three evidence items), optional counter-evidence, an optional mini-verdict | As long as the proof needs; in the original reels roughly 12–30 s |
| Verdict | The answer to the hook's question; the stamp | A held moment: the breath, the stamp, the line |
| End | The last line on the presenter; the optional CTA | Abrupt. It's said, and it's over |

### 7.2 Markers: none (spoken only)
No numbering, no chapter badge, no progress rail (v01–v04). A chapter turn is marked by a **T-06 light-leak burn**, or by
the backdrop changing for a place, never by a numeral. A spoken count ("three cases") is honoured by the cases themselves.

### 7.3 The evidence beat ritual (the same every time; frames at 30 fps)
1. **The claim on the presenter** (L-full): the camera locked, then either the Z-1 slow push (1.00 → 1.05 over 3–4 s)
   through the claim or one Z-2 punch (1.00 → 1.20, 12 f) on its key word, held to the next cut. Every spoken date, name or
   number gets its plate at cy 1380.
2. **The source word → the split** (G-1 slide-down, 28 f) or, for a long read, **into the ring** (G-4 blur-through cut),
   within ±0.25 s of the source word.
3. **The evidence arrives** with a T-04 blur-in (blur held, sharp by f6–f11), starting on f4 of the stage move. A photo
   then pushes 1.00 → 1.15 over 15 f (ease-out); a text card stays still.
4. **Highlight the spoken words:** each span's bar starts on its first word and wipes L → R **linearly over the time the
   phrase is spoken** (≈ 0.3 s per word, 12–30 f per span; measured 0.8 s for "11 September 2001" and for the two-word span
   after it, v02 @0:27.52–0:28.32 and @0:28.80–0:29.60); bars accumulate in speaking order and never fade (`hlAt` =
   the word's time − `t_in`).
5. **Label** the person, place or date on the evidence (P-NAME-PLATE, P-PLACE-PIN) if it's spoken.
6. **Hold** ≥ 1.0 s after the last bar. A text card holds still while something else lives (a new bar, the live
   presenter in the split or the ring, a clip playing inside it); photos and renders drift (v02's BBC card: 0 % scale
   change over 4 s).
7. **Counter-evidence** (optional): a second card with **outline-box** highlights (white 4 px boxes) instead of bars, or
   the data stage, so red stays for the claim.
8. **Return** (G-3 blur-through cut) to L-full for the presenter's conclusion, **or** a T-06 burn straight into the next
   item or beat.
9. **The verdict** (where a run of proof resolves): GR-alarm 3 f before, then P-STAMP on the evidence (or in the chest band
   over the presenter, §3.5) + Z-4 shake; hold 1.5–2.5 s.

### 7.4 Open loops and the re-hook
- **Loops:** the hook's question ("is it a scam?"), the "???" value on the data stage (v04 @0:12–0:14), "but there is one
  more thing", a stamped "?" verdict (H9-C).
- **Every loop is paid on screen:** the "???" becomes the number in the same layout; the "?" stamp becomes the verdict
  stamp; a promised clip is shown.
- **The re-hook is built into the beat.** Each evidence beat opens with a burn and a new kind of proof (a screenshot, then
  a clip, then the data stage, then a map, then a person), so the reel keeps hooking itself without ever saying so. Change
  the kind of card from one beat's opening to the next, so each one feels like a new exhibit arriving. The hook is over
  fast, so the first beat arrives while the promise is fresh. No series card.

### 7.5 Rhythm, by feel
- **The energy curve:** level and serious. It rises at each verdict stamp and at each data reveal, and settles back into
  the calm voice between. No comedy beats.
- **The speech is the rhythm.** It never sits still, but it's never random: something new arrives when the words give it a
  reason (a source named, a number said, a turn). With no captions, the plates, highlights and cards are the pulse; the
  live presenter is motion enough between them.
- **Escalation:** the strongest evidence comes last before the verdict: the official document, the number that settles
  it, the clip that contradicts the claim.
- **Tension and release:** the proof builds bar by bar, then the burn turns the page. The pause of a breath before a
  stamp is the reel's one deliberate silence; the stamp lands harder for it.
- **Holds are evidence too.** A headline needs a beat to be read after its last bar; a number needs a beat after it lands.
  Hold, then move.
- **Contrast keeps attention:** the loud frames (the burn, the stamp, the red tint) earn their loudness because the
  stretches around them are calm: a locked camera, a dark card, a voice.
- **Ending:** abrupt. The last sentence on the presenter in L-full with a Z-1 push or a Z-2 punch on the last clause, a
  hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word (v01 @2:36, v02 @2:57). With `cross_promo`, the thumbnail card fills the last 2–4 s.
- For reference, measured on the four reels (a description, not a target): cuts 3.0–3.4 per 10 s, median shot 2.0–2.9 s
  (p90 6.1–8.0 s, longest 8.4–13.1 s), light-leak burns 3.4–6.0 a minute (the closest two 2.6 s apart, typically about 7 s),
  punches 1.11–1.22×. The cut count is a side effect of the proof arriving, not a goal.

The graphics illustrate; the voice argues. Evidence carries most of the screen time, and most of it shares the frame
with the creator (the split, the ring, the rim card). Compared numbers become pictures (the data stage, one scale); a

person), never from a quota; the evidence-beat ritual is the one deliberate repeat.

### 8.5 Data, citations and truth
**Numbers.**
- **What becomes a figure:** every number compared with another (then vs now, A vs B, a share of a whole, ranked rows, a
  trend), and any number derived by arithmetic in the script ("that's 61%", "it went up 26%"). A lone stated number
  becomes a P-NUMBER-CHIP, still traceable to the script.
- **`plan/figures.json`:** `inputs` with `from: script` (+ the `said` words), `source:<citation id>` (a cited article) or
  `creator` (stated by them); formulas from this style's set: `sum`, `diff`, `ratio`, `percent_change`, `per_period`,
  `unit_convert`, `cagr`; tolerance is display rounding; one `scale_id` shared by compared figures (`S-petrol`,
  `S-cases`).
- **Format:** the reel's number format (§5.5). Dollar values from dollar sources keep `$` with international grouping
  (`format: {currency: "$", grouping: "international"}` on that figure).
- **Display:** values roll 18 f and land on the spoken number word (±5 f); comparisons share one scale and one layout (the
  2014 and 2026 rows of P-DATA-STAGE are identical in position and size); units always shown ("(PER LITRE)", "per month",
  "%", "crore"); the unknown is "???" in the value's colour until its step, never a placeholder number.
- **Illustrations:** a concept curve with no data carries no axis numbers and no label (`illustrative: true`). A
  reconstruction shows no number or name the script doesn't state.

**Citations** (the core of this style).
- **Every source beat** carries `source {masthead, date, headline}` and `highlight_spans[]`; the spans occur in the
  headline (or the excerpt) and are spoken while they're highlighted; a created headline is word for word from the script,
  the transcript or the creator's texts.
- **The real page** (P-CITE-SHOT, the creator's screenshot or captured from the web): body text inside it is decorative; the highlighted phrase and the headline
  must read at ≥ 40 px after framing, or crop tighter (v03 @1:03 had body text at 22 px: that fails).
- **A created card** (P-CITE-CARD, only when no real page turns up): the masthead set in `serif` 700 (broadsheets) or `display` 800 (TV, agencies), the
  date in `display` 600 40 px, the exact headline (TX-8), the highlight recipe of §8.3.
- **Highlight variants, one per card:** red bars = the claim (the default); white outline boxes (P-OUTLINE-BOX) = a number
  or date being checked; the yellow marker (`accent`, ink text) = a post or statement read aloud (P-POST-CARD).
- **No figure numbering.** No credit lines: the masthead and date on the card are the citation.
- **One card per claim.** A created card never imitates an outlet's logo or design. An unsupported claim is flagged in the plan, never
  decorated.

### 8.6 Comedy layer (off)
Comedy is off: stamps are verdicts, not jokes, and no meme sound ever plays. If the creator's copy turns `light` on (the
ceiling), the one device is a die-cut photo of a public figure (the creator's, else fetched), sliding up from the bottom edge beside
the presenter for ≤ 2 s on a sarcastic line (v02 @2:56). It's the reel's one wink: once, on the line that earns it, or not
at all.

### 8.7 Assets
- **The creator's files first** (screenshots, clips, photos, their own thumbnail), framed by these patterns; never
  altered to say something they don't; crops keep the masthead or the date visible.
- **Then the real thing from the web** (§12.5): the article page captured, the post, the public photo, the map, source
  noted; framed and highlighted the same way, never altered.
- **Created substitutes** (the created-substitute toolkit, §12.5) only when nothing usable turns up: no labels, no credit
  lines; a created card never imitates a real outlet's design (no logo, no outlet colours, no outlet fonts).
- **People:** the creator's photo, else a public photo fetched from the web; otherwise P-PERSON-SIL.
- **Maps:** a real map screenshot (the creator's or captured), or the created schematic with its MAP NOT TO SCALE corner
  tag.
- No stock clichés; personal identifiers blurred.

### 9.1 Library

### 9.2 Grammar
| Boundary | Use | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Frame 0 | H9-A: Z-0 + P-FOCUS-PULL; H9-B / H9-C: sharp and static | A fade from black, a title card |
| Claim → source | T-02 on the source word | A hard cut to a full-bleed card while the presenter is mid-sentence |
| Card → card (same beat) | T-01 inside the panel, T-04 for the second card, or T-06 when the item changes kind | A slide (the split is already open) |
| Evidence → presenter | T-11 (or T-06 at a beat boundary) | A slide-up when a cut fits |
| Beat → beat, a chapter | T-06 | A burn stacked on a burn |
| Presenter → long read / the ring | T-11 (G-4) | T-02 for a read the top band can't hold |
| Presenter → full-bleed clip | T-11 or T-06 | T-07 for a short clip |
| A number lands | A Z-2 punch on the word, then the chip or the counter | A burn |
| The verdict | GR-alarm 3 f → P-STAMP + Z-4; out with a white flare + a cut into a T-04 arrival | A slide |
| The last word | T-10 | A black tail, an outro animation |

### 9.3 How the moves breathe
- **The burn is the page turn.** It lands where the argument moves on: a new beat, a new kind of evidence, into or out of a
  full-bleed clip. Burn as often as the argument turns, and no more: two burns in quick succession read as a glitch, not a
  chapter, so let the evidence breathe between them.
- **The blur-in is the exhibit arriving.** Everything new arrives out of focus and snaps sharp; it's what makes each card
  feel examined rather than pasted.
- **The blur-through is the change of view.** It moves between layouts without a slide: the frame goes soft, the cut hides
  in the softness, the new view resolves.
- **The slide is the source opening.** It happens on the source word, once per item; inside an open split, cards change by
  cut or blur-in, never by another slide.
- Change the move from one boundary to the next unless the same meaning repeats; cuts sit on word boundaries ±1 f and the
  audio is never offset.

**The presenter's framing is locked by default** (v01 @1:29.2–1:33.3: < 3 % scale change in 4 s). That stillness is what
makes the one move in a stint mean something: a punch on the key word, or a slow push through the claim, never both, and
never two moves stacked (keep about 0.4 s between them). Change the kind of move from one stint to the next so it never
feels mechanical. No rotation on the presenter (measured ±0.5°, body sway only). The face stays inside the frame and the
plate band stays clear: in L-full a Z-2 at 1.20 must keep the chin above y 1180, else use 1.12.

**Plates ride the punch** (built in). A plate or chip on the presenter (P-DATE-PLATE, P-NAME-PLATE in L-full,
P-KEYWORD-CHIP, P-NUMBER-CHIP) that's on screen while a Z-2 punch plays sets `follow_footage: true`: it scales and moves
with the punch, as the date plate does with the 1.19 punch at v02 @2:09.4. Only while the punched plate stays inside the
safe band: its bottom after the punch, `face_cy + (plate_bottom − face_cy) × punch`, must stay ≤ 1500 and clear of the
face; otherwise leave the plate in screen space (or use the 1.12 punch). Never on cards in a split's graphic band (the band
doesn't move with the presenter), and never with `anchor`.

There is no canvas camera: maps pan inside their own scene.

| Tone | Role colour | Camera | Sound family |
|---|---|---|---|
| `claim` | `primary` | Z-1 push-drift (or one Z-2 on the key word) | whoosh |
| `evidence` | `bad` | none (the stage moves instead) | ui pop, digital text |
| `context` | `accent` | Z-2 punch-in | ui pop |
| `warn` | `bad` | Z-4 shake (on a stamp) | tension riser, low boom |
| `verdict` | `bad` (or `good`) | Z-4 shake | impact, stamp |

### 10.4 Layer order (back to front)
1. The world (W-evidence, W-data, W-map; the W-backdrop values feed P-BACKDROP)
2. z 3 scenes that sit **under** the presenter's window (full-bleed cards and clips under the ring or the rim card)
3. The footage window: the presenter footage → **P-BACKDROP** (`behind`, z 1, in footage space) → the cut-out
4. Stage lines: the split hairline (12 px rim)
5. z 4 evidence cards in a split's graphic band
6. z 5 plates, chips, pins, quote overlays
7. z 6 stamps, year chips, the CTA plate
8. Drawn by core, not scenes: P-FOCUS-PULL (footage blur on the stage), T-06 `leak` and T-11 `blur-through`
   (`timeline.transitions`, over the picture)

### 10.5 Finishing
- Grain only on the backdrop (noise 0.06) and the stages (0.05–0.06); the presenter is untouched.
- Vignette only in the worlds (0.3–0.45).
- Glow only on number chips (24 px), data values (16–28 px), the rim card (28 px), the stamp's fog and the question glyph.
- A soft shadow on cards (`0 30px 90px rgba(0,0,0,.55)`) and the ring (0.45).
- A soft matte shadow behind the cut-out is part of the original look (v01, v04), but the engine can't draw one yet:
  render **none**, and never fake it with a dark blob behind the head.

### 12.3 Props, the cut-out, resolution
- **Props:** none. The hands are the props: counting, pinching, pointing up at the panel.
- **Reaction bank:** none.
- **The cut-out:** required, except TH-room. Look at the hair edge closely; a fringe of the old background wider than 2 px
  means TH-room.
- **Resolution:** a 1080p source allows the Z-2 punch up to 1.25× (1.20 by default) on top of the base reframe; 4K allows
  1.35×. Screenshots under 1080 px wide are shown at most 940 px wide.

### 12.4 Frame rate and audio
30 fps CFR output (60 fps masters are conformed, not slowed). One voice track: high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, light compression,
−14 LUFS. The creator's clips keep their own sound under the rules of §11.

### 12.5 Third-party moments: fetch the real thing
This style is built on third-party material, so this runs on every reel. When the creator names a real article, report,
post, clip, person or place, the viewer should see the real one: that's the receipt.
1. **List the moments:** a headline, a post, a clip, a person, a chart, an event (`veos inserts scan` can start the list).
   One moment per cited source, per person shown, per clip, per map.
2. **The creator's own files first:** screenshots, clips and photos in their folder.
3. **Otherwise search the web and fetch it:** the real article page (captured and framed on the masthead, the date and
   the headline), the real post, a public photo of the person, a real map. Note where each came from.
4. **Use it as it is:** frame it with P-CITE-SHOT / P-CLIP-PANEL / P-PERSON-PHOTO; keep the masthead or the date visible;
   never alter what it says; a headline or post is shown word for word; blur private identifiers.
5. **Nothing usable to be found:** rebuild it from its exact words: P-CITE-CARD (a headline card), P-POST-CARD (a quote
   card), P-PERSON-SIL (a silhouette), the schematic map, P-RECON, a logo plate for a product or company (the name set in
   type). No labels, no credit lines.

A created card quotes only what the script or the real source states; no headline is invented to make a point. If the
script cites a source without its exact headline and the web doesn't turn it up, show a P-KEYWORD-CHIP with the outlet's
name instead of a headline card.

## §13 What your plan should settle

Your `ideas.md` is a page or two, for you. In this style it has decided, before any code:
1. **The case:** the verdict in one line, the evidence beats EB-1…EB-n, where each verdict lands.
2. **The theme pack** and why (the topic of the verdict).
3. **The hook:** the archetype and its variant, the first label (the plate, highlight or stamp text) and the frame it lands

   their word times, the origin (creator / fetched, with its source / created); unsupported claims flagged for the

5. **The plate list:** every spoken date, name, place and number → its plate, chip, card highlight or figure, with its
   word time.
6. **The figures** with recomputed values and shared scales; mismatches flagged.
7. **The stage per beat:** the layout, the split orientation, the ring's corner, every stage move with its `via`, frames
   and the word it lands on, every burn and blur-through in exact frames (§9).
8. **The tone of every line,** and so its camera move and role colour; the one move per stint.
9. **The evidence sources** (creator / fetched / created) and the fallbacks used.
10. **The sound:** the cue moments and the bed's entry.
11. **The CTA** (none, `cross_promo`, `comment_keyword`, `link_bio`) and its value.
12. **The moments you'll look at hardest on the storyboard:** f0 (the blur pulling open); the first split with its label, the head clear of
    the seam; one citation card mid-highlight; one ring read (the text clear of the ring); the data stage, if any; the

## §15 Your look at the storyboard: the checklist

Watch it once as a stranger with a thumb over the next reel, then once as the editor whose name is on it. Fix what
bothers you, in one pass.

**The style (does it feel like §The feel?)**
- No speech captions; the plates carry every spoken date, name, place and number.
- Frame 0 is the presenter alone (H9-A pulling open out of focus; H9-B and H9-C sharp and still); evidence readable by
  1.5 s, the first label by 2.0 s; the opening feels dense.
- Every claim is paid off in the same breath; every beat follows the ritual and opens on a new kind of proof; the
  strongest evidence comes last.
- Arrivals blur, hold and snap; burns turn the page; the split slides only on a source word; the camera is locked
  between moves, one move per stint at most.
- One theme pack for the verdict's topic; the rim only frames the presenter; red evidence, green verified, yellow "look
  here"; three bright hues at most.
- The stamps state what the body proved; the reel ends abruptly on the last line.
- Start to end: on every frame, everything on screen is the person making the case or the evidence for it.

**Craft (by eye, in context)**
- The creator's face reads whenever the moment is about them: plates at chest height in L-full, the face's band of a
  split free of text, nothing on the ring or the rim card, the stamp beside the face; nothing chops the head or buries
  the face by accident.
- No text over text by accident: one plate or chip on the presenter at a time; a stamp never across a plate.
- Every card, plate, chip, figure and stamp lands on its word; highlight bars travel with the speech; every split opens
  on its source word; cuts on word boundaries; no dead air beyond the breath before a verdict.
- Every source beat shows masthead, date and headline; headlines and quotes word for word; every highlighted span is in
  the text and spoken while highlighted; unsupported claims were flagged.
- Every compared number is a figure that recomputes, lands on its word and shares its scale; formats right ($ / K / M,
  or ₹ / lakh / crore); nothing that reads as evidence is made up.
- Names of people, outlets, places and laws spelt exactly; a spoken count equals the cases shown; every "I'll show you"
  is shown; a CTA keyword readable on screen.
- Every third-party moment is the real thing (the creator's or fetched, source noted) or rebuilt from its exact words;
  no outlet design imitated on a created card; private identifiers blurred.
- Plates and chips stay long enough to read; highlighted phrases and headlines big enough to read on a phone; meaning
  text clear of Instagram's buttons.
- The file itself (1080 × 1920, 30 fps, −14 LUFS, the bed under the voice, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word, no black
  tail) is the render's job; it checks it.
