# Monochrome Metaphor Style Playbook (template v2)

## The feel

This reel is a thought you watch. The frame is a black so pure it feels like closing your eyes, and in the middle of it
sits one thing made of light: an eye whose pupil is a ticking clock, a row of glowing dots being eaten one by one, a
hammer hanging over a single nail. It's already moving on the first frame, slow and certain, and it's already the idea.
A stranger stops because it's beautiful, before they know why it matters. Then the voice tells them, quietly, and the
object does exactly what the sentence says.

Nothing ever cuts. The clock becomes the pupil, the camera falls into the pupil, a lighthouse rises out of the dark it left
behind, then sinks back into its own ground and leaves its lamp glowing as one small dot. Every picture is born from the
last, so there's never a clean place to leave. The viewer isn't watching scenes; they're watching one idea change shape.
And that small white dot is them. It's "you" from the first frame: it gets eaten at, grows heavy with other people's noise,
hops through a feed, dims when the cost lands, and blazes into a light source on the turn.

Light is the only emotion. Dim is the cost, lit is the way out, and there's no colour to hide behind. The words whisper at
the bottom of the frame, small and plain, because the voice carries the argument and the picture carries the feeling.
Nothing punches, nothing shakes, nothing bounces. Everything moves like a slow exhale, and the few fast things (the flare
that opens on the thesis, the fall into a pupil) are brief, singular and earned.

It breathes with the essay: a steady glow through the clauses, the darkest frames on the cost, the brightest moment on the
turn, an ending that's lit, quiet and complete. In the seated version the same restraint holds: a black card of white
serif words, one cut, then one still take with small gold words on the chest.

**The test:** pause on any frame; if it isn't one lit thing in the dark that *is* the sentence being spoken (in the seated version, the face), it's wrong.

## What this playbook is

You're editing a calm essay: one idea, told in under a minute, either as a voice-over that becomes one evolving glowing
metaphor (F-B, the default) or as a creator seated on camera (F-A). You have the authority to make it the most beautiful,
most quietly gripping reel in their niche. This playbook is the style, pulled from three of Dan Koe's reels (v01 the seated
essay, v02 and v03 metaphor animations), audited at full resolution and measured frame by frame. Read it all, every time;
take what fits this reel, invent where a moment needs more (in this style's language: line art, light, the dot), and never
break the feel above.

**Who it's for and what it needs.** Creators of reflective essays: work, money, time, attention, habits, identity, growth.
F-B needs only a voice-over of 25–55 s and its script: every frame is built, there's no footage and no cut-out. F-A needs
one continuous seated take of 30–50 s (no cut-out either). A product cover is optional and only feeds the end card.
Captions follow the creator's language (English, Hinglish or Hindi, set in their copy). Machine values live in
`tokens.json`; where this text gives a number tokens also holds, they agree.

## §1 Procedure: how to approach a reel in this style

You listen, plan, build and look at the storyboard yourself; the edit skill has the mechanics. This
style lives or dies on two craft steps: **casting the metaphor** (one noun becomes one object that performs the sentence)
and **designing the chain** (how each object becomes the next). Spend your thinking there.

**F-B (voice-over), the default**
1. **Listen to the voice.** A calm essay voice breathes between sentences; those breaths are where the morphs live. Hear
   which word each sentence leans on: that's the trigger word its object acts on.
2. **Find the essay's shape** (§7.1): `THESIS` (the first sentence), `CLAUSE-n` (each supporting clause), `COST`, `TURN`,
   `PAYOFF`, `CTA`. One unit is one sentence or one breath group of 2–5 s.
3. **Cast the metaphors (the craft step).** For every unit, one object from §8.3 (the lookup in §8.4 is vocabulary, not a
   decision table) or a new one built from the same line art and light. Write the object, its action and the word it acts
   on: "the gear starts turning on *work*". If an object needs a label to be understood, cast another.
4. **Feel the tone of each line:** `state` · `cost` · `turn` · `resolve` · `cta` (§4.2). The tone sets the light level and
   the camera, never a colour.
5. **Design the chain** (§7.6, §9). For every boundary, write how scene N leaves (`morph_out`) and how scene N+1 is born
   from it (`morph_in`), and the dot's state in each scene (§7.7). Decide the reel's voice: a lit-object chain (the v03
   model: morphs, push-throughs, sinks) or a screen-and-game chain (the v02 model: flickers, CRT collapses, flicker swaps).
6. **Write the hook** (§6): the f0 object, its first transformation and the thesis landing. For a split title, write 8–10
   candidates, pick by the stopper test, keep two alternates. Write the post title the same way.
7. **Plan the light and the camera** (§4.2, §10.3): the glow level per tone, every canvas-camera move with its node, the
   push-throughs, the drift on long holds.
8. **Get the real thing** for any third-party moment (§12.4): the creator's files first, else the web. Most essays have
   none. The product cover is the creator's own; without it the end card changes (§12.3).
9. **Plan the sound** (§11): sparse; the morph is the drama.

**F-A (seated), the branch**
1. **Find the cold-open boundary:** the end of the first sentence (or the first two short ones) that falls between 3.0 and
   6.0 s. That word boundary is the reel's only cut (T-CARD-CUT).
2. **Check that no caption chunk straddles it.** A chunk that starts on the card and ends on the face splits mid-phrase:
   move the boundary to the sentence end.
3. **Check the framing** of the take against §3.6 (head top, chin, the chest band for the gold captions) and decide the
   one-off reframe if it needs one.
4. **Decide the end:** an end card (§6.8) or nothing. There's no metaphor casting and no chain: the face is the object.

## §2 Hard rules: the craft and the style's never-list

**Craft, by eye** (judge it on the storyboard, in context, the way an editor does):
- **The voice and the picture stay in sync.** Each object's defining action (the gear turns, the beam opens, the X draws)
  starts 2 f before its trigger word and is complete within ±5 f of it. Captions lead 2 f and are never more than 0.15 s
  early.
- **Keep the person clear (F-A).** In F-A the creator is the object, so keep the face, the hair and the room above the
  head clear of every layer: the head region runs from the top of the frame's safe area down to the chin (§3.6). The
  framing that does it: the gold captions on the chest, about 360 px below the lowest chin line. Behind the person is fair
  game, text included (`behind: true`, which needs a cut-out this style doesn't make). A caption that drifts near the chin
  for a beat is a judgement call; a head chopped or a face buried by accident never is.
- **Nothing important hides under Instagram's buttons.** Meaning text stays inside x 64–1016, y 110–1500; the right 110 px
  between y 900 and 1540 hold no text. F-B captions live in their band, y 1420–1520 (cy 1470): never lower, because the
  reference reels put them at y ≈ 1543, inside the bottom UI.
- **No text over text.** Captions hide under end cards and while the split title is up; an in-object word sits inside its
  object, never under a caption.
- **Say what was said.** A count you draw equals the spoken number (40 hours = 40 ticks; past 120, draw "many", up to 120
  objects, and leave the number in the caption). A quote is word for word. Illustrations may be made-up but realistic (a
  generic phone of grey cards), with no label. Never present an invented dashboard, revenue or follower count as real.
- **Promise integrity.** If the voice promises a resource or a product, the end card shows it; the keyword on the keyword
  card is the spoken keyword and is readable ≥ 1.5 s.
- **Spelling.** Brand, tool and person names exact in captions and on cards (the creator's glossary).
- **Readable.** CS-B captions are 44 px under exception E3 (weight ≥ 500, contrast ≥ 7:1: `#F4F4F4` on black is 19:1, one
  line, ≤ 24 characters); labels 32–39 px only when `redundant: true`; display text ≥ 40 px. F-A captions (62 px, 68 px on
  the card) need no exception. On black, only `mist`, `paper`, `#F4F4F4` or `primary` carry text; `smoke` carries only the
  handle and the disclosure line. Text the canvas camera scales stays at or above its floor after the zoom.
- **Audio.** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP, the bed ≥ 18 dB under the voice, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last
  word, no black tail longer than 0.2 s.

**Never in this style:**
- Colour in F-B graphics: no blue glows, no gold objects, no tinted gradients, no brand colours on objects.
- A hard cut in F-B. Whip pans, RGB-split or datamosh glitches, film burns, light-leak PNGs, full-frame white flash frames.
  (The power flicker is not a glitch: it dims and blinks the object only, in greys, with no more than three blinks. The
  flare reveal is not a flash frame: it peaks at ≤ 40 % mean frame luminance.)
- Overshoot, bounce, elastic, squash-pop, shake, crash zooms, or any zoom on footage (F-A is a locked-off shot).
- Emoji, stickers, memes, comedy marks, labelled arrows, chips, pills, boxes behind captions. Comedy is off.
- Stock footage, stock 3D renders, AI-generated photos, glossy "productivity" clip art, brains with lightning, rocket ships,
  money rain, gym-bro silhouettes. Objects are line art, light shapes or lit 3D objects built in the renderer.
- Bold or coloured words inside captions; ALL CAPS captions; two-line captions.
- Two hero objects competing (a clock *and* a phone *and* a gear as equals). Supporting parts (the gear behind the eye) sit
  at ≤ 50 % opacity and belong to one object.
- A phone mock with app names, logos or a recognisable UI: it's an outline with grey cards.
- Numbers as data: axes, % cards, counters, figure cards. Quantity is a countable set of things; numbers live in the
  captions.
- A metaphor that needs a label to be understood.
- Text above the object zone during F-B body beats: the top 330 px stay empty except for the split title and the end-card
  line.
- In F-A: graphics over the face, lower-thirds, B-roll cutaways, jump cuts, a second angle, a bright or busy background.
- An outro louder than the voice, music with lyrics, a hype outro.

### 3.5 Safe zones and bands
- **Meaning text:** x 64–1016, y 110–1500. The right 110 px between y 900 and 1540 hold no text (Instagram's buttons).
- **The object zone (F-B):** x 96–984, y 330–1330, centre (540, 960). Objects may glow past it; their solid parts stay
  inside it. Objects are 35–55 % of the frame width (hard limits 15–80 %; measured: hammer 17 %, phone 42 %, globe 37 %,
  eye 39 %, dot field 57 %, progress bar 80 %).
- **The caption band:** F-B y 1420–1520 (cy 1470) is reserved: nothing brighter than `slate` sits in it while a caption
  shows, and bloom from objects is masked to ≤ 0.25 luminance there. F-A captions sit at cy 1372, on the chest.
- **The split-title band (the HA-12 alternate in F-B only):** line 1 at y 746, line 2 at y 1160, straddling the motif row
  at y 960.
- **The end-card line:** cy 230.

### 3.6 The person (F-A only)
F-B has no presenter: every frame is built. In F-A the creator is the object, so treat them that way.
- **Geometry from tokens.** L-seated is full frame (face 0.36). The setup puts the head top at y 220–300, the eyes at
  y 600–660 and the chin at y 880–960; the head region is everything from the top of the safe area (y 110) down to the
  chin, hair and the room above the head included. The chest band y 1320–1430 stays plain: that's where the CS-A captions
  sit (cy 1372), about 360 px below the lowest chin line (y 960). Keep the head region clear: the stillness of the face
  is the picture.
- **Presence.** The creator is on screen from the cut to the end; the only absences are the cold-open card (≤ 6.0 s) and an
  optional end card (≤ 4.0 s). They return from the card with one hard cut (G-1) and never leave mid-body.
- **Crop: none.** The take is used as shot, centred. If the creator's take frames the head top higher than y 180 or the
  chin lower than y 1000, reframe once by scaling ≤ 1.10× (a 1080p source allows it) for the whole take, never per
  sentence.
- **Behind the person** is fair game, text included (`behind: true`), but it needs a cut-out and this style never wants it:
  the stillness of the take is the point.

essay. The accent is the one colour a creator may brand; it must read ≥ 7:1 on `#0B0B0B`.

### 4.2 Meanings: light by tone
**The axis is brightness, not hue:** dark → lit is cost → way out, old → new, lost → found. White core = alive, grey
outline = present but inactive, slate fill = a surface, black = nothing. The accent means "the creator speaks to you
directly" (the F-A captions, a branded keyword); it never marks an object. Brand colours never appear (a product cover is
the creator's own image and keeps its colours on the end card only).

| Tone | Light | Camera |
|---|---|---|
| `state` | Objects at rest: `mist` outlines, white glow cores, glow 24 px | C-3 drift or none |
| `cost` | Dimmer: outlines fall to `smoke`, glow 0–8 px, the dot may turn `dim` (`bad` grey) | C-2 slow pull-out or none |
| `turn` | A light event: the dot brightens to `source` (glow 90 px), or a beam or the flare opens | C-4 zoom-through or C-1 push |
| `resolve` | Everything that stays is fully lit (`good` white); everything rejected is struck out in `smoke` | C-2 pull-out to show the whole |
| `cta` | The end card on `ash` with a rim light | none |

### 4.3 Rules
- F-B carries no hue at all; F-A carries one, the gold. Never more than two bright colours in a frame.
- Glow is white only: `drop-shadow(0 0 Npx rgba(255,255,255,a))` with N 8–40 px and a 0.25–0.6; a bloom halo of 20–60 px
  at about 40 % opacity on the hero object only (§10.5).
- Gradients are greyscale only: `lit_sphere` (radial at 0.35, 0.3: `#F2F2F2` → `#9A9A9A` → `#2A2A2A` → `#050505`, the
  hourglass bulbs), `beam` (`rgba(255,255,255,0.28)` → 0, cones and wedges), `ground` (radial `#2E2E2E` → `#0C0C0C` →
  `#000000`, 2D ground lines and horizons).
- Footage (F-A) is never tinted or regraded: the look (cool, low saturation, blacks about `#0B0B0B`, warm skin) comes from
  the set. Exposure and white balance only; the gold caption is the only colour added.

### 5.2 The headline element: P-SPLIT-TITLE

on-screen title (the thesis arrives in the captions). F-A has no headline element: the cold-open caption card is the
stopper.

| Life | Static; the object between the lines acts (the dots are eaten, the dot breathes) |
| Lifetime | The hook: 1.0–2.0 s (v02: 1.33 s) |
| Exit | **T-FLICKER off** (8 f: off, one 1 f flash back, off) together with the uneaten dots, leaving the lone motif dot (v02 @ 0:01.33–0:01.57); the captions take over |
| Scene | `kind: "title_card"`; captions hidden while it's up |

### 5.3 Caption profiles
Captions come from the caption engine; never hand-build them. `captions.default` is CS-B (F-B) or CS-A (F-A); `by_layout`
switches L-card to CS-A0 automatically.

## §6 Hook system

**The hook title, in this style's terms.** Any title on screen or in the post promises the viewer something: an outcome
they want, a curiosity gap, or who it's for ("How to go viral as a doctor creating content", not "Reels for Doctors"). It
needn't repeat the spoken words; it has to be true to what the reel delivers. Here the real headline is the **thesis
sentence**, spoken first and captioned (F-B) or carded (F-A), so the promise lives in the first sentence and in the first
object. The split title (HA-12 in F-B) and the post title follow the same rule: write 8–10 candidates from the formulas in
§6.7 and the proven shapes ("How to X as a Y", "Why your X isn't working", "The X nobody tells you", "Stop doing X", a
contrast), score them on outcome, curiosity, who it's for and brevity, check the best against the stopper test, pick, and
keep the next two as alternates.

### 6.1 The stopper test
| Check | F-B (HA-15) | F-A (HA-12) |
|---|---|---|
| **Thumbnail** at 25 % scale | One lit object or the dot and its glow fills ≥ 25 % of the frame width and reads as a *thing* (an eye, a lighthouse, a row of dots) | The white serif phrase on black reads at 25 % (68 px → 17 px) |
| **Muted** | The first caption chunk is on screen by 0.7 s and the thesis sentence is fully captioned by 3.0 s | The cold-open card shows the whole thesis sentence by 6.0 s |
| **Motion at f0** | A non-text object moves on f0 (an entrance, a spiral, a breathing glow, a drift) | The first phrase is already there; the card itself is still |
| **Read time** | The split title (HA-12 only) reads in ≤ 1.5 s (≤ 8 words) | Each card phrase holds ≥ 0.25 s per word |
| **Payoff by** | The thesis is spoken and captioned by 3.0 s; the first transformation of the object lands by 2.5 s | The thesis on the card by 6.0 s; the face by 6.0 s |

The first three seconds of F-B feel full of life without ever feeling busy: the swirl gathers, the dots snap into a clock,
the flare opens, the eye appears, the camera falls into the pupil. Each change arrives while the last one settles.

### 6.6 Thesis writing
The style has no banner: the headline is the thesis, and the split title is a compressed version of it.
- **Formula:** `[a quiet universal claim about the viewer's life] + [the cost or the hidden mechanism]`, second person,
  present tense, ≤ 12 spoken words; the split title is the same claim in ≤ 8 words, 2–5 per line.
- **Templates:** "You weren't born to ___." · "The fastest way to ___ is to ___." · "Your ___ is ___ in disguise." · "Life is
  a ___, but you aren't ___." · "Nobody ___ by ___." · "The reason you ___ is ___."
- **Case:** F-B captions sentence case; the F-A card and captions lowercase; the split title caps.
- **Banned:** hype words ("insane", "game-changer", "secret"), questions to the viewer ("Did you know…?"), numbers as the
  hook ("3 tips…"), emoji, exclamation marks, "POV:", "Stop scrolling".
- Write 8–10, pick by the stopper test (the object reads at 25 %, the thesis captioned by 3.0 s), keep two alternates.

### 6.7 Hook sound
The hook may carry one soft cue on f0 and one on the first push-through (§11). The bed (an ambient pad) runs from f0,
≥ 18 dB under the voice.

### 6.8 CTA and the end cards
One device, at the end; never a mid-reel CTA. The end card holds ≤ 4.0 s, captions are hidden for it, the camera is
still, and there's no black tail.

| `comment_keyword` | "Comment KEYWORD and I'll send you ___" | P-KEYWORD-CARD | 1.5–4.0 s | 0.3 s |
| `link_bio` | "It's linked in my bio" | P-HANDLE-CARD | 2.0–3.0 s | 0.3 s |
| `none` | — | The reel ends on the last object (or the dot alone) dimming over 12 f after the last word; the hard end follows ≤ 6 f later. The dot is the last thing on screen | — | — |

The three cards are specified in §8.3 (H) and their type in §5.4. The product card is v03's ending (v03 @ 0:21–0:25):
the line reads "NOW AVAILABLE" (+ "ON <STORE>" when the voice names it), the cover is the creator's own image

## §7 Structure and rhythm

### 7.1 Structure: the essay (both formats)
**Claim → clauses → cost → turn → payoff, in one breath.** The essay is one continuous thought, never a list.

| Unit | What it does | F-B light |
|---|---|---|
| `THESIS` | The claim and the hook (§6.2); over fast, so the first clause arrives while the claim is fresh | `state` |
| `CLAUSE-1…n` (2–5 clauses) | Each clause adds one image of the problem or the mechanism ("you stop living the same day… you change the places you go… the books you read"); together they're the body of the reel | `state` |
| `COST` | What it costs to stay, or what the change will cost ("it's going to be difficult and uncomfortable") | `cost` (dim) |
| `TURN` | The insight, the way out ("but if you're able to stick with it…") | `turn` (the light event) |
| `PAYOFF` | The closing line that completes the thesis ("…that is exactly how you change your life extremely fast") | `resolve` |
| `CTA` | §6.8, or nothing | `cta` |

### 7.2 Markers
None: spoken only. No numbers, chips, step badges or progress rails. The morph chain itself shows progress.

### 7.3 The clause ritual (every clause in F-B)
1. **Enter (12–24 f):** the clause object is born from the previous object (T-MORPH, T-PUSH-THROUGH, T-HANDOFF), landing
   2 f before its trigger word.
2. **Act (1.5–4.0 s):** the object performs the mechanism (a strike, a sweep, a hop, a fill) whenever the words give it a
   reason, and something drifts or breathes on every frame in between. A long scene earns its length with events: the
   8.8 s spotlight in v03 sweeps a wedge, lights a cluster, draws an X.
3. **The dot:** present in its declared state (§7.7), or deliberately absent because a lit object owns the frame.
4. **Hand off (12–24 f):** the object's shape, its centre or the dot becomes the next scene's starting point; the next
   caption chunk is already revealing.

F-A has no visual ritual: each caption chunk hard-swaps to the next over a still, locked-off take.

### 7.4 Open loops
- **The only loop is the thesis.** The payoff line completes or answers the first sentence, and in F-B the final object is
  the *lit* version of the opening object, or the dot as a source (P-DOT-SOURCE).
- No re-hooks: the reel is short and one thought long.
- **Bookend** (off by default): when the creator wants it, the last 1.5 s reuse the opening object, now lit.

### 7.5 Rhythm by feel
- **The curve is a light curve, not an energy curve.** Energy stays calm and level. The light glows steadily through the
  clauses, falls to its dimmest on the COST (outlines in `smoke`, glow ≤ 8 px, a slow pull-out), rises to the brightest
  event of the reel on the TURN (the dot becomes a source, a beam opens, or the flare, if you've saved it), and leaves only
  the chosen thing fully lit on the PAYOFF. Then the quiet end card. No comedy beats.
- **It never stops, and it never hurries.** The void ambient runs under every frame and the hero object drifts, breathes or
  turns; new things arrive when a word gives them a reason. The one true stillness is the lone dot held for a breath, and
  it reads as a held breath precisely because nothing else in the reel stops.
- **It follows the voice.** The breath between sentences is where a morph lives; the trigger word is where the object acts;
  a pause in the voice is carried by a drift or a slow action, never by a dead frame. A faster clause gets a quicker
  object; the COST gets slower motion and more dark.
- **One object at a time, and the next one comes from it.** Variety comes from the metaphors, never from a quota. A
  pattern repeats only as the chain's return (the opening object, lit, at the end). The dot may link any two objects.
- **The fast moments are rare because they're loud.** The flare, the push-through, the zoom-out roll: each is a gesture,
  spent on the word that deserves it.
- **F-A breathes with the take.** A chunk every second or two, hard swaps, nothing else moving but the creator; one
  deliberate pause (≤ 0.8 s) before the payoff line.
- For reference, measured on the three reels (a description, not a target): F-B scenes ran a median of about 4 s (v03
  2.4 s, v02 5.9 s), p90 about 8.5 s, shortest 0.75 s on hook beats; about 13 scenes a minute in v02 and 22 in v03;
  pixels changed on 89 % of v03's frames and 49 % of v02's (v03 is the busier model, v02 the slower one; both are the
  style). F-A: one cut in 38 s, a caption swap every 1.2–2.0 s, the card still for up to 1.6 s.

### 7.6 The morph chain (F-B)
- **Every scene has a parent and a child.** Each scene declares `morph_in` (its parent: the previous scene's object, its
  centre, or the dot) and `morph_out` (its child), as `extra: {morph_in, morph_out, motif_state}` on the scene, so the plan
  and the storyboard can read the chain.
- **Boundaries** are T-MORPH, T-PUSH-THROUGH, T-HANDOFF, T-SINK, T-FLICKER-SWAP or T-COLLAPSE-DOT. T-FLICKER and T-CRT-OFF
  are the screen voice: for screens, devices, games and "switching off" lines, or wherever no shape links two scenes.
  T-BLOOM-DISSOLVE is the last resort, when no shape, dot or flicker fits.
- **No orphan scene:** zero hard cuts (v02 and v03: no cuts in 75 s). A flicker through black is the only boundary without
  a shared shape, and it still never cuts mid-sentence: the caption carries across the black.
- **Shared-object rule:** parent and child overlap on screen for ≥ 10 f; declare `overlaps: ["<parent id>"]` on the child
  for that span.
- **One voice for the whole reel.** A lit-object chain (v03) morphs, pushes through and sinks, and flickers almost never. A
  screen-and-game chain (v02) lives on flickers, CRT collapses and flicker swaps. Mixing them blurs the reel's world.
- The chain is checked by eye: look at every boundary mid-move on the storyboard's move strips.

### 8.5 Data and truth
- There are no charts, counters or figure cards. A quantity is a countable set of objects whose count equals the spoken
  number; past 120, draw "many" (up to 120 objects) and never print the number on screen.
- This style prints no numbers, percentages or dates on its objects at all: not for truth's sake but because a number is
  loud, and this style whispers. A clock shows no specific time unless the script names one.
- Illustrations are free to be made-up but realistic (a generic feed of grey cards, a quiet quote), with no label. A
  quote or a number the creator says is shown exactly as said.

### 8.6 Assets
- Everything except the product cover and the seated take is built in the renderer.
- Device mocks in a metaphor are generic: an outline phone with grey cards; no app names, no logos, no recognisable UI.
- No stock, no AI imagery, no clip-art packs. Icons come from `fx.icon` or the bespoke recipes above.
- A named brand is a P-QUIET-PLATE: the real logo (the creator's file, else fetched from the web, source noted) in one
  grey, or the name set in type when none can be found (§12.4). Never in colour.

### 9.3 How the moves breathe
The morph is the backbone: most boundaries are one outline becoming the next, quietly, on a breath of the voice. The
push-through is a door into a new world of meaning, so it's a big gesture: follow one with a morph, a sink or a hand-off,
because a second fall in a row feels like falling, not thinking. Every push-through is a canvas-camera move, so it keeps
≥ 0.4 s from the camera move before it and after it. The flicker belongs to screens and games; in a lit-object reel it
almost never appears, because a shared shape almost always exists. The bloom dissolve is the admission that no shape linked
two ideas: if you reach for it often, recast the metaphors. The flare and the zoom-out roll are once-in-a-reel gestures by
nature: one is the reveal, the other answers a literal "zoom out". Change the kind of move from one boundary to the next so
the chain feels alive, never mechanical.

Transition map format (part of the plan):
```yaml
transitions:
  - {t: 0.00, id: HA-15, from: null, to: S1-swirl, note: "icon swirl breathing at f0"}
  - {t: 0.45, id: T-MORPH, from: S1-swirl, to: S2-eye-clock, frames: 15}
  - {t: 1.95, id: T-PUSH-THROUGH, from: S2-eye-clock, to: S3-beam, node: pupil, frames: 21, note: "12 f contraction + 9 f push", sfx: "soft whoosh"}
  - {t: 9.80, id: T-HANDOFF, from: S5-feed, to: S6-ladder, carrier: M-dot}
```

### 10.2 Footage camera
None to speak of: `zoom_policy: none`. F-A is a locked-off take: no punch-ins, push-ins, per-sentence crops, shake or

has no footage.

### 10.3 The canvas camera (F-B only)
The camera moves over the void (z1–6), never over the captions (z7) or end cards (z8). It's how the viewer leans in, steps
back and falls into an object; most of the time it barely moves.

| ID | Move | Scale | Frames | Ease | Use in this style |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C-1** | push | 1.0 → up to 1.6, to a node (`fill` 0.6) | 18–36 | inOut | leaning into the object on a `turn` beat; reading a small detail (the nail, the pupil) |

- Every C-4 target is a declared node (`canvas_nodes` or a scene's `nodes`) at the object's darkest centre (a pupil, a

- **≥ 0.4 s** between the end of one move and the start of the next (an engine limit that keeps motion smooth).
- Text scenes that ride the camera (P-SPLIT-TITLE, P-OBJECT-WORD) stay at or above their size floor after scale
  (`text_px` × zoom); end cards set `parallax: false`.
- Keep zoomed content out of the caption band (y 1420–1520), or hide the captions for that span.

1. z1 the W-void ambient (`fx.ambient` void, continuous); in F-A, W-card or the footage.

### 10.5 Finishing
- **Bloom** on the hero object and the dot only: `drop-shadow(0 0 Npx rgba(255,255,255,a))` with N 8–40 px and a .25–.6,

- **Noise** 0 on W-void (the void is flat `#000000`); grain on lit objects only through their gradient stops (the
  hourglass sand, the lighthouse).
- **Vignette** 0. The haze around lit objects is their own bloom: v03's lit-tower and end-card scenes lift the surround to
  a soft grey glow (frame mean luma 15–35) from that bloom, not from a vignette.
- **Light cones** alpha ≤ .28; one cone or wedge per frame.

Calm and sparse: the morph is the drama, and sound only marks a landing. If in doubt, leave it out. (Sound isn't
observable in the reference reels, so this is a decision, not a measurement.) Cues come from the bundled pack at its calm

## §12 Footage handling

### 12.1 The voice (F-B)
A quiet room, a close mic (15–25 cm), a calm, low, unhurried delivery at 130–155 words a minute, with natural breaths
between sentences (they become the chain's morphs). One take, or comped takes with matched tone; WAV 48 kHz. Pauses are
kept as spoken, they're the breath of the essay, but trim any pause past 1.2 s, and through any pause of 0.6 s or more an
object action or a camera drift keeps going.

### 12.2 The seated take (F-A, setup A, from v01)
- Camera: tripod, eye level, front-on, 9:16 (4K or 1080p), 50–85 mm equivalent, shallow depth of field.
- Framing: medium close-up; head top y 220–300, eyes y 600–660, chin y 880–960, hands entering from the bottom and free
  to gesture; the chest band y 1320–1430 stays plain (the gold captions sit there).
- Background: a dark, low-detail wall (slate grey-green, about `#3A4A4A`) with two soft vertical light strips at the
  frame edges (x ≈ 8 % and 92 %), or any dark, plain wall.
- Light: a soft frontal key, low contrast, no coloured practicals.
- Wardrobe: a plain black or charcoal tee (no text across the chest band; a small logo at chest left is fine).
- Frame rate: 24, 25 or 30 fps, conformed to 30 fps CFR (v01 was 24 fps: conform, never speed-change).
- Mic: off-frame (a hidden lav or a boom).
- Gaps: tighten the dead air between sentences; keep one deliberate pause (≤ 0.8 s) before the payoff line.
- No props, no reaction bank, no cut-out. A 1080p take is enough: it's never punched in (the one-off reframe ≤ 1.10× in
  §3.6 is the only scaling).

### 12.3 Fallbacks and what you build without files
- Every lit object is an engine 3D object (B-4): no files needed.
- No product cover (a PNG/JPG ≥ 1000 px tall, the creator's own)? The end card becomes P-KEYWORD-CARD or P-HANDLE-CARD, or
  there's none.
- No seated take? The reel is made in F-B.

### 12.4 Third-party moments: fetch the real thing
When the voice names a real post, person, headline, product, app, another creator's clip or a book that isn't the
creator's, the viewer should see the real one, in this style's greys.
1. The creator's own files in their folder come first.
2. Otherwise search the web and fetch it: the real post, the real logo, the real page (captured and framed on the part
   that matters). Note where it came from.
3. Show it inside the style: on an `ash` card (radius 24), desaturated to greyscale
   (`filter: grayscale(1) contrast(1.05)`), centred in the object zone, entering by T-FOCUS-PULL, never altered to say
   something it doesn't; a post or headline word for word. A product cover the creator owns keeps its colours, on the end
   card only.
4. **Nothing usable to be found: rebuild it** from its exact words: P-QUIET-QUOTE (`fx.quoteCard`, dark, greys, word for
   word), P-QUIET-PLATE (`fx.logoPlate`, the name set in type), P-QUIET-SCREEN (`fx.appUI`, dark, generic), or a
   silhouette (`fx.silhouette`, greys) for a person. No labels, no credit lines.

Most essays have **no** third-party moment. When one appears, prefer recasting it as a metaphor (a quoted crowd becomes
P-EMITTERS-CONVERGE with the words in the captions) unless the exact words of a named person matter.

### 12.5 Frame rate and audio
30 fps CFR output, 1080 × 1920. The voice chain: a high-pass at 80 Hz, a gentle de-ess, light compression (2:1), −14 LUFS.

## §13 What your plan should settle

Your `ideas.md` is a page or two, for you. In this style it has decided, before any code:
1. **The format** (F-B or F-A) and **the thesis**, word for word, with the hook archetype (HA-15, HA-12 or HA-14).
2. **The essay map:** every unit (THESIS, CLAUSE-n, COST, TURN, PAYOFF, CTA) with its span, its tone and its trigger word.
3. **The casting (F-B):** for every unit, the object, what it does, the word it does it on, its size and place in the
   object zone; any new object written as a recipe in this style's line art and light.
4. **The chain table (F-B):** scene id · pattern · t_in–t_out · `morph_in` (the transition and the parent) · `morph_out` ·
   the dot's state; and the reel's voice (lit-object or screen-and-game).
5. **The light plan:** the glow per tone, the dimmest frame on the COST, the light event on the TURN, what stays lit at
   the PAYOFF, and where (if anywhere) the flare is spent.
6. **The camera list (F-B):** the `canvas_nodes`, every move (C-…, its target, scale, frames, ease), every push-through's
   node.
7. **The F-A specifics:** the cold-open boundary (its exact word and time), the card's phrases, the framing check and any
   reframe.
8. **The end:** the CTA device, its words, the asset, the hold; or how the last object dims.
9. **The sound:** the few cues and the bed.
10. **The moments you'll look at hardest on the storyboard.** F-B: f0, 1.5 s, the first push-through landing, one body
    beat per tone (state, cost, turn), **every chain boundary mid-move** (the move strips show them), the end card. F-A:
    the f0 card, the cut frame, one body frame (the top of the head clear, the captions on the chest), the end card.

Beats carry this style's own fields: `extra: {morph_in, morph_out, motif_state}` on every F-B scene, `overlaps` on a child
during its ≥ 10 f with its parent, `camera_canvas` on every canvas move, `exception: "E4"` on an ambient field,
`insert` on every third-party moment, `sponsor` on a paid mention. One F-B beat, for the shape:
```yaml
- id: 4
  section: CLAUSE-2
  t0: 9.80
  t1: 13.40
  spoken: "and you numb your mind with endless scrolling"
  trigger: {word: "scrolling", at: 12.62}
  tone: cost
  layout: L-void
  pattern: P-FEED-PHONE
  visual: "The dot drops into a grey outline phone; cards scroll; the dot hops card to card and the outlines dim to smoke on 'numb'"
  layers: [void, s4-phone, s4-dot]
  morph_in: {t: T-MORPH, from: s3-tower, note: "the tower's outline narrows into the phone frame"}
  morph_out: {t: T-HANDOFF, to: s5-hourglass, carrier: M-dot}
  motif_state: idle
  camera_canvas: {move: C-3, to: {by: {x: 0, y: -30}}, frames: 90}
  caption: {profile: CS-B, overrides: []}
  sfx: []
```

## §15 Your look at the storyboard: the checklist

Watch it once as a stranger with a thumb over the next reel, then once as the editor whose name is on it. Fix what
bothers you, in one pass.

**The style (does it feel like §The feel?)**
- Frame 0: one beautiful moving object in the dark that is already the idea (F-B), or the still black card with the
  first phrase (F-A).
- Every frame: one hero object, centred, that *is* the sentence; nothing decorates; no object needs a label.
- The chain holds: watch every boundary on the move strips; each scene is born from the last (morph, push-through with its
  contraction first, sink, hand-off), and flickers stay object-level with ≤ 3 blinks. Zero hard cuts in F-B.
- The dot is "you": one lit dot per frame, in most scenes, its state matching the story (dim on the cost, a source on the
  turn).
- The light curve reads: dimmest on the COST, brightest on the TURN, the ending lit (the opening object's lit return, or
  the dot as a source).
- Monochrome: no hue in F-B graphics; the only warm note is the F-A gold.
- It never stops and never hurries: no overshoot, no shake; the one held-still beat is a breath long.
- Start to end, without sound: you followed the essay from the captions and felt it from the light.

**Craft (by eye, in context)**
- F-B: no presenter anywhere. F-A: the face, hair and the room above the head read clear; the gold captions on the
  chest, the chest band plain; the take never zoomed, punched or shaken; nothing chops the head by accident.
- Every object acts on its trigger word; captions keep pace with the voice; no F-A chunk straddles the cold-open cut;
  nothing lingers after its point.
- No text over text by accident: captions out of the way under end cards and while the split title is up.
- Meaning text clear of Instagram's buttons (§2); F-B captions in their band; text on black only in `mist`, `paper`,
  `#F4F4F4` or `primary`, readable on a phone, camera-scaled text above its floor.
- Every drawn count equals the spoken number; no number printed on an object; quotes and fetched posts word for word; no
  invented dashboards or results shown as real; every third-party moment the real thing in greys or a quiet rebuild.
- The CTA keyword on the card is the spoken keyword and readable; a promised product is the one on the card; a sponsor's
  disclosure held long enough to read.
- Names spelt exactly.
- The file itself (1080 × 1920, 30 fps, −14 LUFS, the bed under the voice, a hard end, no black tail) is the render's
  job; it checks it.
