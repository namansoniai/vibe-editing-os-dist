# Whiteboard Split Style Playbook (template v2)

## The feel

This reel is the smartest friend you have, at a whiteboard, turning round every few seconds to look you dead in the eye.
The top of the screen is a clean board where one idea draws itself exactly as it's said: a curve bending on "drops", a
node lighting on "step two", a counter ticking on the number. Under it, a single word of caption beats like a pulse. Below
that, the creator sits in a rounded window, head breaking over its top edge as if leaning into the room.

Then, on the claim, the board vanishes. A hard cut to their face, close, with nothing else on screen but one word. That
cut is the drama. No whooshes, no shakes, no stickers: the power is the switch itself. Explanation, then conviction.
Explanation, then conviction. Every opinion lands like a verdict because it gets the whole screen, and every return to
the board feels like turning a page, because the board has always moved on.

The board is never decoration. It's one diagram the viewer learns to read: built early, revisited at every step with the
part we're on lit and the rest dimmed, recapped at the end. A minute of talk feels like one idea unfolding, and the viewer
leaves able to draw it themselves. The edit practises what it teaches: every return is a small reveal, every punch a small
stakes moment, every numeral a reason to stay for the next one.

Colour stays quiet so the ideas can be loud: warm grey paper or a black stage, crimson only on the words that matter, neon
only on the node we're on. The headline reads like a confident sentence, with one italic-serif word that sounds like the
creator's own voice. Nothing waits: the diagram never lags the speaker, the speaker never waits for the diagram, and
nothing is on screen unless it was just said.

**The test:** pause on any frame and you know what's being taught and which part of it we're on.

## What this playbook is

You're editing one talking-head take of a creator teaching a framework, a before/after model or a step-by-step workflow,
and you have the authority to make it the clearest, most gripping lesson in their niche. This playbook is the style, pulled
from three of Kallaway's reels (v01 story arcs, v02 the addiction loop, v03 a tool workflow) and measured frame by frame.
Read it all, every time; take what fits the reel, invent where a moment needs more, and never break the feel above.

**Who it's for and what it needs.** Teachers, coaches and explainers whose content has parts: steps, models, mechanisms,
tools. One take is enough; a 4K landscape take is what makes the full-face punch sharp (§12). Diagrams, UI and documents
are built; real logos, posts and pages the creator names are fetched from the web when they didn't supply them (§12.4). Captions follow the creator's language (English, Hinglish or Hindi, set in their copy). The
person cut-out is optional and only draws the head over the window's edge. Machine values live in `tokens.json`; where
this text gives a number tokens also holds, they agree.

### Style directives (non-negotiable)
| # | Directive | Where it lives |

| D3 | **One idea per card.** A card evolves node by node while its idea lasts and is replaced when the idea changes; never two cards side by side | §8.2 |

## §1 Procedure: how to approach a reel in this style

You watch, listen, plan, build and look at the storyboard yourself; the edit skill has the mechanics. This style's
craft is two decisions: turning a talk into named parts the board can show, and deciding, word by word, when the viewer
sees the board and when they see the face.

1. **Extract the framework.** Write the reel's idea in one line: its name (a lockup), its parts (each a 1–3-word name), and
   the **motif shape** that carries it (loop, ring, ladder, road, axes or chain; §7.6). If the talk has no named parts,
   name them from the speaker's own words.
2. **Pick the structure** (§7.1): named parts → FW-1; a "used to / now" contrast → FW-2; a sequence of actions in a tool →
   FW-3.
3. **Feel the tone of each line:** `explain` · `hype` (a claim, an opinion) · `warn` (a mistake, the old way) · `awe` (a
   reveal) · `win` (the payoff) · `cta`. Claims and warnings want the face; explanations want the board.
4. **Write the hook** (§6): the lockup (8–10 candidates, the best by the stopper test, two alternates), the f0 card, the
   first punch word.
5. **Mark the punches and returns** on the words (§7.5): every punch on the first strong word of a claim, every return on
   the first word the board illustrates, with a new card state on that frame.
6. **Plan the worlds** (§3.1): paper for things on a screen or a page, stage for ideas; flips only at section starts.
7. **Plan the board per beat** (§8): the recurring diagram's states, a pattern for every term, a figure for every number,
   the third-party moments.
8. **Plan the sound** (§11): sparse; the cut is the drama.

## §2 Hard rules: the craft and the style's never-list

**Craft, by eye** (judge it on the storyboard, in context, the way an editor does):
- **Keep the person clear.** The face window, the full-face face and the head where it breaks over the window's top edge
  (the breakout strip y 1280–1400) are the creator's; the frame is built so nothing needs to sit there. The caption band
  (y 1178–1258) sits clear above that strip, and cards stay in their slot above the window. What's never fine is a head
  chopped or a face buried by accident.
- **No text over text.** One lockup, one label group and the caption is the most the screen ever holds; never two cards.
- **On the word.** Every card event, node, label, numeral and tool lands 2 f before its word and is fully on within ±5 f.
  Cuts sit on word starts; a full-face run never starts mid-word.
- **Say what was said.** Every number shown is spoken, scripted or the creator's own data (`plan/figures.json`); concept
  curves carry no axis numbers; illustrations may use made-up but realistic numbers, no label. Never a fake dashboard or a
  follower count presented as real.
- **Promise integrity.** A promised count ("4 steps") equals the steps shown (numerals 1…N, motif nodes N); the CTA keyword
  is on screen ≥ 2.0 s; the asset previewed is the asset promised.
- **Spelling.** Tool, brand and person names exact in captions and on cards.
- **Readable.** Captions ≥ 54 px; labels ≥ 40 px, or 28–39 px only when the same word is spoken or shown larger (E3:
  weight ≥ 600, contrast ≥ 7:1, ≥ 4.5:1 on their own white tag). Crimson text never sits on the black stage (2.8:1).
- **Audio.** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP, the bed ≥ 18 dB under the voice, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last
  word, no black tail.

**Never in this style:**
- Captions anywhere but their band: not on a card, not in the window, not at the top, never more than a word or two.
- A card in the full-face shot. The face run carries only the face and the one-word caption.
- A return to an unchanged card.
- Stock footage, brand-coloured icon packs, emoji, stickers, meme sounds, light leaks, film burns, glitches, whip pans of
  the footage (the in-band card smear, P-SMEAR-PUSH, is not one), spinning 3D logos.
- Gradients on cards, drop shadows on text in the card band, rounded "bubble" captions, caption boxes.
- More than one neon colour on a diagram state (white + one accent); more than three bright hues in a frame.
- Regrading the footage, slow motion, speed ramps.
- An end card after the CTA asset.

## §3 Worlds, layouts, stage moves, safe zones

### 3.1 Worlds
| ID | Kind | Look | Carries | Enter / exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **W-stage** | stage | `night` `#050505`; a faint white dot grid (pitch 36, alpha 0.05, r 1.2); a 900 px soft white glow at (540, 700), alpha 0.035; no noise, no vignette | Concepts, psychology, models, curves, loops, roads, numbered frameworks; the motif's neon | The default world. Flips in or out only on a split return at a section start (G-5) |
| **W-paper** | canvas | `canvas` `#E4E4E0` (measured `#E1E2DD`–`#E4E4E4`); `grid` `#DADAD6` lines (faint), pitch 40, 1.5 px, alpha 0.9, fading out toward the frame edges: world `grid.fade {x: 540, y: 770, inner: 0.1, outer: 0.3, min: 0}` | Tools, apps, documents, profiles, workflows: anything that is a screen or a page; the CTA asset | Same |

**Which world.** A section about *a thing on a screen* (an app, a site, a document, a profile, a prompt) is paper. A
section about *an idea* (a model, a curve, a habit, a mechanism) is stage. The hook takes the world of its first card. A
flip is a section break: only on a split return at a section start, never inside a unit, and rare enough that each one
feels like a new chapter. The CTA asset takes paper for a document or tool, stage for a video.

Text flips with the world: ink `#0B0B0B` on paper, paper `#FFFFFF` on stage (lockups, labels and the CS-1 caption).

### 3.2 Layouts: the three bands
| ID | Engine | The creator | The board | Caption |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L-split** | `card` | A rounded window x 64, y 1400, w 952, h 560 (it bleeds 40 px off the bottom, so only the top corners show), radius 30, crop 16:9, face 0.33, eye 0.16 (the face sits high, so the head rises over the window's top edge), `breakout: 120` (the cut-out draws the head up to y 1280), shadow 0.25, no border | x 64–1016, y 150–1160: the headline slot y 150–360, the card slot y 380–1160 | CS-1, cy 1218 |
| **L-full** | `card` | Full frame x 0, y 0, w 1080, h 1920, radius 0, crop 9:16, face 0.26, eye 0.36 | none | CS-2, cy 1160 |
| **L-clip** | `hidden` | none: a full-bleed clip scene (z4) fills the frame | the whole frame | CS-2, cy 1160 |

The reel opens on L-split (frame 0) and ends on L-split (the CTA asset).

### 3.3 The frame template (the slots)
In L-split the frame never changes; only what's in the slots changes. In L-full and L-clip the whole template steps aside
and returns unchanged.

| Slot | Rect (x, y, w, h) | z | Holds | Enters | Swaps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S-HEAD** | 64, 150, 952, 210 | 5 | the lockup (LK-1…LK-5) or a step name | blur build, 6 f per word | P-TITLE-SWAP (4 + 6 f) |
| **S-CARD** | 64, 380, 952, 780 | 3 | a diagram, the motif, a recreated UI, a doc card, a clip card, a tile grid, a counter card, a logo plate | P-CARD-SCALE-IN, 8 f | evolve (T-05), or a new card (T-03 / T-10) |
| **S-CAP** | 64, 1178, 952, 80 (cy 1218) | 7 | the one-word caption (CS-1; in L-full CS-2 moves to cy 1160) | hard | hard |
| **S-WIN** | 64, 1400, 952, 520 (bleeds off the bottom) | 4 | the face (footage) | cut | cut |

- Slot rects hold steady (within ±4 px) for their whole life; slots never overlap.
- Card content stays inside S-CARD (bottom ≤ 1160), clear of the caption band that starts at y 1178.
- The only content outside S-CARD is an in-band push (P-DIAGRAM-PUSH, P-MOTIF-VISIT), clipped to x 0–1080, y 110–1170;
  the lockup is hidden while it runs.
- A hero numeral and its step name live inside S-CARD, beside the node, never in S-HEAD.

### 3.4 Stage moves
There are no animated window moves (no shrink, no slide): every mode change is a hard cut (`stage_morphs: {cut: 0}`).

| ID | Move | Recipe (30 fps) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G-1** | **Punch cut** | `stage {layout: L-full, via: cut}` (0 f). No camera move on the cut: a static crop at face 0.26. Captions switch CS-1 → CS-2 on the cut frame. Inside a longer run, a mid-run jump cut may step tighter (`punch-in`) or the run may drift (`push-drift`) (§10.2) | Every claim |
| **G-2** | **Return cut** | `stage {layout: L-split, via: cut}` (0 f), with a card event on the same frame (a title swap starts, the next node pops, a new card scales in) | Back to the explanation |
| **G-3** | **Clip in** | `stage {layout: L-clip, via: cut}` + the full-bleed clip scene (z4) starting on the same frame, already playing (no fade) | The example moment (P-CLIP-INTERCUT) |
| **G-4** | **Clip out** | `via: cut` to L-full (the reaction; preferred) or to L-split with a new card | After the example |
| **G-5** | **World flip** | On a G-2 return frame, `world {world: W-paper or W-stage}` with `fade: 0`; the card is new anyway | A section start whose subject changes kind |

### 3.6 Safe zones and bands
- Meaning text stays inside x 64–1016, y 110–1500; nothing in the right 110 px between y 900 and 1540 except the face
  window (no text there).
- **S-HEAD** y 150–360 (the lockup's top at y 160); **S-CARD** y 380–1160; **the caption band** y 1178–1258 (CS-1 cy 1218);
  **the head's breakout strip** y 1280–1400; **the window** from y 1400.
- In L-full the caption sits at cy 1160 (CS-2), on the chest; nothing else is placed.
- A pushed diagram (P-DIAGRAM-PUSH) is clipped to x 0–1080, y 110–1170; its remaining text stays inside x 64–1016.

### 3.7 The person
- **A face is always there.** In the window or full frame, nearly the whole reel; the only absences are short full-bleed
  example clips (L-clip), and the face comes back by a hard cut.
- **The window crop (L-split):** face 0.33 of the window's height, eye 0.16: the face sits high, so the head rises over the
  window's top edge (about 50 px in a typical take), and the breakout (`breakout: 120`, the matted cut-out) draws it over
  the edge, as in every reference split. The caption band ends at y 1258, 22 px above the highest point the head can reach
  (y 1280). Keep that strip clear; it's where the creator leans into the room.
- **The full crop (L-full):** face 0.26 (a face box ≈ 500 px tall), eyes at y ≈ 690, the chin at ≈ 940; the caption at
  1160 lands on the chest.
- **Returns** are cuts; the creator never slides or shrinks.

## §4 Colour

### 4.1 Role palette
| Role | Hex | One job | Text on it | Contrast |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `primary` crimson | `#B0122A` | The key phrase: coloured lockup words (paper only), the 4 px underline, the key slab, the CTA deliverable line, count chips on paper | `paper` | 7.1:1 (white on it); 5.5:1 as text on `#E4E4E0` |
| `accent` neon signal | `#FF2D55` | The recurring motif and the lit node or segment on the stage; its glow | `ink` | 5.4:1 vs `#050505` |
| `data` magenta | `#D61FD0` | The reading: the viewer's or measured curve, the overlay series, the area fill | `ink` | 4.7:1 vs `#050505` |
| `highlight` yellow | `#FFE000` | The threshold: segment bars under an axis, the highlighted page, row or line | `ink` | 15:1 |
| `bad` red | `#E0102A` | The old way, the failing curve, the drop | `paper` | 4.9:1 |
| `good` green | `#2BD96B` | The new way, the working result, the success outline | `ink` | 11:1 |
| `mute` grey | `#8A8A8A` | Inactive nodes, the ghost serif line, axis titles, grey bevel chips | `ink` | — |
| `ink` | `#0B0B0B` | Text and strokes on paper; CS-1 on paper | — | — |
| `paper` | `#FFFFFF` | Text and lines on stage; node dots; CS-1 on stage; CS-2 | — | — |
| `canvas` / `grid` | `#E4E4E0` / `#DADAD6` | The paper world | — | — |
| `night` | `#050505` | The stage world | — | — |

### 4.2 Meanings
- **Crimson = the words that matter** (the lockup's key phrase, the deliverable). Never a diagram colour.
- **Neon accent = the part of the diagram we're on.** Exactly one lit thing at a time.
- **Magenta = the reading** (what the data actually does, the viewer's own curve), against a white or red model curve.
- **Red → green = old → new**, the before/after of every comparison.
- **Yellow = the threshold** (the window that decides: the first 3 seconds, the line that matters).
- A tool's own colours appear only on its logo plate (its real logo in a neutral tile).

### 4.3 Rules
- At most three bright hues in a frame (crimson, accent, data, highlight, bad, good); the typical frame is a crimson
  underline + one accent + one of data / bad / good.
- Crimson text only on paper. On the stage the key phrase is paper-white with a crimson underline or a crimson slab.
- Coloured diagram lines on paper are darkened to ≥ 3:1 against `#E4E4E0` (accent → `#D81B45`, data → `#B514AE`, good →
  `#12A150`); text labels stay ink.
- Glow (canvas `shadowBlur` 20–28 px) only on the stage, only on the lit element and the neon lines.
- Footage is never graded; only exposure and white balance are matched if the take has two segments.

### 5.3 The captions: one word at a time
Two profiles, chosen by layout (`captions.by_layout: {L-full: CS-2, L-clip: CS-2}`; CS-1 otherwise). Both extend
`lib:kallaway`.

| | CS-1 "split word" | CS-2 "face word" |
|---|---|---|
| Chunking | One word per chunk (two only when the engine glues a name or number, or two very short words: "The reason", "of a"), max 18 characters, 1 line; never split a name, number or unit; punctuation kept, and it breaks the chunk; a pause ≥ 0.9 s clears the caption | Same, max 16 characters |
| Timing | Lead 1 f; hold ≥ 0.25 s per word; tail 0.12 s; **hard swap** (0 f: the next word replaces the last on its frame, no pop, no fade; every burst of the three reels) | Same |

## §6 Hook system

**The hook title** promises the viewer something: an outcome they want, a curiosity gap, or who it's for ("How to go viral
as a doctor creating content", never the label "Reels for Doctors"). It needn't repeat the spoken words; it must be true
to what the reel delivers. In this style the title is the lockup: its shape is fixed (§5.2), its voice is yours to make
irresistible.

### 6.1 The stopper test
1. **Thumbnail:** frame 0 at 25 % scale shows a recognisable card skeleton and the face window; from 0.5 s the lockup
   reads (L2 ≥ 60 px is ≥ 15 px at that scale), so the cover frame comes from about 1.5 s.
2. **Mute:** the first 3 s tell the topic: the lockup, the card's reading and the one-word captions.
3. **Motion at f0:** the card skeleton is scaling in or a line is drawing on frame 0; the window is live.
4. **One read:** the lockup reads in ≤ 2.0 s.
5. **Payoff:** the diagram or the proof is readable by 2.5 s.

The opening feels quick and certain: the board fills on the nouns, one decisive event lands, and the first cut to the face
arrives on the first claim, a couple of seconds in.

### 6.2 HA-06 Framework build (default)
| t (s) | Beat | Layout / world | Visual | Caption | Camera | Sound |

## §7 Structure and rhythm

### 7.1 Structure: a framework, three ways
| Variant | Arc | The recurring diagram (§7.6) | Units | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FW-1 Numbered framework** | the promise → the model built → step 1…N (each a visit) → recap → CTA | The motif (loop, ring, ladder) with N nodes | `STEP-1…N`, 3–5 steps | v02 (the 4-step addiction loop) |
| **FW-2 Old vs new** | the problem → the old model (named) → "but" → the new model on the **same axes** → the part that matters (the threshold) → CTA | The shared axes: the old curve, then the new curve, then the threshold bars | `OLD`, `TURN`, `NEW`, `DETAIL` | v01 (the traditional vs the modern story arc) |
| **FW-3 Workflow** | the result → station 1 (do this) → station 2 (get this) → station 3 (connect this) → the result again → CTA | The station chain (3–4 stations on a line; the active one lit) | `STATION-1…N`, 3–4 stations | v03 (paste a URL → the analysis → install) |

### 7.2 Markers
- **SM-NUM** (FW-1): the hero numeral + a 2-line step name beside the lit motif node (§5.4), on the step word ±2 f,
  counting up, at **every** step.
- **SM-STATION** (FW-3): the station's number in a 64 px circle on the chain + its LK-4 title.
- FW-2 has no numerals: the lockup names the model ("Traditional *Story Arc*" → "Modern *Story Arc*").
- **The recap** (FW-1, FW-3): P-MOTIF-RECAP, all N labels around the motif, before the CTA. No teaser chips.

### 7.3 The unit ritual (the same at every step; frames at 30 fps from the step word, f0)
| Frames | What happens |
|---|---|
| f−2…f24 | **P-MOTIF-VISIT** in L-split: the whole band pans to node k (400–700 px, expo ease-out: most of the travel in 8 f, then a 16 f settle drift; the caption hides during a silent pan); node k−1 dims to 35 % (6 f); node k lights in `accent` with a 28 px glow at f12 |
| f0…f10 | **P-HERO-NUMERAL:** numeral k fades grey → white (6 f) already placed beside the node and travels with the pan, landing on the step-name word; the two name lines stagger 3 f. The list cue sounds on the landing frame |
| +1.2…2.0 s | **P-TITLE-SWAP** to the step's LK-4 lockup; the step's explainer card scales in (P-CARD-SCALE-IN, 8 f) in place of the motif |
| the next stretch | The explainer evolves on its nouns (§8.4) |
| on the claim word | **P-FULL-PUNCH** |
| on the example word | **P-SPLIT-RETURN** with a new card state, or **P-CLIP-INTERCUT** |
| the next step word | back to the first row |

FW-2's ritual per model: P-AXES-BUILD (once) → the curve draws → a callout per term → the punch on "but" → P-ARC-SWAP on the
return. FW-3's per station: the chain slides to station k (12 f), station k lights, then the tool card (P-CHAT-TYPE /
P-RESULT-SECTIONS / P-SETTINGS-LIST) takes the card slot.

The ritual is the one place repetition is the point: the viewer learns it and feels the count go up.

### 7.4 Open loops
- **The count loop:** FW-1 shows N empty nodes at the build; the viewer watches them fill, and the numerals match.
- **The model loop:** FW-2 names the old model first and holds the new one back until after the "but" punch.
- **Re-hooks:** every motif visit with its hero numeral is a re-hook. FW-2 and FW-3 plant one in the middle stretch: an
  LK-3 key-slab lockup ("This Is The Part Nobody Shows") or the example clip (P-CLIP-INTERCUT).
- **The payoff rule:** every promised part, model and deliverable appears on screen before the CTA.

### 7.5 Rhythm: the punch
- **Two modes, one pulse.** The board explains; the face convinces. **Punch** (L-split → L-full) on the first strong word
  of a claim: *is, because, but, instead, never, don't, the reason, actually, now, then*, or a number. **Return**
  (L-full → L-split) on the first word the board illustrates (a noun, a step name, "here's", "look"), and the board shows a
  new state on that same frame: a new card, a title swap, the next node.
- **A face run lasts as long as the claim.** Short and sharp: a lone "But." can be under a second; a full opinion a few
  seconds; never long enough to sit. Two face runs are separated by real board time (an example clip may sit between
  them).
- **The board holds as long as it keeps moving.** While the split is up, something on the card is always arriving; when
  the card has nothing new to give, it's time for the face.
- **It follows the speech.** Lists move fast; a deliberate breath before a punch line can land it; no dead air anywhere
  else. The caption keeps the pulse underneath it all, a word every third of a second.
- **The energy curve:** the hook (fast: the first punch within a few seconds) → the model build (steady, explain) → each
  step (a punch on its claim) → the last step escalates (the longest motif push, the example clip) → the recap (calm, all
  lit) → the CTA (clean; no punch once it starts).
- For reference, measured on the three reels (a description, not a target): face runs 0.83–4.38 s (median 2.2), split
  runs 1.75–17.7 s (median 5.5); v01 cut to the face 9 times in 74 s, v02 6 times in 108 s, v03 8 times in 63 s. v02, the
  reel built on one long motif, punches least and holds the board longest.
- No comedy beats.

### 7.6 The recurring diagram
One per reel, chosen when you extract the framework:

| Shape | Variant | Nodes | On the stage | On paper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Loop** (infinity) | FW-1, 4 parts | 4 numbered nodes on the crossings and lobes | an `accent` neon tube (14 px, glow 24), white numerals | an ink tube 10 px, a `primary` lit node |
| **Ring** | FW-1, 3–5 parts | N nodes evenly on a circle (r 280) | same | same |
| **Ladder** | FW-1, steps that build on each other | N rungs, bottom → top | same | same |
| **Road** | FW-1/FW-2 with a twist | 2 paths, A/B points | `mute` dashed + `good` glow | ink dashed + `good` |
| **Chain** | FW-3 | 3–4 stations (Ø 64 circles, numbered) on a line | a white line, an `accent` lit station | an ink line, a `primary` lit station |
| **Axes** | FW-2 | the old curve, the new curve, the threshold bars | white axes, curves per §4 | ink axes, darkened curves |

**Its life:** `build` (P-MOTIF-BUILD, early, while the hook's promise is fresh) → `idle` (smaller, under a lockup) →
`visit:k` (P-MOTIF-VISIT; node k lit in the accent with a 28 px glow, the others at 35 %) → `recap` (P-MOTIF-RECAP, all
lit).
- It's drawn at the same world position and size every time it returns (`same_position`), so a visit is a pan to a place
  the viewer already knows.
- It appears at the build, at every step (one visit per step in FW-1), and at the recap.
- Exactly one node is lit at a time, except in the recap.
- Hand-offs are hard (cut returns, title swaps); no morph chain, no bookend.
- Beats carry `motif_state`; visits carry `rehook: true`.

### 8.5 Data and truth
- Every number on screen is a figure in `plan/figures.json`:

| Figure kind | Used by | Provenance | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `counter` (a series) | P-COUNTER-TICK | `creator` (the start and end numbers they say, show or wrote in the brief) or `script` | Steps every 5 f between the stated values; the first stated value lands within ±5 f of its spoken number; an E6 slot |
| `hero_number` / stat chip | P-STAT-CHIP, P-CURVE-OVERLAY chips | `spoken@t` or `creator` | Written by `ctx.fmtNum` in the copy's format ("10K", "4%", "$1.2M") |
| `line` (illustrative) | P-CURVE-DRAW, P-ARC-SWAP, P-MINI-FRAMES, P-AREA-FILL | none | `illustrative: true`: no axis numbers, a concept label (the lockup or the axis titles), no other label |
| `bar` (an illustrative threshold) | P-THRESHOLD-BAR | the spoken window ("first 3 seconds", "14 days") | Integers up to 12 may be shown as words; larger ones need a figure |

- Formulas: `sum`, `diff`, `ratio`, `percent_change`, `per_period`. Compared curves share one `scale_id`: every comparison
  (P-ARC-SWAP, P-CURVE-OVERLAY, P-MINI-FRAMES) on the same axes.
- A recreated UI that stands in for a real product shows only the script's words.

```json
{"inputs": {"start": {"value": 2000, "from": "creator"}, "end": {"value": 19000, "from": "creator"}},
 "figures": [{"id": "followers", "kind": "counter", "formula": "none", "format": {"compact": "k_m_b", "suffix": " followers"},
   "steps": [{"value": 2000, "at": 1.70}, {"value": 5000, "at": 1.87}, {"value": 8000, "at": 2.03}, {"value": 12000, "at": 2.20},
             {"value": 15000, "at": 2.37}, {"value": 18000, "at": 2.53}, {"value": 19000, "at": 2.70}]}]}
```

### 8.6 Annotations live inside the card
The hand-drawn arrows and outline boxes (the "HOOK" curl, the "10K followers" box) are drawn **inside** their card scene
(P-THRESHOLD-BAR, P-HAND-LABELS, P-CLIP-TAG), never as a free ink layer over the footage. No comedy layer: no stickers,
stamps, freezes or meme sounds.

### 8.7 Assets
- Real captures first: the creator's own screenshots, reels and data (SH-2, SH-3).
- Mocks are generic and unbranded (never a look-alike of a real app); products appear as logo plates: the real logo (the
  creator's file, else fetched from the web) in a neutral rounded tile, the name set in `display` only when none can be
  found.
- No stock footage, no stock 3D, no AI-generated scenes. Line-art objects come from `fx.icon` or are drawn in SVG.
- Third-party moments: fetch the real thing, source noted (§12.4).

### 10.2 Footage camera (`zoom_policy: crop_on_cut`)
| ID | Preset | Recipe | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | (the entry) | No move: the L-full entry is a static crop (face 0.26; scale 1.00 over the first frames, measured v01, v02) | Every punch |
| **Z-2** | `push-drift` | 1.00 → 1.05, linear, over the whole L-full run | Now and then (v02), never on two runs in a row |
| **Z-3** | `punch-in` | 1.00 → 1.15 (measured 1.08–1.22) in 1 f on a jump cut, held to the run's end | A mid-run word in a longer face run; about half of those runs in the reference (v01, v03) |

The camera moves only on a mode cut or a jump cut, and never in L-split (the window stays still) or L-clip. The two
presets take turns so the face never feels mechanical.

### 10.3 The in-band camera
The reference reels move a camera over their diagrams, but the move stays inside the card band while the caption band and
the window hold still. The engine's canvas camera frames targets on the frame centre (y 960) and has no viewport, so a push
would run into the caption band; the style does it **inside the diagram scene** instead (P-DIAGRAM-PUSH, P-MOTIF-VISIT:
drawn on canvas, `box` = the band, clipped to x 0–1080, y 110–1170). The canvas camera module stays off.

### 10.4 Layer order (back to front)
1. The world (stage / paper), z1
2. The world glow, z2
3. Card-slot content: diagrams, the motif, UI, docs, tiles, clip cards, z3 (drawn under the footage group, which never
   overlaps the band)
4. The face window / the full-face footage; the full-bleed clip scene in L-clip, z4
5. The lockup, labels, tags, chips, hand labels, z5
6. Hero numerals, stat chips, counter chips, z6
7. The captions (CS-1 / CS-2), z7

Nothing above z7: no big caption layer, no comedy layer, no banner, no light passes.

### 10.5 Finishing
No grain, no vignette, no light leaks. Glow only on the stage's neon lines and the lit node (`shadowBlur` 20–28 px, the
role colour at 70 %). Paper cards: radius 18–28, shadow `0 10px 30px rgba(0,0,0,.15)`. Stage cards: `#121214` fill, a
1.5 px rgba(255,255,255,.35) hairline, glow `0 0 40px rgba(255,255,255,.08)`. The face window: radius 30, shadow 0.25, no
border.

## §11 Sound

The cut is the drama; sound only marks a landing. (The reference reels' sound couldn't be measured, so this is a decision,
not a measurement.)
| Line | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Where sound goes** | The hook (f0 and the first reading event); reveals (a card or diagram landing, a counter's last value); the list cue (each P-HERO-NUMERAL or station landing: the one sound allowed to repeat); the CTA lockup landing; optionally a soft whoosh on a P-SMEAR-PUSH. **Mode cuts, jump-cut steps, title swaps and pushes are silent.** Sparse: if in doubt, leave it out |
| **Meme sounds** | None |
| **The bed** | On; it enters at the first unit after the hook and drops out 0.5 s before the recap |
| **Ducking** | The bed sits ≥ 18 dB under the voice while the voice speaks; a creator clip's own audio (P-CLIP-INTERCUT) sits 20 dB under the voice, or is muted if it has speech |
| **Loudness** | −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP; a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word |

No file more than twice in a reel, except the list cue.

| **A (assumed)** | Landscape 16:9, **3840×2160**, 30 fps (or 24/25, conformed), locked off at eye level, medium-wide (waist up), the creator centred (face centre at 42–58 % of the width), head top at 12–22 % of the frame's height, face 20–26 % of the frame's height; one coloured practical light in the background (an LED strip or a lamp) on a dark set; a plain dark top; hands free (the style lives on gestures); a lav or shotgun mic out of frame |
| **B (fallback)** | Vertical 9:16, 1080×1920, chest-up, head top at 10–14 % of the frame's height (FB-1) |

From setup A the window shows the whole wide frame (a 0.27× scale of the 4K frame) and the full face is a 9:16 slice of the
same frame at ≈ 1.0× (no upscaling): one camera gives both modes. A full-face punch at face 0.26 needs a face box ≥ 450 px
in the source, so a 4K landscape take; 1080p sources allow ≤ 1.35× upscale.

### 12.2 Shots and fallbacks
| ID | Shot | Without it |
|---|---|---|
| SH-1 | The single talking-head take (setup A) | **FB-1** vertical 1080×1920 (setup B): the window frames head and shoulders (face 0.30 of the window), L-full uses the native frame at face 0.24 (≤ 1.15×); the window loses the room and the two modes look closer. **FB-2** landscape 1920×1080: L-full at face 0.20 (a 1.35× crop of the 9:16 slice), the window as setup A; looser, slightly soft punches |

The cut-out is optional: it only draws the head over the window's top edge (`breakout`). Without it the head stays inside
the window.

### 12.3 Frame rate and audio
Output 1080×1920, 30 fps CFR, BT.709. One voice track: high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, light compression, −14 LUFS.

### 12.4 Third-party moments: fetch the real thing
When the creator names a film or TV moment, another creator's reel, a tweet or post, a news headline, a product's screen,
a person or a brand, the viewer should see the real one.
1. The creator's own files in their folder come first.
2. Otherwise search the web and fetch it: the real logo, the real post, the real page (captured and framed on the part
   that matters), the real clip. Note where it came from.
3. Use it as it is (in P-CLIP-INTERCUT, P-CLIP-CARD, P-CLIP-TAG, P-SHOT-CARD or P-MINI-PLAYER), never altered to say
   something else; a post or headline is shown word for word.
4. **Nothing usable to be found:** rebuild it from its exact words, with no labels and no credit lines:

| Moment | Rebuilt as |
|---|---|
| A film / TV / another creator's clip | A `fx.silhouette` or line-art scene of the action in the card slot, or a P-TILE-FLIP / P-ROAD-FORK beat carrying the same point; never a full-bleed fake clip |
| Another creator's reel or thumbnail | Created typographic tiles (a topic word, a tint; no faces, no handles) |
| A post or quote | `fx.quoteCard` with the script's exact words |
| A product screen | `fx.appUI` / `fx.device`, a recreated generic UI |
| A brand or tool | `fx.logoPlate` (the name set in type) |
| A person | `fx.silhouette` with the name and role from the script |

## §13 What your plan should settle

Your `ideas.md` is a page or two, for you. In this style it has decided, before any code:
1. **The framework line:** its name, its N parts, the motif shape, the structure variant (FW-1/2/3).
2. **The hook:** the lockup with its alternates, the f0 card, the first punch word.
3. **The mode map:** every punch and every return, each on its word, each return with the card event it lands on.
4. **The worlds:** paper or stage per section, and where they flip.
5. **The recurring diagram:** its build, every visit, the recap (`motif_state` on the beats).
6. **The board per beat:** a pattern for every term, a figure for every number, the inserts (the creator's, fetched or
   rebuilt) and the fallbacks used.
7. **The sound:** the few cues, the list cue, the bed's entry and drop-out.
8. **The moments you'll look at hardest on the storyboard:** f0, the first punch, one motif visit with its numeral, one
   diagram push, the CTA asset, and the head over the window's edge on a split frame (clear of the caption).

## §15 Your look at the storyboard: the checklist

Watch it once as a stranger with a thumb over the next reel, then once as the editor whose name is on it. Fix what
bothers you, in one pass.

**The style (does it feel like §The feel?)**
- Frame 0: the card skeleton is moving and the window is live; the lockup reads within a second.
- Every claim gets the face, by a hard cut, static on entry; every return finds the board moved on.
- The board never freezes while it's up; one idea per card.
- One recurring diagram: built early, visited at every step with only the current part lit, recapped before the CTA.
- The lockup is a confident sentence: Title Case, one crimson device, at most one serif span.
- Colour stays quiet; neon only on the active part; red → green is old → new.
- Sound is sparse; the mode cuts are silent.
- Start to end: you could redraw the diagram afterwards.

**Craft (by eye, in context)**
- On split frames the head breaks the window's edge cleanly, the caption band above it; the face reads on every face
  frame. Nothing chops the head or buries the face by accident.
- One caption word at a time, in its band, ink on paper and white on stage, not on a card.
- No text over text: one lockup, one label group, the caption; never two cards.
- Every card event, node and numeral lands on its word; every cut on a word start; nothing lingers after its point.
- Every number shown is spoken, scripted or the creator's own; concept curves carry no axis numbers; illustrations carry
  no labels; fetched posts and headlines word for word.
- Names, tools and brands spelt exactly; the promised count = the steps shown; the CTA keyword readable; the asset shown
  is the asset promised.
- The file itself (1080×1920, 30 fps, −14 LUFS, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word, no black tail) is the render's
  job; it checks it.
