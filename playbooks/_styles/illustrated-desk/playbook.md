# Illustrated Desk Style Playbook (template v2)

## The feel

This reel is a generous friend at a bright desk, showing you everything they use. They say the name of an app and, before
the word is out, it's there: its tile and its name drop into the space above their head, and the whole picture of them
glides down to make room, like someone sliding their coffee aside so you can see the screen. Then the actual screen slides
in under a soft teal glow. Every noun becomes a neat, real-looking thing: a book tilting up on deep navy, a list of goals
wiping open row by row, a chat thread rising on a pastel gradient. The viewer never has to imagine anything. They see it,
tidy and polished, the moment it's said.

That's the engine: a constant, small reward. Every name pays off at once, and beautifully, so the viewer stays for the next
one, and a list of tools feels like opening presents. The picture says what; the creator says why. When they turn to
opinion ("I love it because…"), the screen slides away left, the face rises back into the room, and it's just them and the
caption pill, the words darkening one by one as they speak. Show the thing, then give the face back.

Nothing shouts. No shake, no crash zoom, no flashing colour: the energy is in how quickly and cleanly things move, a push
with a little smear, a tile popping into place, a red pen circle drawing itself round the one number that matters. Colour
is rare and always means something: teal is a window onto a tool, red is a hand saying "look here", green is the one they
chose, and yellow glows once, on navy, for the reel's big idea. One thing on screen at a time, and the face is never lost
for long.

It breathes like a good explainer: quick and even through the list, the richest picture saved for the last item, then a
quiet ending on the face with a question, and a clean stop. Calm on the surface, generous underneath.

**The test:** pause right after the creator names something: you can see that exact thing, polished and alone, and nothing
on screen is shouting.

## What this playbook is

You're editing a warm, well-lit desk talker explaining something useful (the tools they use, one idea that changed how
they work, a story of trying something), and you have the authority to make it the most generous, most watchable version
of that reel in their niche. This playbook is the style, pulled from three reels by a productivity creator (v01 five apps
they use daily, v02 focusing on one thing, v03 a product story told over team B-roll) and measured frame by frame, then
sharpened. Read it all, every time; take what fits the reel, invent where a moment needs more, and never break the feel
above.

**Who it's for and what it needs.** Teachers, reviewers and builders whose talk is full of named things: apps, books,
people, routines, documents. One talking-head take carries the list (F-A) and concept (F-B) formats; the story format
(F-C) needs the creator's own B-roll. Their screenshots, screen recordings and logos make the strongest inserts; what they
don't have is fetched from the web (the real logo, the real page), and only what can't be found gets built as a clean,
generic version (§12). No cut-out by default (only P-17 needs one). Captions follow
the creator's language (English, Hinglish or Hindi, set in their copy). Machine values live in `tokens.json`; where this
text gives a number tokens also holds, they agree.

## §1 Procedure: how to approach a reel in this style

You watch, listen, plan, build and look at the storyboard yourself; the edit skill has the mechanics. This style
lives or dies on one craft step, the named-thing inventory, and on deciding where each named thing goes: above the head,
on a full-screen world, or over B-roll.

1. **Pick the format from the script** (§3.1). A spoken count of items → F-A Desk list. One concept with a few examples →
   F-B Concept cutaways. A chronological story with people or a product in use → F-C Story with B-roll. When the script fits
   two, choose the one whose footage exists: no B-roll, never F-C.
2. **Measure the head.** Read the head top y on three frames of the take (full frame). It sets the make-room offset
   (§3.4 G-1) and tells you whether a top-band insert clears the hair.
3. **Build the named-thing inventory (the craft step).** List every tool, app, site, book, person, place, product,
   document and number the script names, in order, each with: its first word and time, what the viewer sees (§8.4), its
   source (the creator's file, fetched from the web, or what you'll build) and its placement (top band, cutaway, card over B-roll). Every row gets
   a visual; none waits for the next sentence. A name mentioned again soon after reuses its visual; a later mention gets a
   smaller echo (the tool's tile in the corner of a window, or an ink circle on the card already up). Every pointing word
   ("this one", "right here") gets its target.
4. **Feel the tone of every line:** `explain` · `awe` · `win` · `warn` · `cta`. Most lines explain; the one big idea is
   `awe`; a chosen or finished thing is `win`; "don't do this" is `warn`; the closing invitation is `cta`. The tone sets the
   camera, colour and ink:

   | Tone | Camera | Colour | Ink |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | `explain` | Z-1 or none | neutral, accent rims | allowed |
   | `awe` | Z-1 | the `primary` glow headline on navy | none |
   | `win` | Z-2 on a cut | `good` on the picked row | a circle on the result |
   | `warn` | none | a marker underline only | underline |
   | `cta` | none | none | none |

5. **Write the hook** (§6): the format's archetype, 8–10 headline candidates, the best by the stopper test, two alternates.
6. **Plan the top band, item by item** (F-A): the tool drop, then its window or its built substitute, then the settle-back
   on the opinion line (§7.3). Give each tool its own type voice and each window the next rim tint.
7. **Plan the ink** (§8.6): each mark on a named target, with the target's exact rect.
8. **Get the third-party pictures** (§12.4): the creator's files first, then the web (real logos, real pages); build only
   what can't be found. Note any paid or affiliate segment (§6.7).
9. **Plan the sound** (§11): sparse, soft, on landings.

## §2 Hard rules: the craft and the style's never-list

**Craft, by eye** (judge it on the storyboard, in context, the way an editor does):
- **Keep the person clear.** The face stays findable, so keep it, the hair and the room above the head clear of front
  layers, captions included. The framing that does it: on L-desk nothing is drawn on the footage but the pill on the
  chest; in L-topband every insert ends at y 560 and the lowered head starts at y ≥ 584 (§3.7); ink marks and name tags
  stay 40 px clear of the head region. A pill brushing a lowered chin for a beat is a judgement call; a head chopped or a
  face buried by accident never is. Behind the person is
  fair game (`behind: true`, with a cut-out), though this style rarely needs it.
- **No text over text.** One insert (or one cutaway card group), at most three ink marks and the pill; the pill sits 40 px
  clear of every card, row and window; the outgoing insert has finished leaving before the next one enters (≤ 2 f overlap).
- **On the word.** Every named thing lands its visual 2 f before its trigger word and is fully on within ±5 f; a tool drop
  lands within ±2 f of the tool's name. Cuts sit on word boundaries ±1 f; never two camera moves within 0.4 s.
- **Say what was said.** A number or quote the creator says is shown exactly as said, never rounded. Illustrations may use
  made-up but realistic numbers, names and screens, with no label. A recreated chat, calendar or file card shows the words
  the script gives; it never invents a stat, a reply or a time and presents it as the creator's real result. Quotes
  verbatim.
- **Promise integrity.** A spoken count ("5 apps") equals the items shown, in order, one tool drop per item; the count title's
  numeral equals the number of ITEM sections.
- **Spelling.** Tool, product, book and person names spelt and cased exactly as the creator writes them ("Notion",
  "ChatGPT", "iPhone").
- **Readable.** Pill captions 48–56 px (default 50) at weight ≥ 600, one line, ≤ 30 characters, every word state ≥ 4.5:1
  on the pill (active #111111 ≈ 17:1, queued #666666 ≈ 4.8:1) (E3). Display text ≥ 40 px; UI meta text 28–39 px only when
  the same words are spoken or shown larger (E3: weight ≥ 600, contrast ≥ 7:1). Titles hold ≥ 10 f after they finish
  building; text ≥ 0.25 s per word.
- **Private data.** Emails, phone numbers and account ids in a creator's screenshot are blurred for their whole time on
  screen.
- **Paid segments disclosed:** "Paid partnership" under the plate for the whole segment and ≥ 2.0 s (§6.7).
- **Audio.** −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP, the bed ≥ 18 dB under the voice, a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last
  word, no black tail.

**Never in this style:**
- Stock 3D art, glowing brains, generic "productivity" stock clips. Use the named thing itself.
- A redrawn brand logo, or a built UI that imitates a real product's look (its colour scheme, logo glyph, layout). Logos
  are the real files: the creator's, else fetched from the web; when none can be found, the name is set in type (P-07).
- Two inserts at once, or a top-band insert and a full cutaway together.
- Shake, crash zooms, rotation snaps, whip pans of the footage, RGB split, glitches, light leaks, film burns, white flash
  frames, emoji spam. The calm is the style.
- A second glow headline, or a glow headline on the desk footage: it lives on W-navy and carries the reel's one big idea.
- Coloured words inside the pill: it's two greys only.
- White or cream text on W-paper or W-pastel; ink text on W-navy.
- Ink as decoration: every circle, underline or arrow points at the thing being said.
- An end card, a keyword card, a subscribe sticker or a follow stack.
- Regrading the footage.

## §3 Worlds, layouts, stage moves, safe zones

### 3.1 Formats
Three formats, one look: the karaoke pill, one polished literal visual per named thing, soft quick motion with no
loud effects, red ink on clean UI, and a warm, bright desk talker.

| Format | When (reel type) | Layouts | Default hook | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F-A Desk list (default) | "N tools / apps / habits / resources I use", "my setup", any numbered list said in one take | L-desk, L-topband, L-frost | HA-04 topic build (count title + tile arc) | list |
| F-B Concept cutaways | One idea, principle or method: "focus on one thing", "why X works", a framework with examples | L-desk, L-cutaway, L-frost, L-topband | HA-12 question → glow headline | explainer |
| F-C Story with B-roll | A story or case: "a week with…", "how our team…", "what happened when…" | L-desk, L-topband, L-cutaway, L-broll, L-frost | HA-04 ink-bubble topic | story |

F-A is an anchor: the face never leaves. F-B is a host: the face hands over to a full-screen world for a sentence and takes
the screen back. F-C is a guest in its own story: B-roll and UI carry whole sentences and the face comes back to reflect.
F-B moves the pill up to cy 1220; F-C runs longer (60–90 s against 45–60 s) and needs the B-roll.

### 3.2 Worlds
| ID | Kind | Look | Carries | Enter / exit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **W-desk** | footage | The creator's own room: warm, bright, soft key from camera left, a bokeh shelf or a pale wall; not regraded | Every talking line, top-band inserts, opinions | Hard cut (T-01) back from a cutaway |
| **W-navy** | stage | `night` #0A1220 with a soft radial lift (#14213A at x 50 %, y 42 %), grain 0.05, vignette 0.35 | Concepts: the glow headline, list rows, book float, object dome, person card, stat card | Hard cut on the noun (T-01); out by hard cut |
| **W-paper** | paper | `canvas` #F2F2F0, grain 0.03, vignette 0.12 (a light world: ink text) | Question card, calendar, checklist | Blur-out then cut from the face (T-02), or a hard cut |
| **W-pastel** | canvas | A vertical gradient `G-pastel` #F4E3A0 (top) → #A9E6A3 (bottom), grain 0.02 (a light world) | Recreated chat threads, file cards, hub dots (F-C; also F-A for a long UI moment) | Hard cut; leaves by the T-10 curtain wipe (the B-roll slides down over it, v03 @ 22.0) or a hard cut to the face. The card stack drifts slowly while held (≈ 1–2 px/f) |

One cutaway world per beat; worlds switch by cut, never by cross-fading into each other.

**Entering and leaving the top band (F-A).** L-topband is entered on a named noun (a tool, a site, a document) and left on
the first opinion or aside ("I love it because…", "which is super nice"), or when the insert has nothing more to show,
whichever comes first. Consecutive items chain inside L-topband: the outgoing window pushes out left while the next tool's
name pushes in from the right (T-11), with no settle-back between them (v01 @ 9.44–9.84, window → "Claude"). When the face
does settle back, give it a real moment on L-desk before the next make-room, long enough that the return feels like the
creator talking to you, not a hiccup between inserts.

### 3.6 Safe zones and bands
- Meaning text inside x 64–1016, y 110–1500; nothing meaningful at x > 970 between y 900–1540 (Instagram's buttons).
- **Top band:** x 150–930, y 130–560: one insert (its own content may reach x 120–960 for a two-plate row).
- **Pill band:** centre y 1420 (F-B 1220), height ≈ 96 px (50 px type + 20 px padding top and bottom; measured y 1386–1482,
  v01 @ 0:20, 0:45), so ≈ 1372–1468 (F-B ≈ 1172–1268). Every other element keeps 40 px clear of it: y ≤ 1332 in F-A and F-C;
  in F-B content ends ≤ 1132 above it or starts ≥ 1308 below it.
- **Cutaway content box:** x 90–990, y 160–1300 (in F-B, content ends at 1132 above the pill or starts at 1308 below it).

### 3.7 The person
- **L-desk:** the take as shot, head top y 260–380, eyes y 520–640. Nothing is drawn on the footage except the pill, which
  sits on the chest (band ≈ 1372–1468; F-B ≈ 1172–1268), several hundred px below the chin.
- **L-topband:** the footage lowers by O so the head top lands at y ≥ 584; the insert band ends at y 560, so the hair has
  24 px of air above it. The top band is the room above the head, which is why the inserts stop where they do: never let a
  window, a plate or a tile reach below 560 unless you mean it. If the measured head top is so high that O hits its 340 px cap and the hair
  would still rise above 584, shorten the insert (a lower window height) so its bottom stays 24 px above the hair; check it
  on the first top-band frame of the storyboard.
- **The hook's tiles and the bubble:** the tile arc sits above and beside the head: the centre tiles end above the hair and
  the outer tiles, which hang lowest, sit beside the head, not over it. The F-C ink bubble (y 130–400) would sit on the
  hair of an unlowered take (head top 260–420), so it runs on L-topband, the take lowered from t 0 as in HA-02, or behind
  the head with the cut-out.
- **The lowered chin and the pill:** in F-A and F-C the pill (top ≈ 1372) stays clear of the lowered face. In F-B's rare
  top-band beats the pill (top ≈ 1172) sits right under a lowered chin: look at that still; the engine moves the pill below
  the chin when it would touch (`avoid_face`); keep it off the face unless it looks right there.
- **L-frost and the cutaways:** in L-frost the presenter is blurred and darkened into a backdrop, and the card may sit over
  the blurred figure; it reads as a cutaway, and the face comes back sharp by a cut. L-cutaway and L-broll have no presenter.
- **Ink and name tags** stay 40 px clear of the head region (face, hair, the room above the head); a name tag sits beside a
  person in B-roll, not across their head.
- **Returns:** from a cutaway by a hard cut on a sentence start (T-01) (the T-02 blur-out only goes face → cutaway); from
  L-topband by the G-2 settle-back.
- **The face stays findable.** F-A never loses it. F-B leaves for the length of a cutaway sentence and cuts straight back on
  the next one. F-C lets a chapter's B-roll and UI run, but every chapter comes back to the face for at least a couple of
  seconds of reflection. For reference, measured on the three reels (a description, not a target): the face on screen
  ≈ 100 % of v01, ≈ 52 % of v02 with cutaways up to 6 s, ≈ 36 % of v03 with a UI run of 13 s.

Gradients: `G-count` #F2A7C3 → #FBE8C8 (the count numeral and caps title; with the creator's own colour it becomes primary →
paper at 70 %); `G-pastel` #F4E3A0 → #A9E6A3 (W-pastel). The creator's brand colours, when given, replace `primary` and
`accent` at setup; everything else stays.

### 4.2 Meanings
- **Neutral is the base.** White cards, the off-white pill, navy and pastel worlds. Colour is rare and always means something.
- **Yellow (primary) = the big idea.** Only on navy: the glow headline, the picked row's edge.
- **Teal (accent) = this is a window onto a tool.** Rims, the bar under a tool's name, links between tools. Rims rotate
  accent → violet → data across consecutive windows so each tool feels distinct.
- **Red (marker) = look here.** Only hand-drawn ink. Never "bad".
- **Green (good) = the chosen one, or done.**
- **Gold (concept) = the phrase to remember** inside a hero statement.
- **Brand colours** appear only inside real logo files (the creator's or fetched); built tool plates never use a brand's colour.

### 4.3 Rules
- At most three bright hues in a frame (roles primary, accent, concept, data, violet, marker, good; `warm` and `rose` don't
  count). A window rim + an ink circle + the glow headline is the most; in practice two.
- Coloured text on a light world sits on a chip or is ≥ 4.5:1 (data blue on white passes; gold never goes on paper).
- The glow headline is the only glowing text; window rims are the only glowing shapes.
- The footage is never regraded: match exposure and white balance between setups only.

## §6 Hook system

**The hook title.** The on-screen title promises the viewer something: an outcome they want, a curiosity gap, or who it's
for ("5 apps that run my whole week", not "My productivity apps"). It doesn't have to repeat the spoken words; it has to be
true to what the reel delivers, and the reel then shows every thing it names. In this style the title's shape is the
format's title card (§5.2): a count title with a serif subline, a glow headline with a hand subline, or an ink bubble.
Write 8–10 candidates from the formulas in §6.5 and the proven patterns ("How to X as a Y", "Why your X isn't working",
"The X nobody tells you", "Stop doing X", a number or a contrast), score them on outcome, curiosity, who it's for and
brevity, run the best through the stopper test, pick, and keep the next two as alternates.

### 6.1 The stopper test
| Test | How it passes in this style |
|---|---|
| Mute | The topic is readable without sound in the first 3 s: count title + tiles (F-A), glow headline (F-B), ink bubble + hero statement (F-C) |
| Motion at f0 | Live footage mid-gesture (F-A tile 1 follows by 0.2 s; F-C blob already on screen, its first word by 0.2 s) |
| Read time | The headline reads in ≤ 1.5 s (≤ 6 words) |
| Density | The first three seconds feel full and generous: tiles popping, the picture making room, the numeral and title snapping on, the subline typing, the pill darkening. Dense in time, never in space: each thing lands as the last one settles |
| Payoff by 1.8 s | The title card (or F-B's glow headline, tagged `payoff: true`) is on screen by 1.8 s |

Frame 0 isn't a thumbnail in this style: it's the creator mid-gesture. Choose the post's cover frame from 1.0–2.5 s, where
the title and tiles (or the glow headline) are fully readable.

## §7 Structure and rhythm

### 7.1 Structure per format
| Format | Type | The arc |
|---|---|---|
| F-A | `list` | HOOK → ITEM-1…ITEM-N (usually 3–7) → OUT. The hook is over in about three seconds; each item gets as long as its tool deserves, and the last item gets the most |
| F-B | `explainer` | HOOK → PROBLEM (list rows of the many things) → CONCEPT (glow, books, a quote) → EXAMPLES (row pick, a person, a stat) → QUESTION (the question card) → TACTIC (the calendar or a checklist) → OUT |
| F-C | `story` | HOOK (bubble + hero statement) → CHAPTER-1…CHAPTER-K (usually 2–4) → TURN (the result: a file card or a stat with ink) → OUT (the face, a reflection) |

Beat `section` names are exactly these (`HOOK`, `ITEM-1`, `PROBLEM`, `CHAPTER-2`, `OUT`…): the promise check counts the
`ITEM-n` sections.

### 7.2 Markers
- **SM-1 Tool drop as marker (F-A).** Numbering is spoken only ("First", "Next up", "Third", "And finally"); no numerals on
  screen after the hook. Each item opens with its P-07 tool drop on the item's name; that drop is the marker.
- **SM-2 Chapter bubble (F-C).** Each chapter after the first opens with a P-05 ink bubble above the lowered head (G-1
  first): "then came / [SUBJECT]", "a week with / [PERSON]", 1.5–2.5 s, scene `rehook: true`.
- **F-B has no markers** (spoken only): its sections are carried by the world changes (face ↔ navy ↔ paper).
- **A series** (only when the creator runs one): a tag `{name} · part {n}` in Poppins 600 40 px paper on a 60 % ink chip at
  x 64, y 130, for the hook only, left of the count title.

### 7.3 The unit ritual (frames at 30 fps; t = the item noun's onset)
**F-A item:**
| When | What |
|---|---|
| t − 12 f | The ordinal word on the pill; the face on L-desk |
| t − 6 f | G-1 make-room starts (13 f), unless already in L-topband |
| t − 2 f | **P-07 tool drop**: when the previous insert is still up, the name pushes in from the right (T-11, 8 f) as the old insert pushes out left; otherwise the tile pops (3–4 f). The logo tile lands beside the name 2–4 f after it (v01 @ 9.60–9.84) |
| t + 1.5–2.5 s | On the next noun ("workspace", "dashboard", "inbox") the drop **rockets up off the top** (3–4 f, vertical motion blur) while the **P-08 screen window slides in from the right** (9 f, expo-out) in the same frames (v01 @ 20.28–20.60), or its built substitute P-09 / P-10 / P-21 |
| while it's described | The window holds with one or two things happening inside it (a scroll, a screen swap, an ink circle on the feature named); jump cuts in the take run under it untouched |
| The next item named | T-11 push: the window slides out left, the next tool's name slides in from the right; stay in L-topband |
| The opinion line | The window slides out left (8 f, expo-out with a slow tail) **while** the G-2 settle-back runs (12 f) → the face on L-desk for the opinion; a Z-2 reframe on a jump cut is welcome |

**F-B concept beat:** a face line on L-desk → on the noun, a G-3 hard cut to the cutaway; the pattern builds over 0.3–1.5 s
(one row, one book or one object per spoken item) → holds while the sentence runs → back to the face by T-01 on the next
sentence start.

**F-C chapter:** the P-05 chapter bubble (re-hook) → B-roll beats (G-6, a few seconds per clip) with a P-24 card over the
B-roll when a UI moment comes up → a pastel UI run (G-3 on W-pastel: P-21 thread, P-22 file card) with one ink circle on
the key number → a person beat with a P-30 name tag → back to the face (L-desk, or L-topband with one card) for the
reflection.

### 7.4 Open loops
- **The count loop (F-A):** the title's numeral is paid off item by item; "And finally" brings in the last item with the
  richest insert (a P-10 sequence, or P-08 + ink).
- **The question loop (F-B):** the question card (P-18, `kind: "question-card"`, which also counts as a re-hook) is answered
  by the tactic right after it.
- **The story loop (F-C):** the hero statement's doubt ("I didn't know if…") is answered in TURN. A chapter bubble around
  the middle of the story re-hooks it; a long story earns a second one late.
- The hook is over fast, so the first item arrives while the promise is fresh. Every promise is paid on screen: a count, a
  question, a story's doubt.

### 7.5 Rhythm by feel
- **Even and warm, never flat.** Something new arrives every time the creator names something, and between names the
  inserts keep living (a scroll, a screen swap, a circle drawing). The reel never sits still, but nothing is random: every
  change is a word paying off.
- **Show the thing, then give the face back.** An insert holds as long as it has something to show; on the opinion it
  slides away and the face rises back. A face run lasts as long as the thought. When a face-only stretch starts to feel
  long, a Z-2 reframe on a jump cut or a small top-band insert for the next noun keeps it alive, never a decorative element.
- **The list builds.** Items flow at a steady, confident pace, each a small present; the last item (or chapter) gets the
  richest visual (a screen sequence, the row pick, the stat with ink), never a louder effect.
- **Variety comes from the things, not a quota.** Each tool gets its own type voice and the next rim tint so the list feels
  like distinct things; the F-A item ritual is the one deliberate repeat.
- **The end is quiet:** the face, the pill, a question or a thought, then a hard stop (P-35).
- No comedy beats. For reference, measured on the three reels (a description, not a target): scene changes at 2.8, 2.9 and
  1.6 every ten seconds (v01 / v02 / v03) plus the inserts' own events and a pill swap every few words; v02 shots median 2.5 s;
  v03 median 3.3 s with UI runs up to 15.7 s; v01 overlays changing every 2.5–5.5 s on one take.

### 8.5 Data and truth
- Numbers appear as the words the script says (P-37, chat bodies, file cards), written exactly as spoken (§5.5).
- No charts with axes or values unless every value is spoken; a file card's mini bars are illustrative and carry no numbers.
- Recreated UIs are generic (no real brand's logo, colours or layout unless it's a real capture, the creator's or fetched). Made-up but
  realistic names, messages and screens are fine as illustration, with no label; the creator's real claims (results,
  prices, benchmarks) are shown only as they said them.
- Personal data in a screenshot is blurred for its whole time on screen.

### 8.6 The ink layer: annotation marks
The handmade layer on top of clean UI: it tells the eye where to look.

**Stroke tokens**
| Token | Value |

| Colour | `marker` #D92D3A (red to coral, ≥ 4.5:1 with white; the reels' red #E63946 reached only 4.2:1); name tags and bubble outlines in `paper`; sparkles in `concept` |
| Width | 7 px (6–8), round caps and joins |
| Wobble | seeded ±1.5 px perpendicular noise along the path (8–12 control points), fixed per mark (`ctx.rngStable`) |

| Overshoot | circles start at 200° and run 375° (15° past the start), so the end crosses the start like a pen stroke |

The marks are P-27…P-31 (§8.3) and the P-05 bubble outline (offset 10 px, 3 px paper). No highlight sweeps or boxes in this style.

**Targets.** Ink lands on rects you know exactly: the element rect inside a card you built (draw the mark inside that card's
own markup so it nests and moves with it), or a pixel rect inside the creator's screenshot (scaled into the window). On
footage, only a person beside whom a name tag sits, read from three sampled frames of that span and static within ± 30 px
for the tag's life; a moving target gets no ink (put P-30 beside the person instead, 40 px clear of the head region).
There's no tracking in this style.

**How it behaves**
- At most three marks on screen, and one circle per card at a time: a second circle on the same card replaces the first
  (fade 4 f).
- A mark starts drawing 2 f before its word, finishes within its draw time, lives as long as its target and exits with it;
  it finishes before the next scene. Off the person and off the pill.
- Ink is a pen pointing, not punctuation: spend it on the one number, field or phrase each card is really about, so every
  circle feels like the creator leaning over to show you.
- Ink never means good or bad: a "winner" in P-12 gets the circle because it's the one being talked about.

**Recipe (SVG inside the target scene; deterministic):**
```js
function inkCircle(ctx, r, p, seed) {            // r = {x, y, w, h} target rect; p = 0..1 draw progress
  const R = ctx.rngStable(seed), pad = 18, cx = r.x + r.w / 2, cy = r.y + r.h / 2;
  const rx = r.w / 2 + pad, ry = r.h / 2 + pad, tilt = (R() * 12 - 6) * Math.PI / 180, pts = [];
  for (let i = 0; i <= 40; i++) {                 // 200° -> 575° (375° sweep: 15° overshoot)
    const a = (200 + 375 * i / 40) * Math.PI / 180, w = 1 + (R() - 0.5) * 0.03;
    const x = rx * w * Math.cos(a), y = ry * w * Math.sin(a);
    pts.push([cx + x * Math.cos(tilt) - y * Math.sin(tilt), cy + x * Math.sin(tilt) + y * Math.cos(tilt)]);
  }
  const d = "M" + pts.map(q => q[0].toFixed(1) + " " + q[1].toFixed(1)).join(" L"), L = 2 * Math.PI * Math.max(rx, ry) * 1.1;
  return `<svg width="1080" height="1920" style="position:absolute;left:0;top:0;overflow:visible"><path d="${d}" fill="none"
    stroke="${ctx.col("marker")}" stroke-width="7" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"
    stroke-dasharray="${L.toFixed(0)}" stroke-dashoffset="${(L * (1 - ctx.ease.out(ctx.clamp(p)))).toFixed(1)}"/></svg>`;
}
```

### 8.7 Comedy: off unless the creator turns it on
No gags, stickers or meme beats: the style's warmth is its generosity, not jokes. When the creator turns comedy to `light`,
one emoji sticker tab (as on P-18) may sit on a P-07 / P-08 corner for a light moment, off the person; no meme sounds.

### 8.8 Assets
- **Real captures first:** the creator's screenshots and recordings of the tools they name, their logos, their B-roll,
  their photos; then the real thing fetched from the web (the logo, the page, the cover, the person's photo), source
  noted.
- **Built substitutes are generic:** no real product's logo glyph, colour scheme or layout (a chat UI is white cards with
  initials avatars, not a look-alike of a known chat app).
- **No stock:** no stock B-roll, no stock 3D renders. The reels' 3D objects (weight plates, a bulb, books) become the
  creator's cut-out photos or `fx.icon` line icons on a CSS dome, and built covers.
- **Logos:** the real file, never redrawn: the creator's, else fetched from the web; a monogram tile only when none can be
  found. The name beside it is always set in its type voice.
- **Third-party moments:** fetch the real thing (§12.4).

### 9.2 Grammar
| Boundary | Use | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Frame 0 | The live take mid-gesture (+ the F-C blob); F-B the plain take | A fade-in from black, a title card before the face |
| Hook → item 1 (F-A) | T-07 tile hand-off | A hard pop of the first tool |
| A new item (F-A) | From L-desk: T-03 make-room + P-07; from an insert: T-11 push | A cut to a cutaway world |
| Tool drop → its window | The drop rockets off the top + the window slides in from the right, in the same frames | Two inserts cross-dissolving; a rise-and-fade |
| Item → opinion line | The window slides out left + the T-04 settle-back together | Holding the insert over the opinion |
| Face → concept (F-B) | T-01 on the noun (± 1 f) | A blur-out on a noun (too slow) |
| Face → question card / calendar | T-02 | — |
| Cutaway → face | T-01 on the next sentence start (T-02 only runs face → cutaway) | — |
| B-roll → B-roll (F-C) | T-01 on clause ends; under a pinned card the clips may stutter through a 2-frame clip (v03 @ 38.23–38.30) | Dissolves, whips |
| Pastel UI → B-roll (F-C) | T-10 curtain wipe | A cross-fade between worlds |
| The last word | T-09 | A black tail, an end card |

### 9.3 Cutting the B-roll (F-C)
- **R-1** Cut B-roll on clause ends (±1 f of the word end); a few seconds per clip (2.5–6 s).
- **R-2** When a person is named, cut to a clip of that person within ±5 f and add a P-30 name tag.
- **R-3** Alternate wide and detail clips; never two wides back to back.
- **R-4** Every chapter comes back to the creator's face for a reflection of at least a couple of seconds.
- **R-5** B-roll that shows the creator counts as their presence only when the face is ≥ 6 % of the frame height; the engine
  reads presence from the main take, so plan the face moments from the take.

### 9.4 How the moves breathe
The moves are quick and quiet: an 8-frame push, a rocket off the top, a hard cut. Their rhythm lives in where they fall. In
F-A the make-room and settle-back are the reel's breathing, down to show, up to talk, and the T-11 push is what makes a run
of tools feel like a flowing list rather than a slideshow. In F-B the hard cuts on nouns carry the idea and the face
returns on every new sentence; the slower blur-out is saved for a turn inward, a question, a plan. In F-C the curtain wipe
is the chapter's exhale back into life footage, kept for leaving a UI run. The tile hand-off happens once, as the hook
becomes the list. Never let the same move become a tic: if three moves in a row feel identical and the item ritual isn't
the reason, change one.

| Hold | titles ≥ 10 f after building; text ≥ 0.25 s per word |

### 10.2 Footage camera and zoom (`zoom_policy: presets`)
| ID | Preset | Recipe | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Z-1** | `push-drift` | 1.00 → 1.12 (1.10–1.12) over the beat, linear, no blur. Optional: not measured in the reels (v02's hook growth is the presenter leaning in; ORB on the take shows no steady digital zoom) | A reflective line, rarely |
| **Z-2** | `snap-punch` (reframe) | 1.00 → 1.12 over 2 f, **only exactly on a jump cut**, so it reads as a new angle; held to the next cut | Face-only stretches that want a new angle; `win` lines |
| **Z-3** | `pull-out` | 1.12 → 1.00 over 10 f at a later cut | Back to the wide crop |

Z-0 (`reset`) returns to the base framing. The camera is gentle. Change the kind of move from one to the next so it feels alive, not mechanical, and keep moves 0.4 s
apart. No camera move while a top-band insert is up (the insert is the event, and a stage change resets the camera); plain
jump cuts in the take may run under an insert (v01 @ 9.44). Never shake, crash zoom or rotation snap. The biggest
camera-like moves in this style are the make-room and settle-back morphs (§3.4).

### 10.3 Layer order (back to front)
1. z1 the world (W-navy / W-paper / W-pastel) or the B-roll clip (`fx.clip`)
2. The footage (the take; lowered in L-topband, dimmed in L-frost)
3. z3 objects on the dome (P-15)
4. z4 cards, windows, UI recreations, list rows, covers, person cards
5. z5 tiles, tool drops, plates (P-01, P-07, P-11, P-12, P-36)
6. z6 titles (P-02, P-03, P-04) and the ink layer (P-27…P-30)
7. z7 the pill (auto)
8. z8 hero statement, question card, ink bubble (the pill hides)
9. Core-drawn, no scenes: the T-02 blur-out (a `timeline.grades` event on the footage) and the T-10 `curtain` transition (the
   picture layer: the world, the footage and z1–6, under the pill)

### 10.4 Finishing
- Window rims: a 3 px solid rim + an 18 px outer glow at 45 % of the rim tint; the only glowing shapes.
- Card shadows soft (`0 10 30 rgba(0,0,0,.12)` on light worlds; `0 18 40 rgba(0,0,0,.35)` on navy); no hard offset shadows.
- Grain only on W-navy (0.05), W-paper (0.03) and W-pastel (0.02); vignette only on W-navy (0.35) and W-paper (0.12).
- Glow only on the glow headline (P-04) and the window rims.
- The footage untouched (no grade, no grain, no vignette).

## §11 Sound

Soft and sparse: sound marks a thing arriving, never a move. (The reference reels' sound couldn't be measured, so this is a
decision, not a measurement.) Cues come from the bundled pack by catalogue id; every cue marks a visible event, and the
pack's calm, premium sounds suit this style.

| Line | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Where sound goes** | The hook (tile 1 landing, the title reveal, the cut into the glow headline); reveals (a tool drop landing, a window or card arriving, an ink circle finishing, a row pick); the list cue (one soft pop or swish on every F-A tool drop or F-C chapter bubble, the same file each time). Transitions are silent. If in doubt, leave it out |
| **Meme sounds** | None |
| **The bed** | On, a calm bed entering after the hook |
| **Ducking** | The bed sits 20 dB under the voice while the voice speaks; the creator's B-roll sound is muted under the voice unless the script calls for it |
| **Loudness** | −14 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ −1.5 dBTP; a hard end ≤ 6 f after the last word |

No sound in the 1.0 s before the CTA.

### 12.3 Props, matte, resolution
- **Props:** none required. A real object the creator holds up (a book, a notebook) is its own visual; then skip P-16 for it.
- **Reaction bank:** none.
- **Matte:** only for P-17 (a person cut-out from a clip or photo).
- **Resolution:** 1080 px wide for Z-2 reframes up to 1.12×; 2160 px for anything tighter. L-topband needs no extra
  headroom (the engine fills the revealed band with a blurred copy), but the head top has to sit low enough that the offset
  brings it to y ≥ 584 (§3.7).

### 12.4 Third-party inserts: fetch the real thing
Every named-thing row that names a product, app, site, book, person, post, article or quote is a third-party moment, and
the viewer should see the real one.
1. **The creator's own files** in their folder come first.
2. **Otherwise search the web and fetch it:** the real logo (the brand's site, its press kit), the real page or app screen
   (captured and framed on the part that matters), the real cover, a real photo of a public person. Note where each came
   from (`veos asset add --source`).
3. **Use it as it is** (P-08 window, P-07 tile with the logo, P-16 cover, P-17 cut-out); crop, frame and circle, never
   alter what it says; a post or headline word for word; blur personal data.
4. **Nothing usable to be found: build it from the script's words,** with no labels and no credit lines:

| Moment | Built substitute | Recipe id |
|---|---|---|
| A product / app / site / company | P-07 tool drop (name in type, monogram tile) or `fx.logoPlate` | `logo_plate` |
| An app's screen, a UI flow, an AI prompt | P-09 / P-21 / P-20 / P-22 (`fx.appUI` or a bespoke generic UI) | `recreated_ui` |
| A post or quote read aloud | P-34 (`fx.quoteCard`, verbatim) | `quote_card` |
| A book / course | P-16 built cover | `diagram` |
| A person | `fx.silhouette` + P-30 | `silhouette` |
| An article or headline | `fx.headlineCard` on W-paper | `headline_card` |

Put each moment's id on its scene as `insert`.

### 12.5 Frame rate and audio
1080 × 1920, 30 fps CFR (VFR conformed). One voice track: high-pass 80 Hz, de-ess, light compression, −14 LUFS.

## §13 What your plan should settle

Your `ideas.md` is a page or two, for you. In this style it has decided, before any code:
1. **The format** (F-A, F-B or F-C) and why, from the script and the footage that exists.
2. **The hook:** the archetype, the headline (8–10 candidates, the pick, two alternates), the subline, the hook pair, and the
   first second frame by frame.
3. **The named-thing inventory:** every named thing in order, with its word and time, its visual, its source (the creator's
   file, fetched, or what's built) and its placement. No name without a picture.
4. **The make-room offset** and the measured head top it came from; the first top-band frame on the storyboard is where
   you check the hair.
5. **The beat map:** every beat's layout, world, pattern, trigger word and tone; each tool's type voice and each window's rim
   tint; the moves between beats (make-room, push, rocket, settle-back, cut, blur-out, curtain) in frames.
6. **The ink plan:** every mark, its target rect and its word.
7. **The inserts** (the creator's, fetched with their source, or built) and the fallbacks used; any sponsor segment and
   its disclosure.
8. **The sound:** the few cues, the list cue, the bed's entry.
9. **The moments you'll look at hardest on the storyboard:** f0, the title at 1.0 s (or the glow headline), one top-band
   insert (the hair clear of the window), one cutaway or card over B-roll, one ink mark on its target, one F-B pill frame
   over a lowered head if the reel has one, and the last frame.

**Beat fields this style adds:** `make_room_offset` (on L-topband entries), `type_voice` (P-07: sans / serif / airy /
round), `rim` (P-08: accent / violet / data), `world` (W-…), `ink [{mark: circle | underline | arrow | name_tag | sparkle,
target, rect, at, frames}]`, `insert {id, origin}`, `sponsor {id, disclosure}` on paid segments, `shot_id` and
`fallback_used`, and `exception: "E3"` on a scene with 28–39 px redundant labels (captions inherit E3 from CS-1).

## §15 Your look at the storyboard: the checklist

Watch it once as a stranger with a thumb over the next reel, then once as the editor whose name is on it. Fix what
bothers you, in one pass.

**The style (does it feel like §The feel?)**
- Frame 0: the creator is live and moving; the title card reads by 1.8 s (F-A by about 0.8 s), and the first three
  seconds feel full and generous.
- Pause right after any name: that exact thing is on screen, polished and alone.
- F-A never loses the face; F-B comes straight back after each cutaway sentence; F-C returns to the face every chapter.
- The moves are quick and clean (push, rocket, make-room, settle-back, hard cut); nothing shakes, flashes or crash-zooms.
- Each tool has its own type voice and the next rim tint; the last item has the richest insert.
- Colour means something every time: teal windows, red ink on the thing being said, green on the chosen one, yellow once.
- Start to end: it feels like a generous friend showing you their desk, and you'd save it.

**Craft (by eye, in context)**
- The face reads: on L-topband frames the hair has its air under the insert; the pill sits on the chest (look hard at any
  F-B pill over a lowered head); ink and name tags beside people, not on them; nothing chops the head or buries the face
  by accident.
- No text over text by accident: one insert (or one cutaway card group), at most three ink marks, the pill clear of every
  card, row and window; the outgoing insert gone before the next one lands.
- Every named thing has its visual on the word; tool drops on the name; cuts on word boundaries; nothing lingers after
  its point.
- Every number the creator says is shown exactly as said; quotes and fetched posts verbatim; recreated UIs generic;
  illustrations carry no label; personal data blurred.
- The count title's numeral equals the ITEM sections; names, tools and brands spelt exactly; logos are the real ones.
- Captions: CS-1, short one-line chunks, the karaoke greys, hard swaps, readable on a phone; hidden only under P-05 /
  P-06 / P-18.
- A paid segment carries "Paid partnership" for the whole segment, long enough to read.
- The file itself (1080 × 1920, 30 fps, −14 LUFS, a hard end on the face, no black tail, no end card) is the render's job;
  it checks it.

## §16 Build notes
- **Fonts:** Poppins, Anton, Instrument Serif (regular + italic), EB Garamond (italic), Caveat, Montserrat, Lilita One,
  JetBrains Mono, Noto Color Emoji, Noto Sans Devanagari: all bundled. Pre-paint the emoji used on list rows and the
  question-card tab before they're first used.
- **Determinism:** every frame is a function of its index; seeded noise only (tile bob phases, ink wobble, hub-dot angles);
  no CSS animation in scenes; recordings play through `ctx.videoFrame`.
- **The make-room offset** is written inline on each L-topband stage entry (`"offset": O`); the tokens default is 280.
- **The question card** is one built-in recipe for every length (`fx.card` with `grow: "fit-text"`, §8.3 P-18).
- **The blur-out and the curtain** are core-drawn (`timeline.grades`, `timeline.transitions`), not scenes (§10.3).
- **Pushes and rockets** use the enter / exit presets with `smear: true`; they're measured at rest, so their per-frame travel
  is accepted.
